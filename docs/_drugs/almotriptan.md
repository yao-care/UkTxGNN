# Almotriptan: From Migraine to Migraine with Brainstem Aura

## One-Sentence Summary

Almotriptan is a selective 5-HT1B/1D agonist (triptan), originally used for the acute treatment of migraine with or without aura.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **migraine with brainstem aura**, a subtype of migraine, but there are currently **no registered clinical trials** and the **20 publications** compiled are general migraine and triptan literature, with none specific to this subtype.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on the published literature, almotriptan is a selective agonist of serotonin 5-HT1B and 5-HT1D receptors. This is thought to act through cranial vasoconstriction and inhibition of trigeminal nociceptive transmission, including CGRP release.

Migraine with brainstem aura is a subtype of migraine that shares the underlying trigeminovascular pathophysiology. The mechanistic overlap is therefore strong. However, this is better described as an extension within the same disease family than true repurposing. The very high TxGNN score most likely reflects the existing migraine association rather than independent new evidence.

There is also a safety caveat. Triptans have historically carried cautionary labelling for basilar-type and hemiplegic migraine because of a theoretical vasoconstrictive risk. Safety in this subtype needs explicit evaluation before any recommendation.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications flagged in the knowledge graph** (these come from the TxGNN knowledge graph, not from label text):
  - Cardiovascular and cerebrovascular: coronary artery disease, myocardial infarction, myocardial ischaemia, Prinzmetal angina, hypertension, stroke, transient ischaemic attack, cerebral infarction, brain ischaemia, vertebrobasilar insufficiency, peripheral arterial disease, Raynaud disease, sudden cardiac arrest, heart conduction disease
  - Other: familial or sporadic hemiplegic migraine, serotonin syndrome, liver disease, gallbladder disease, diabetes mellitus, diabetic ketoacidosis, obesity disorders, haemoglobinopathy
  - Vertebrobasilar insufficiency and hemiplegic migraine are particularly relevant to a brainstem aura population.
- **Drug Interactions** (56 recorded; main ones below):
  - **Major**: ondansetron, granisetron, dolasetron, palonosetron, fenfluramine, dexfenfluramine, lorcaserin, sibutramine (serotonergic combinations)
  - **Moderate**: clarithromycin, aprepitant, morphine (including liposomal morphine)

Please refer to the SmPC and BNF for the full warnings and contraindications. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests on model output and general migraine evidence only. No trials or literature specific to brainstem aura were identified, and the triptan class has theoretical vasoconstrictive concerns in this subtype. Evidence level is L4.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Subtype-specific efficacy and safety evidence for brainstem aura
- A review of current SmPC and BNF wording on triptan use in basilar-type and hemiplegic migraine
- Verification of the UK authorisation details (MHRA number, dosage form, approved indication)
- Mechanism of action data to be completed from DrugBank
- A cardiovascular and cerebrovascular risk screening plan for the target population
