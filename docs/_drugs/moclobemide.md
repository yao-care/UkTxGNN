## Moclobemide: Agoraphobia
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Moclobemide is a reversible inhibitor of monoamine oxidase A (RIMA), originally used for anxiety disorder, neurotic disorder and dysthymic disorder.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **agoraphobia**, with **no registered clinical trials** but **10 publications** (including 2 randomised controlled trials in panic disorder) currently supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, moclobemide is a reversible inhibitor of monoamine oxidase A. By reducing the breakdown of serotonin, noradrenaline and dopamine, it can plausibly reduce panic and phobic anxiety. The drug's targets are listed as MAO-A and MAO-B.

Agoraphobia usually occurs together with panic disorder, so panic disorder data are the most relevant evidence. Moclobemide has already been studied in panic disorder with and without agoraphobia, and in social phobia. This fits with its existing use in anxiety and dysthymic disorders.

This mechanistic link rests on the known pharmacology of the drug class rather than on the supplied record, so it should be treated as a hypothesis.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: The compiled data record only pharmacological targets (monoamine oxidase A and monoamine oxidase B), not specific drug-drug interactions. As a monoamine oxidase inhibitor, moclobemide needs careful review of co-prescribed serotonergic and sympathomimetic medicines.

Please refer to the SmPC and BNF for full safety information. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Two randomised controlled trials in panic disorder with or without agoraphobia support this direction, and moclobemide is already marketed in the UK for related anxiety conditions. However, there are no registered trials specific to agoraphobia, and the available trials are older and enrol mixed panic disorder populations. This is best treated as a research question rather than a ready-to-use indication.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Review of the two RCTs for agoraphobia-specific outcomes
- Mechanism of action data (MOA) from DrugBank
- Full SmPC and BNF warnings and contraindications, which are not in the compiled data
- Approved indication text and dosage form for the UK authorisation
- A safety monitoring plan, including interaction screening for serotonergic and sympathomimetic medicines
