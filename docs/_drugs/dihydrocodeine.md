## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Dihydrocodeine is an opioid with analgesic and antitussive effects, so its action is on pain and cough pathways rather than on nasal tissue itself.

The prediction may reflect the fact that dihydrocodeine-containing products are used for symptom relief in upper-airway conditions such as colds and rhinitis. If so, the model may be recognising existing symptomatic use rather than a new disease-modifying indication. This is an interpretation only: no study in the compiled data tests dihydrocodeine in nasal cavity disease, and the mechanistic link has not been assessed further.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction is supported only by a very high model score, with no trials or publications on nasal cavity disease. The most likely explanation is overlap with existing symptomatic use in cold and rhinitis products, not a new indication.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data for dihydrocodeine
- A clear definition of what "nasal cavity disease" would mean clinically, and which symptoms or outcomes would be targeted
- Clinical or observational studies in the predicted condition
- A safety review covering opioid-related risks (dependence, misuse, respiratory depression) and the interactions noted above
