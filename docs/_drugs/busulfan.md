## Busulfan: From Marketed Oral Alkylating Agent to Myelodysplastic Syndrome
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Busulfan is an oral alkylating (DNA cross-linking) chemotherapy agent, marketed in the UK as a 2 mg tablet. The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS)**, as the backbone of conditioning before allogeneic stem cell transplantation. This direction is supported by **39 clinical trials** and **20 publications** in the data compiled for this report, including randomised comparative studies.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, busulfan is a bifunctional alkylating agent that cross-links DNA and is myeloablative. Its efficacy in haematological conditions is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to MDS.

Allogeneic haematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT) is the only potentially curative option for MDS. Busulfan-based conditioning serves two purposes: it eradicates the malignant clone and it creates marrow space for donor cells to engraft. This is why busulfan features so often as the backbone of MDS transplant conditioning regimens.

An important caveat is that busulfan is used within combination regimens (for example with fludarabine, cyclophosphamide or cladribine), not as monotherapy. The new use therefore concerns a transplant conditioning role rather than a standalone tablet treatment.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications suggested by the knowledge graph (not label text):** the TxGNN knowledge graph links busulfan with epilepsy, thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, agranulocytosis, anaemia (including macrocytic and Fanconi anaemia), pulmonary fibrosis, hyperglycaemia, infectious disease (including fungal infection and dermatophytosis), potassium deficiency and hyperuricaemia. These should be checked against the SmPC.
- **Drug interactions:** 100 interactions were found in total. Notable ones include **metronidazole (major)**, **aprepitant (moderate)** and **miconazole (moderate)**; levofloxacin is a minor interaction. Many others (for example amphotericin B, omeprazole, ondansetron, dexamethasone) have an unclassified severity level.
- **Conditioning-specific concerns (from the evidence):** veno-occlusive disease/sinusoidal obstruction syndrome, seizures, long-term complications such as growth impairment in children, and subsequent malignancy.

Label warnings and contraindications were not available in the data compiled for this report. Please refer to the SmPC and BNF for full safety information. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Busulfan-based conditioning for MDS is supported by two completed Phase 3 trials, several Phase 2/3 trials and randomised literature. However, most evidence is for busulfan within combination regimens in mixed AML/MDS populations, and several of the Phase 2/3 trials are still recruiting or of unknown status.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Review of the full texts of the randomised studies (PMID 31606445, 35617104, 36702138, 42285355) to confirm the MDS-specific findings and the busulfan-specific contribution
- The licensed indications, warnings and contraindications from the current SmPC and BNF, which were not available in the data compiled for this report
- Mechanism of action data to complete the mechanistic analysis
- Use only within combination conditioning regimens, with PK-targeted dosing, intensity matched to age and comorbidity, and monitoring for VOD/SOS, seizures and long-term complications
- Review of interactions, particularly metronidazole, aprepitant and azole antifungals
