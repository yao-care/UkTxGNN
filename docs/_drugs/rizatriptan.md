## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known class pharmacology, rizatriptan is a serotonin 5-HT1B/1D receptor agonist. Its effect is thought to come from cranial blood vessel constriction and inhibition of release of pro-inflammatory neuropeptides such as CGRP along the trigeminovascular pathway.

Migraine with brainstem aura is a subtype of migraine with aura (formerly basilar-type migraine). It shares the trigeminovascular pathway with the migraine types rizatriptan is already used for, so there is a plausible mechanistic overlap.

There are important caveats:
- None of the supplied evidence is specific to brainstem aura.
- Triptans have historically been used cautiously or avoided in brainstem aura and hemiplegic migraine because of theoretical vasoconstriction concerns, so safety needs explicit review.
- The very high TxGNN score largely reflects the drug's existing migraine indication, so it adds little independent support for this subtype.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindication-related conditions (from the knowledge graph, not label text):** The TxGNN knowledge graph links rizatriptan with the following conditions as contraindications:
  - Cardiovascular and cerebrovascular: hypertension, stroke and cerebrovascular disorders, cerebral infarction, myocardial infarction, coronary artery disease, myocardial ischaemia, coronary thrombosis, Prinzmetal angina, peripheral vascular disease and peripheral arterial disease
  - Organ disease: liver disease, gallbladder disease, chronic kidney disease and end-stage renal failure
  - Other: hemoglobinopathy and **familial or sporadic hemiplegic migraine**
- **Drug interactions:** 80 interactions were identified in total. Those graded **Major** are:
  - 5-HT3 antagonists: ondansetron, granisetron, dolasetron, palonosetron
  - Serotonergic agents: lorcaserin, fenfluramine, dexfenfluramine, sibutramine
  - Several of these are likely to carry a serotonin-syndrome concern, which should be confirmed in the SmPC.
- **Moderate interactions** include morphine (including the liposomal formulation).

The hemiplegic migraine and cerebrovascular entries are particularly relevant, because brainstem aura has similar theoretical vascular concerns. Please refer to the SmPC and BNF for full safety information. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
There is no trial evidence and no literature specific to brainstem aura, and the high TxGNN score mostly reflects the existing migraine indication. Triptans have historically been used cautiously in this subtype, so safety is a real concern.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A specific review of the safety of triptans in migraine with brainstem aura, including cerebrovascular risk
- Subtype-specific clinical data (case series or controlled studies) in brainstem aura
- Mechanism of action data to replace the class-based inference
- The SmPC warnings and contraindications for the UK-authorised product, along with its approved indication text and dosage form
- Clinical neurology and headache specialist input on whether this is a viable research question
