# Everolimus: From Renal Cell Carcinoma to Liposarcoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Everolimus is an mTOR inhibitor, originally used for renal cell carcinoma, breast cancer and tuberous sclerosis-related conditions.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **liposarcoma**,
with **1 clinical trial** (Phase 2, combination design) and **4 publications** (only one of which is a clinical study report) currently supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, everolimus is an inhibitor of mTORC1, a kinase that drives cell growth and survival. Its efficacy in renal cell carcinoma has been established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to liposarcoma.

Dedifferentiated liposarcoma is typically driven by MDM2/CDK4 amplification, and the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway is frequently active as well. Combining everolimus with the CDK4/6 inhibitor ribociclib targets two complementary pathways: cell-cycle progression and mTOR-mediated growth and survival. This is a plausible rationale, but everolimus on its own has not been shown to be effective in this disease. The single-arm combination design of the available trial also cannot separate everolimus's contribution from that of ribociclib.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold (research question only)**

**Rationale:**
The only direct evidence for liposarcoma is a single Phase 2 trial of a combination (ribociclib plus everolimus). Everolimus's own contribution cannot be isolated, and no Phase 3 data exist. Label-level safety data and mechanism data are also missing. The other predicted indications (rhabdomyosarcoma subtypes, rare sarcomas and renal cell carcinoma subtypes) have little or no evidence and rank at L5, apart from rhabdomyosarcoma, which has early-phase data (L2).

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Detailed results of NCT03114527 and PMID 37967116, in particular response rates and safety for the liposarcoma cohort
- Evidence for everolimus-specific activity, such as monotherapy data or randomised comparisons
- Mechanism of action data
- SmPC warnings and contraindications for the UK product, plus a verified MHRA licence number and indication text
- A safety monitoring plan covering metabolic, haematological, pulmonary and interaction risks (especially CYP3A4/P-gp-related interactions)
