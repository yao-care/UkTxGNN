## Calcipotriol: From Plaque Psoriasis to Seborrheic Keratosis
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Calcipotriol is a topical vitamin D3 analogue marketed in the UK as a 50 microgram/g ointment. It is established in dermatology for hyperproliferative skin disease, but the data compiled for this report do not state the licensed indication.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **seborrheic keratosis**, with **no registered clinical trials** and **4 publications** (small clinical studies and case reports) currently supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, calcipotriol is a vitamin D3 analogue that activates the vitamin D receptor in keratinocytes. This inhibits hyperproliferation and promotes differentiation and apoptosis.

Its established use in psoriasis relies on these effects on abnormal keratinocyte growth. Seborrheic keratosis is a benign epidermal proliferative lesion, so the same antiproliferative and prodifferentiation effects are biologically plausible. Published reports describe topical vitamin D3 analogues (tacalcitol, calcipotriol, maxacalcitol) inducing apoptosis in senile warts, the older term for seborrheic keratosis.

This is a plausible link, not proof. The evidence consists of small clinical series and case reports, and study designs would need verification from the full texts.

## Safety Considerations

**Contraindications suggested by the knowledge graph** (drug-disease associations from the TxGNN knowledge graph, not product label text) include:
- Calcium and vitamin D-related disorders: hypercalcaemia, hypervitaminosis, nephrocalcinosis, calcinosis, calciphylaxis, and disorders of phosphorus and calcium metabolism
- Adrenal conditions: adrenocortical insufficiency, Addison disease
- Skin and vascular conditions: skin atrophy, telangiectasis, peripheral vascular disease
- Other skin disorders: several ichthyoses, Darier disease, pseudoxanthoma elasticum, guttate psoriasis

**Drug Interactions:** 84 interactions were recorded, none with a severity grade. Examples include corticosteroids (prednisolone, prednisone, hydrocortisone, triamcinolone), proton pump inhibitors (omeprazole, pantoprazole, lansoprazole, rabeprazole), clarithromycin, doxycycline, metformin, simvastatin and morphine.

**Site-specific caution:** Genital or mucosal skin may be irritated by topical calcipotriol.

Please refer to the SmPC and BNF for full safety information. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold (research question)**

**Rationale:**
Seborrheic keratosis is the only prediction with direct supporting literature, but this is limited to small series and case reports, with no registered trials. The compiled data also lack the licensed indication and label safety information. The other nine predictions (including vulvar conditions, Paget disease of bone and fibrocystic breast disease) have no supporting evidence or only indirect evidence, and remain on Hold.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The licensed indication, dosage form and SmPC warnings and contraindications for the UK product
- Mechanism of action data confirmed from DrugBank
- Verification of the study designs and outcomes of the published reports
- A controlled study (ideally randomised) of topical calcipotriol versus standard treatments such as cryotherapy for seborrheic keratosis
- A review of local tolerability, and of systemic calcium effects where large areas are treated
