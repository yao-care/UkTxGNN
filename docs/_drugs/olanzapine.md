## One-Sentence Summary

Olanzapine is an atypical antipsychotic, originally used for schizophrenia, bipolar disorder and depressive conditions.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Agoraphobia** (the highest-ranked prediction with any supporting literature), with **no registered clinical trials** and **6 publications** currently supporting this direction. Most of this literature concerns panic disorder and anxiety disorders more broadly rather than agoraphobia alone.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not currently available for this drug. Based on known pharmacology, olanzapine is a multi-receptor antagonist acting at serotonin (5-HT2A/5-HT2C) and dopamine D2 receptors, among others. Its efficacy in schizophrenia and bipolar disorder is established, and it may be applicable to anxiety-spectrum conditions.

Agoraphobia is closely linked to panic disorder and the wider anxiety spectrum. Olanzapine has been used as an add-on treatment in anxiety disorders that have not responded to first-line therapy, which gives a plausible mechanistic basis for the prediction. The prediction comes from the TxGNN model (deep learning) and may reflect similarities the model learned between related diseases, not biological evidence.

The evidence is indirect, however. It relates mainly to treatment-resistant panic disorder (with or without agoraphobia) rather than agoraphobia as a standalone diagnosis. Metabolic adverse effects also limit its appeal where first-line options (SSRIs and CBT) are available.

## Safety Considerations

**Contraindications / cautions (from knowledge graph drug-disease edges, not label text):** The following conditions are linked to olanzapine as contraindications or cautions:
- Metabolic: diabetes mellitus, diabetic ketoacidosis, hyperglycaemia, hyperlipidaemia (including familial), hypertriglyceridaemia, obesity
- Cardiovascular and cerebrovascular: orthostatic hypotension, postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome, hypotensive disorder, multiple system atrophy, stroke, cerebral infarction, myocardial infarction, coronary thrombosis
- Haematological: neutropenia, agranulocytosis, blood coagulation disease
- Hepatic and biliary: liver disease, liver failure, cirrhosis, gallbladder disease
- Other: epilepsy, benign prostatic hyperplasia, female breast carcinoma

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold (treat as a research question)**

**Rationale:**
The model score is high, but the supporting evidence for agoraphobia is limited to one small open-label trial in panic disorder, systematic reviews and case reports, with no RCTs or registered trials. Olanzapine's metabolic and cardiovascular risks, together with available first-line treatments, weigh against progressing without stronger evidence.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Randomised controlled trials of olanzapine in agoraphobia or panic disorder with agoraphobia
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Approved indication text and dosage form for the UK authorisation, verified against the MHRA record and the SmPC
- Complete warnings and contraindications from the SmPC and BNF
- A metabolic and cardiovascular monitoring plan for any anxiety-disorder use
