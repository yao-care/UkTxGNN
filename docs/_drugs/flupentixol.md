## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in this evidence pack (original MOA and original indications are both recorded as data gaps).

The evidence pack's own repurposing rationale, however, does identify flupentixol pharmacologically as a **dopamine D1/D2 receptor antagonist**. Based on that, no known or plausible mechanistic link to retinal dystrophy with extraocular anomalies has been identified — this is a congenital, monogenic developmental disorder, and its pathophysiology does not intersect with dopaminergic receptor blockade.

The same pattern holds across all nine TxGNN-predicted indications for this drug (see table below): each is a rare congenital or genetic disorder (e.g. Charcot-Marie-Tooth type 1G, a congenital disorder of glycosylation, polymicrogyria, hydranencephaly, X-linked myopias, atypical glycine encephalopathy) with no dopaminergic mechanistic rationale, no clinical trial evidence, and — for eight of the nine — no literature evidence at all. This pattern is consistent with a **high-score artefact on sparsely-connected ("orphan") disease nodes** in the knowledge graph, rather than a biologically grounded repurposing signal.
