# Tobramycin: From Bacterial and Ocular Infections to Exposure Keratitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Tobramycin is an aminoglycoside antibiotic, originally used for bacterial infections of the eye and other sites, including conjunctivitis, uveitis and *Staphylococcus aureus* infection.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **exposure keratitis**, but there are **no registered clinical trials** and only **8 publications** (none directly addressing this condition), so the prediction rests on the model alone.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general pharmacological knowledge, tobramycin is a broad-spectrum aminoglycoside that inhibits bacterial protein synthesis by binding the 30S ribosomal subunit, and it is used topically for ocular bacterial infections.

Exposure keratitis occurs when the cornea is inadequately protected, for example when the eyelids cannot close fully. The damaged corneal surface is prone to secondary bacterial infection, so a prophylactic or treatment role for a topical antibiotic is plausible.

However, the retrieved literature does not directly address exposure keratitis. It covers bacterial keratitis (*S. aureus*, *Bacillus cereus*, *Shewanella*), veterinary susceptibility data and an in vitro study showing that aminoglycosides, tobramycin included, are toxic to corneal epithelial cells. That toxicity is a particular concern when the epithelium is already compromised.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is high, but there are no registered trials and no published studies directly on exposure keratitis. The only relevant safety signal (corneal epithelial toxicity) is unfavourable, and the evidence amounts to a plausible mechanism only (L4).

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Clinical or well-designed preclinical studies of tobramycin in exposure keratitis, including its prophylactic role against secondary infection
- Comparison against established care, such as lubricants and lid protection, and against alternative topical antibiotics
- Assessment of corneal epithelial toxicity in a compromised epithelium
- Mechanism of action data and the full UK product information (dosage form, approved indications, contraindications), because the current licence record is incomplete
- Confirmation of which ophthalmic tobramycin formulations are available in the UK, since the one authorisation on record is a nebuliser solution and not an eye preparation

### Other predictions reviewed

Other high-scoring predictions were considered, and none is stronger than the lead indication:

- **Otitis externa (L3, research question)**: tobramycin is active against *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* and *S. aureus*, and there are case reports and a historical report for malignant otitis externa. No controlled human studies were found, and ototoxicity (especially with a perforated tympanic membrane) must be considered.
- **Post-bacterial disorder (L4)**: the two linked Phase 3 trials ([NCT01069705](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01069705), [NCT00982930](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00982930)) are single-arm open-label extension studies of inhaled tobramycin in cystic fibrosis, so the link appears to be a mapping artefact.
- **Epidemic keratoconjunctivitis (L4)**: the condition is viral and tobramycin has no antiviral activity; any role is limited to secondary bacterial infection.
- **Infective urethral stricture (L4)**: only a 1976 study in complicated urinary tract infection, which is indirect evidence.
- **Remaining predictions (L5, no supporting evidence)**: non-human animal disease, postinfectious vasculitis, post-infectious syndrome, Chagas cardiomyopathy and infection-related haemolytic uraemic syndrome. For the last of these, antibiotic use is generally avoided and aminoglycoside nephrotoxicity is a serious concern.
