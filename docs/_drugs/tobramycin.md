# Tobramycin: From Bacterial Infections to Exposure Keratitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Tobramycin is an aminoglycoside antibiotic internationally used to treat serious Gram-negative bacterial infections (notably *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*), including pulmonary infections in cystic fibrosis and external eye infections. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Exposure Keratitis**, but this direction is currently supported only by **2 clinical trials** (neither directly testing tobramycin) and **7 publications**, none of which evaluate tobramycin specifically in this condition.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known pharmacology, tobramycin is an aminoglycoside antibiotic active against common secondary pathogens such as *Staphylococcus aureus* and *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*, and ophthalmic tobramycin formulations are already used for bacterial external eye infections.

Exposure keratitis, however, is fundamentally a mechanical/exposure-related corneal condition — typically caused by inadequate eyelid closure — rather than a primary bacterial disease. Antibiotics such as tobramycin could only address a *secondary* bacterial complication of exposure keratitis; they do not treat the underlying cause (eyelid closure failure, corneal exposure, tear film deficiency).

The two clinical trials linked to this prediction (NCT05313828, NCT06200727) do not test tobramycin as the intervention: one concerns dendritic (herpetic) corneal ulcers, and the other evaluates a platelet-rich fibrin membrane. The supporting literature is similarly indirect — mostly case reports of unrelated keratitis pathogens and an in vitro corneal toxicity study of aminoglycosides. Overall, the mechanistic rationale is plausible only as adjunctive management of secondary infection, not as a primary treatment for exposure keratitis itself.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The evidence for tobramycin in exposure keratitis is weak — neither of the two linked clinical trials tests tobramycin directly, and the literature consists largely of unrelated case reports and an in vitro toxicity study. Combined with the absence of UK marketing authorisation and a blocking data gap on safety warnings/contraindications, this candidate does not currently meet the bar to proceed.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Detailed mechanism of action (MOA) data for tobramycin
- MHRA/SmPC warnings, contraindications and DDI data (currently a blocking data gap)
- Clinical trials or studies that directly evaluate tobramycin's efficacy in exposure keratitis, rather than in unrelated corneal conditions
- Clarification of UK market status/import pathway, given no marketing authorisation is currently recorded

**Note:** Within the same evidence pack, **otitis externa** (rank 3) shows substantially stronger and more directly relevant evidence — an L3 evidence level with multiple tobramycin-specific cohort and case-series studies, including long-term safety data — and is scored "Proceed with Guardrails." This may be a more promising repurposing candidate for further evaluation than exposure keratitis.
