## Voriconazole: From Invasive Fungal Infections to Multidrug-Resistant Tuberculosis
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Voriconazole is an azole antifungal, originally used for fungal infections such as aspergillosis, candidaemia and fusariosis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **multidrug-resistant tuberculosis**, but there are **no registered clinical trials** and only **3 publications**, none of which show that voriconazole treats tuberculosis itself.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available from the data compiled for this report. Based on general pharmacology, voriconazole is a triazole antifungal that inhibits fungal CYP51 (lanosterol 14-alpha-demethylase), and its efficacy in invasive fungal infections is established.

No direct antimycobacterial mechanism is established for voriconazole. Azoles have shown some in vitro activity against *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* cytochrome P450 enzymes (CYP121 and CYP125), but this is preclinical and is not supported by the data compiled here. The high TxGNN score most likely reflects proximity to other anti-infective drugs in the knowledge graph rather than validated efficacy.

The retrieved literature mainly describes fungal disease occurring alongside, or mimicking, tuberculosis. In those reports voriconazole treats the fungal infection, not the TB. This prediction should therefore be regarded as hypothesis-generating only.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests on a model score alone. There are no clinical trials, and the literature concerns fungal disease occurring alongside TB, not antimycobacterial activity. No established mechanism links voriconazole to TB, and its safety profile (QT prolongation, hepatic, visual and many interaction concerns) is significant.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data and evidence of in vitro activity against *M. tuberculosis*, including MDR strains
- Preclinical efficacy and pharmacokinetic data at achievable drug concentrations
- Interaction assessment with rifamycin-based and second-line TB regimens, since rifampicin is a strong CYP inducer and is contraindicated with voriconazole
- UK SmPC warnings and contraindications, and approved indication text for the licensed product
