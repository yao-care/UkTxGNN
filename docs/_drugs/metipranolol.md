# Metipranolol: From Open-Angle Glaucoma to Primary Hereditary Glaucoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Metipranolol is a topical non-selective beta-blocker traditionally used to lower intraocular pressure (IOP) in chronic open-angle glaucoma and ocular hypertension. The TxGNN model predicts a very high likelihood of relevance to **Primary Hereditary Glaucoma**, but this specific link currently has **no clinical trials and no dedicated literature** — the prediction rests on mechanistic extrapolation from the drug's established beta-blocker pharmacology.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

DrugBank does not currently hold a formal mechanism-of-action record for metipranolol (data gap). Based on the pharmacological information available in this evidence pack, metipranolol is a **non-selective beta-adrenergic receptor antagonist** applied topically to the eye. It blocks beta-2 receptors on the ciliary body epithelium, reducing aqueous humour production and thereby lowering intraocular pressure — the central pathophysiological target in glaucoma.

Primary hereditary glaucoma (encompassing genotype-defined subgroups such as primary congenital glaucoma and juvenile open-angle glaucoma) shares the same core pathology of elevated IOP as classic open-angle glaucoma, so the beta-blocker mechanism is theoretically applicable. However, these hereditary forms — particularly congenital-onset disease — are typically managed with **surgery as first-line treatment**, and no genotype-specific clinical trials or publications currently exist for metipranolol in this population.

Notably, a related and much better-supported prediction in this evidence pack (open-angle glaucoma, TxGNN score 99.35%) is backed by 20 publications including multiple RCTs (evidence level L1), confirming metipranolol's established efficacy in lowering IOP in the classic disease phenotype. This lends indirect mechanistic credibility to the hereditary-glaucoma hypothesis, even though it does not substitute for direct evidence in that specific population.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The predicted indication (primary hereditary glaucoma) currently has zero supporting clinical trials or publications, and evidence level L4 reflects a mechanism-only rationale rather than direct clinical data. Hereditary/congenital glaucoma subtypes are also typically treated surgically as first-line, which limits the practical applicability of a topical beta-blocker without further evidence.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- UK/EU SmPC-level safety data (key warnings, contraindications) — currently a blocking data gap
- Formal MOA documentation from DrugBank to support mechanistic-relevance analysis
- Genotype-stratified clinical evidence for specific hereditary glaucoma subpopulations (e.g., primary congenital glaucoma, juvenile open-angle glaucoma)
- Confirmation of UK marketing authorisation status/pathway, given metipranolol is not currently marketed in the UK
- Consideration of the stronger, already-established open-angle glaucoma evidence base (L1, 20 publications) as supportive — but not substitutive — context when designing any hereditary-glaucoma study
