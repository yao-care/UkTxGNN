## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Benzocaine is an ester-type local anaesthetic, and drugs of this class block voltage-gated sodium channels. In theory this could ease surface discomfort or itching, which is the only plausible link to an ocular condition.

The link is weak. Papillary conjunctivitis is usually driven by allergic or mechanical irritation (for example contact lens wear, ocular prostheses or exposed sutures). Management centres on removing the trigger and using antihistamines, mast cell stabilisers or topical corticosteroids, not anaesthetics. Benzocaine's recorded uses are topical, symptom-relief or dermatological, so there is no clear shared pathway with ocular allergic or inflammatory disease.

There is also a safety concern. Repeated application of topical anaesthetics to the eye can cause corneal toxicity, delayed epithelial healing and keratopathy, so any ocular use would need careful risk assessment.
