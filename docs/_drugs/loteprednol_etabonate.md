## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not currently available. Based on known information, loteprednol etabonate is a topical corticosteroid. Its anti-inflammatory effect is established in allergic and inflammatory conjunctival disease, and mechanistically it may be applicable to conjunctival folliculosis.

Conjunctival folliculosis is a follicular reaction of the conjunctiva. It is closely related to the allergic and papillary conjunctivitis conditions that are already among the drug's original indications. This overlap in conjunctival inflammation is the main reason the prediction is biologically plausible.

This is a model prediction only. Folliculosis can also be infective or toxic in origin, so any anti-inflammatory benefit would depend on the underlying cause.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications (from knowledge-graph links, not label text)**: ocular hypertension, glaucoma, herpes simplex virus keratitis, fungal eye infection, viral eye infection, ocular tuberculosis and ophthalmic herpes zoster.
- **Drug Interactions**: 30 potential interactions were identified, but none has a severity level assigned. Examples include other corticosteroids (triamcinolone, prednisone), warfarin, clopidogrel, acetylsalicylic acid, ibuprofen, simvastatin, rosuvastatin and several proton pump inhibitors. These are screening signals only. Systemic exposure from eye drops is low, so their clinical relevance is uncertain.

The contraindicated infective conditions matter here. Several other predicted indications are infective (viral, parasitic and acute contagious conjunctivitis), and corticosteroids could worsen these. Any extension should exclude infective causes first.

Please also refer to the SmPC and BNF for full safety information. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.
