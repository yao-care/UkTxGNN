## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available for this drug. Based on known information, loteprednol etabonate belongs to the class of topical ophthalmic corticosteroids designed as a "soft steroid" (metabolised to inactive compounds after exerting local effect), with established efficacy in allergic conjunctivitis. Mechanistically, its anti-inflammatory action — suppression of inflammatory mediators and reduced vascular permeability — could plausibly extend to other inflammatory conditions of the conjunctiva.

However, conjunctival folliculosis is typically a benign, self-limiting lymphoid follicular hyperplasia rather than a classic corticosteroid indication. Unlike allergic conjunctivitis, where anti-inflammatory therapy is well established, this condition generally resolves without treatment and does not represent a clear unmet clinical need. The evidence pack's own mechanistic rationale explicitly flags this mismatch, noting the prediction is "atypical" for steroid use and lacks both clinical need and supporting evidence.

It is worth noting that this evidence pack contains ten TxGNN-predicted indications for loteprednol etabonate, several of which show stronger mechanistic plausibility or direct supporting literature — for example, pseudomembranous conjunctivitis (a 2025 comparative study explicitly evaluates loteprednol etabonate) and ocular rosacea (consistent with established low-potency steroid use in chronic ocular surface inflammation). These may represent more promising candidates for further evaluation than the top-ranked prediction discussed here.
