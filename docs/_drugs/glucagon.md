## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data for Glucagon is currently unavailable in this evidence pack, and no original indication is recorded. This makes a direct pharmacological rationale difficult to construct from the data provided.

An important caveat needs to be raised here for clinical review. The clinical trial and literature evidence retrieved under "Glucagon" overwhelmingly concerns **glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonists** (e.g. ROSE-010, exenatide/exendin-4, liraglutide) rather than glucagon itself. Glucagon and GLP-1 are both derived from the same precursor gene, proglucagon, but they act on distinct receptors and have largely opposing physiological effects — glucagon raises blood glucose and is used to treat severe hypoglycaemia, whereas GLP-1 receptor agonists lower blood glucose, slow gastric emptying, and are the agents actually studied for gut-motility effects in IBS. It is plausible that the TxGNN knowledge graph has linked "Glucagon" to IBS via this shared lineage/naming proximity with GLP-1 pathways, rather than through direct evidence for glucagon hormone itself.

Given this, the mechanistic plausibility of Glucagon (as distinct from GLP-1 receptor agonists) being effective in IBS should be treated with caution pending clarification of drug identity and a dedicated mechanism-of-action review.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
- The evidence base predominantly concerns GLP-1 receptor agonists, a related but mechanistically distinct peptide class, rather than glucagon itself — this identity ambiguity must be resolved before the prediction can be relied upon.
- Glucagon has no current UK marketing authorisation and no safety (SmPC) data is available, which blocks the standard S1 safety screening step.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Confirm whether the TxGNN signal reflects glucagon hormone specifically or is an artefact of its shared proglucagon lineage with GLP-1 receptor agonists
- Obtain glucagon's mechanism of action (MOA) data (DrugBank or equivalent)
- Obtain SmPC/product warnings and contraindications data (DG001, Blocking)
- If glucagon-specific evidence is confirmed to be minimal, consider whether this candidate should instead be reframed around the GLP-1 receptor agonist class
