Apixaban is an oral factor Xa inhibitor (anticoagulant) marketed in the United Kingdom, although the data compiled for this report do not state its approved indication.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, apixaban is a direct oral factor Xa inhibitor used for anticoagulation. Its efficacy in thromboembolic conditions is established, but no approved indication was recorded in the data compiled for this report.

The proposed link to migraine is indirect. It rests on paradoxical embolism or hypercoagulability (for example patent foramen ovale or antiphospholipid antibodies) in a subset of patients with migraine with aura. Some earlier case reports describe improvement of migraine on heparin or warfarin.

The evidence for apixaban itself is contradictory. One case report describes migraine with aura worsening after starting apixaban. Another describes symptoms returning when a patient was switched from warfarin to apixaban, and resolving again on resuming warfarin. There is no established mechanism supporting factor Xa inhibition as a migraine treatment. The high TxGNN score should therefore be treated as a hypothesis, not as evidence of benefit.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only evidence is two contradictory case reports, one retrospective study that does not clearly test apixaban, and an indirect stroke-prevention trial. There is no plausible mechanism and no controlled evidence for apixaban in migraine. The high TxGNN score is not supported by clinical data.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data, and a clear mechanistic rationale linking factor Xa inhibition to migraine
- Confirmation of the licensed indications from the MHRA register and SmPC
- Warnings and contraindications from the SmPC and BNF
- Controlled clinical data (for example a prospective study in migraine with aura and PFO or antiphospholipid antibodies) before any further consideration
