# Pimozide: From Schizophrenia/Tourette Syndrome to Trichotillomania

## One-Sentence Summary

Pimozide is a diphenylbutylpiperidine-class antipsychotic historically used for schizophrenia and Gilles de la Tourette syndrome. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Trichotillomania** (compulsive hair-pulling disorder), with **no registered clinical trials** and **10 supporting publications** — mostly reviews and case reports — currently available for this specific indication.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data for pimozide is not available in this evidence pack. Based on the supporting literature and repurposing rationale, pimozide is a selective dopamine D2 receptor antagonist. This central D2-blocking action underlies its established antipsychotic effect and its efficacy in Tourette syndrome (tic suppression).

The proposed link to trichotillomania is not a direct pharmacological match to the original indication, but rather an extrapolation from the obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD) "augmentation" literature: trichotillomania sits within the OCD-spectrum of disorders, and low-dose pimozide has historically been used as an add-on to serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs/SRIs) in SRI-refractory OCD-spectrum presentations, including hair-pulling behaviour. Several of the supporting papers (e.g. PMID 1532960, PMID 10357517) describe this augmentation strategy rather than pimozide monotherapy for trichotillomania specifically.

Mechanistically, this is a reasonable hypothesis given the D2-antagonist augmentation precedent in related compulsive-spectrum conditions, but it remains an indirect, class-based rationale rather than evidence generated specifically for trichotillomania. No modern (post-1990s) prospective trials support this use, and current trichotillomania management (e.g. N-acetylcysteine, habit-reversal therapy) has largely moved away from typical antipsychotics.
