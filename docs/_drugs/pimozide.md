# Pimozide: From Tourette Syndrome to Trichotillomania

## One-Sentence Summary

Pimozide is a dopamine D2 receptor antagonist antipsychotic, originally used to treat Tourette syndrome.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **trichotillomania**,
but there are currently **no registered clinical trials** and only **9 publications** (mostly reviews, small open-label studies and case reports) supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, pimozide is a potent D2 dopamine receptor antagonist. Its efficacy in Tourette syndrome is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to trichotillomania.

Trichotillomania is a chronic hair-pulling disorder that overlaps clinically with tic disorders and the obsessive-compulsive spectrum. Dopaminergic dysregulation in cortico-striatal-thalamic circuits is implicated in these habit-like and tic-like behaviours, which provides a plausible link to the original indication.

The supporting data are limited. A 1992 report (PMID 1532960) described low-dose pimozide added to serotonin reuptake inhibitors, and dermatology reviews discuss its use. These data are small, mostly open-label or case-based, and not of high quality. Pimozide's QT prolongation risk and its many CYP-mediated interactions (CYP2D6/CYP3A4) are significant constraints.

## Clinical Trial Evidence

Currently no related clinical trials registered.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications (from knowledge-graph drug-disease associations, not label text):** long QT syndrome (including familial), torsades de pointes, sudden cardiac arrest, heart conduction disease, epilepsy, kidney disease, liver disease, gallbladder disease, potassium deficiency, benign prostatic hyperplasia, glaucoma, neuroleptic malignant syndrome, neutropenia, leukopenia, agranulocytosis (and congenital neutropenia syndromes), female breast carcinoma, and Alzheimer disease. These should be confirmed against the SmPC.
- **Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions were recorded; the 20 reviewed include these **major** interactions: hydrocortisone, amphotericin B (including lipid complex), bupropion, aprepitant, morphine, lorcaserin, cimetidine, potassium citrate, clarithromycin, picosulfuric acid, and polyethylene glycol 3350 with electrolytes. **Moderate** interactions include famotidine, epinephrine, hyoscyamine, loperamide, atropine, bisacodyl, glycopyrronium and dicyclomine. Many of these probably reflect QT prolongation, electrolyte disturbance or CYP inhibition.
- **Other concerns from the literature:** QT prolongation risk and CYP2D6/CYP3A4-mediated interactions (for example, a reported paroxetine–pimozide interaction, PMID 7961347).

Please refer to the SmPC and BNF for full safety information. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold (research question only)**

**Rationale:**
The TxGNN score is very high, but the evidence is limited to reviews, one small open-label report and case-based data, with no registered trials. Pimozide's QT and interaction burden is a significant constraint for a non-life-threatening condition.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data (MOA) and the SmPC warnings and contraindications
- A UK authorisation record with dosage form and approved indication
- A randomised, controlled trial or well-designed prospective study in trichotillomania, compared against established options (such as habit reversal therapy and SRIs)
- An ECG and electrolyte monitoring plan, with a CYP2D6/CYP3A4 interaction management strategy
