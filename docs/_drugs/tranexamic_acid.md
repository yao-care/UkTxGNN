# Tranexamic Acid: From Haemorrhage Control to Amenorrhea

## One-Sentence Summary

Tranexamic acid is a well-established antifibrinolytic agent generally used to control or reduce excessive bleeding (e.g. heavy menstrual bleeding, surgical and traumatic haemorrhage). The TxGNN model predicts a possible association with **Amenorrhea**, but this direction is currently supported only by **2 indirectly related publications** and **no registered clinical trials**, so the evidence base is thin.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the Evidence Pack. Based on general pharmacological knowledge, tranexamic acid is a synthetic lysine analogue that competitively inhibits plasminogen activation, thereby reducing fibrinolysis and limiting blood loss. This mechanism is well established for conditions involving excessive or abnormal bleeding, most notably heavy menstrual bleeding, surgical haemorrhage and trauma-related bleeding.

The relationship between the original bleeding-control use and the predicted indication of amenorrhea (absence of menstruation) is not straightforward, and on the surface appears directionally opposite: tranexamic acid is used to *reduce* menstrual blood loss, not to suppress menstruation altogether. The supporting literature offers a plausible but indirect link — both papers discuss the broader clinical management of abnormal uterine bleeding and menstrual suppression, including the use of nonhormonal antifibrinolytic agents alongside hormonal regimens in patients (e.g. those with haematologic malignancy) who require menstrual suppression to avoid bleeding complications during treatment-related cytopenia. It is possible that TxGNN's knowledge graph captured this co-occurrence within the shared "menstrual bleeding disorder management" neighbourhood rather than a direct causal treatment effect on amenorrhea itself.

Given this ambiguity, the mechanistic plausibility should be treated as low-to-moderate, and the prediction warrants closer scrutiny before any further evaluation.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
There are no registered clinical trials and only two review-level publications with an indirect (and mechanistically somewhat counterintuitive) link to the predicted indication. Combined with missing MOA data, missing safety data, and no confirmed UK marketing authorisation, the current evidence is insufficient to progress this candidate.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Confirmed mechanism of action (MOA) data from DrugBank
- SmPC/BNF-sourced key warnings, contraindications and drug interaction data
- Direct clinical trial or observational evidence specifically evaluating tranexamic acid for menstrual suppression/amenorrhea, rather than for reducing menstrual bleeding
- Clarification of study type/tier for the two cited publications (currently marked "pending" in the Evidence Pack)
- Confirmation of whether tranexamic acid holds any UK marketing authorisation under any brand name
