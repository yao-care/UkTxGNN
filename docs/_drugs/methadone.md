# Methadone: From Opioid Use Disorder to Tourette Syndrome

## One-Sentence Summary

Methadone is a long-acting synthetic opioid agonist, most widely used for opioid use disorder maintenance therapy and severe chronic pain management. The TxGNN model predicts a possible new role in **Tourette syndrome**, but this is currently supported only by **a single historical case report and a handful of mechanistic/observational papers** — there are no registered clinical trials.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available (DrugBank query flagged as a data gap). Based on known pharmacology, methadone is a full mu-opioid receptor agonist with additional NMDA receptor antagonist activity, licensed internationally for opioid dependence and pain control.

The proposed link to Tourette syndrome rests on the idea that mu-opioid receptor activation can modulate striatal dopamine release — a pathway theoretically relevant to tic generation. However, this is an **indirect mechanistic inference**, not part of the established core pathology of Tourette syndrome, which is primarily a cortico-striato-thalamo-cortical circuit disorder.

A key concern is population mismatch: Tourette syndrome predominantly affects children and adolescents, while methadone carries well-known risks of abuse, dependence, respiratory depression and QT prolongation. Any therapeutic exploration would need to weigh this theoretical benefit against a substantial safety burden in a paediatric-heavy population.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The evidence for methadone in Tourette syndrome consists of a single 30-year-old case report and a small number of mechanistic/comorbidity papers, with no clinical trials, no MOA confirmation, and no UK marketing authorisation on file. Given the significant abuse/dependence risk of methadone weighed against a paediatric-predominant target population, the current evidence base does not support progression beyond a research question.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- TFDA/MHRA product labelling — warnings, contraindications and DDI data (blocking gap, DG001)
- Confirmed mechanism of action data from DrugBank (DG002)
- Prospective, controlled studies or larger case series specifically evaluating methadone (or opioid agonists generally) in tic-disorder populations
- Confirmation of UK regulatory/marketing status before any further repurposing assessment
