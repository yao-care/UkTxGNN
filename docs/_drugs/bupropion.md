# Bupropion: From Depression and Nicotine Dependence to Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Bupropion is an antidepressant that is also used for smoking cessation, and it is originally indicated for depressive and anxiety-related disorders and nicotine dependence.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD)**,
with **6 clinical trials** and **19 publications** currently supporting this direction, including a Cochrane review of bupropion in adults with ADHD.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. The published literature describes bupropion as a norepinephrine and dopamine reuptake inhibitor with no serotonergic activity.

ADHD is linked to dysfunction of dopamine and noradrenaline signalling in several brain regions. Established ADHD treatments, such as methylphenidate and atomoxetine, act on these same catecholaminergic pathways. Bupropion's pharmacology overlaps with them, which is why reviews and meta-analyses list it as a second-line or non-stimulant option for adults with ADHD, particularly when stimulants are not tolerated or are ineffective.

The original indications (depression, anxiety, nicotine dependence) are also conditions in which dopamine and noradrenaline signalling is thought to play a role. ADHD often co-occurs with depression and with tobacco and other substance use, which makes the prediction clinically plausible.

## Safety Considerations

**Contraindications suggested by the drug knowledge graph** (these come from knowledge-graph relationships, not from product label text, and must be verified against the SmPC):
- Neurological: epilepsy, central nervous system neoplasm
- Psychiatric: manic bipolar affective disorder, psychotic disorders, schizoaffective and schizophreniform disorders, anorexia nervosa, bulimia nervosa, substance abuse or dependence, opiate dependence
- Cardiovascular: hypertension, myocardial infarction
- Hepatic and renal: liver disease, liver failure, cirrhosis, kidney disease
- Other: gallbladder disease, haemoglobinopathy

**Drug interactions:** 100 interactions were identified. Those listed below are graded Major:
- Opioids: fentanyl, alfentanil, codeine, dihydrocodeine, tramadol
- Others: lidocaine, methylphenidate, hydrocortisone, aldesleukin, amantadine

Methylphenidate is a common ADHD treatment, so this Major interaction needs particular attention if the two are combined.

**Points specific to the ADHD setting** (from the evidence review):
- Bupropion lowers the seizure threshold.
- It can raise blood pressure.
- It carries a potential for misuse in patients with substance use disorders, who make up much of the trial population.

Please refer to the SmPC and BNF for full safety information. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
One completed Phase 3 randomised, placebo-controlled trial, a Cochrane review and several network meta-analyses support bupropion in adult ADHD. However, there is only one completed Phase 3 trial, it is small, and several other studies are in patients with comorbid substance use. The conservative evidence level is therefore L1 as scored, but it should be read as L2-equivalent on trial count alone. Seizure risk, blood pressure effects, misuse potential and the broad contraindication list call for guardrails.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Verification of the authorised indications, dosage form and contraindications from the current SmPC and BNF, as the data compiled for this report are missing these
- Confirmation of the NCT00048360 comparator and population, and of the bupropion arm in NCT00061087
- Review of the Cochrane assessment of the certainty of evidence for adult ADHD
- Mechanism of action data
- Patient selection criteria excluding epilepsy, eating disorders, uncontrolled hypertension and bipolar disorder
- A monitoring plan for blood pressure, seizure risk and misuse, and a check for interactions with co-prescribed ADHD medicines
- NICE guidance and MHRA licensing review, since ADHD is not a licensed indication for bupropion in the UK

**Other predicted indications:** The ADHD inattentive subtype (L4, Research Question) has no dedicated trials, and the parent ADHD evidence would need to be extrapolated. The other predictions (faciodigitogenital syndrome, specific developmental disorder, chondromyxoid fibroma, proximal 16p11.2 microdeletion syndrome, hypervitaminosis, benign paroxysmal torticollis of infancy, trigeminal nerve neoplasm, obsolete hypertelorism) are model predictions only with no supporting evidence, and are recommended as **Hold**.
