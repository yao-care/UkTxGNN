## Dasatinib: From BCR-ABL1-Positive Chronic Myeloid Leukaemia to Ewing Sarcoma
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Dasatinib is a tyrosine kinase inhibitor originally used for BCR-ABL1-positive chronic myeloid leukaemia (CML), including blast phase disease.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Ewing sarcoma**, but only **2 clinical trials** and a small body of mostly laboratory literature support this, and the one large trial found no meaningful single-agent activity in Ewing sarcoma.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on the published literature, dasatinib inhibits BCR-ABL and Src-family kinases. It also inhibits KIT and PDGFR. Its efficacy in CML is driven by BCR-ABL inhibition.

Ewing sarcoma is a bone and soft-tissue tumour of adolescents and young adults, and it has no direct link to BCR-ABL. The rationale rests on Src and FAK signalling. Laboratory studies show that Src activity promotes invasion, migration and invadopodia formation in Ewing sarcoma cells. Dasatinib reduced proliferation and migration in Ewing sarcoma and other sarcoma cell lines, and induced apoptosis in bone sarcoma cells that depend on Src.

The mechanistic case is plausible but has not translated into clinical benefit. A review of the Phase 2 sarcoma study reports that single-agent dasatinib failed in Ewing sarcoma and rhabdomyosarcoma. Interest has since shifted to combination approaches, such as the RIST regimen (rapamycin, irinotecan, dasatinib, temozolomide) in relapsed disease.

## Safety Considerations

- **Disease associations of concern (knowledge-graph contraindication links, not label text):** long QT syndrome (including familial), torsades de pointes, liver disease, gallbladder disease, thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, agranulocytosis, anaemia, congestive heart failure, myocardial infarction, coronary thrombosis, intracerebral haemorrhage, active peptic ulcer disease, pericardial effusion, haemopericardium and haemoglobinopathy. The SmPC should be checked to confirm which of these are formal contraindications and which are cautions.
- **Drug interactions:** 100 interactions were recorded and 20 were available for review. Those rated **Major** were:
  - Acid-suppressing agents: famotidine, ranitidine, cimetidine, omeprazole and rabeprazole. These may reduce dasatinib absorption.
  - Dexamethasone and clarithromycin. These may alter dasatinib exposure through CYP3A4.
  - Acetylsalicylic acid. The concern is bleeding risk.
- **Moderate** interactions included hydrocortisone, budesonide, loperamide, aprepitant, bisacodyl, saxagliptin, magnesium oxide, aluminium hydroxide, calcium carbonate, castor oil and polyethylene glycol 3350 with electrolytes.
- **Reported adverse events:** pleural effusion is reported frequently with dasatinib, and chylothorax has been reported as a rare event.

Please refer to the SmPC and BNF for full safety information. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The Src/FAK rationale is biologically plausible, and dasatinib is already marketed in the UK. However, the only sizeable clinical study was a single-arm Phase 2 trial in mixed sarcomas, and published commentary says single-agent dasatinib failed in Ewing sarcoma. The remaining support is laboratory work and one descriptive case series.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Ewing sarcoma-specific results from NCT00464620, or a subtype-stratified re-analysis.
- Prospective data on combination regimens such as RIST, ideally from a controlled trial.
- Biomarker work to identify Ewing sarcoma patients whose tumours depend on Src or FAK signalling.
- Mechanism of action data, and the SmPC safety information, which was not available for this report.
- A paediatric and young-adult safety plan, covering pleural effusion, cytopenias and QT monitoring.

For context, the same data identify **myeloid leukaemia** as the best-supported predicted indication. It carries L1 evidence, including a Phase 3 trial in core-binding factor AML (PMID 41490515). Most of that evidence overlaps with dasatinib's existing CML use, so only the AML data represent genuine repurposing.
