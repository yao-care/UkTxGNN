## Letrozole: From Original Indication Not Recorded to Female Breast Carcinoma
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Letrozole is a nonsteroidal aromatase inhibitor that lowers oestrogen production. The data compiled for this report do not record its original licensed indication.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **female breast carcinoma**, with **40 clinical trials** and **20 publications** currently supporting this direction. Breast cancer is in fact an established use of letrozole, so this is better read as confirmation than as true repurposing.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data are not available in the compiled data. Based on established pharmacology, letrozole is a nonsteroidal aromatase inhibitor. It blocks the conversion of androgens to oestrogens, which reduces oestrogen-driven tumour growth in hormone receptor-positive (HR+) breast cancer.

Because no original indication was recorded, the prediction cannot be compared with a licensed starting point. Letrozole is already widely used for HR+ breast cancer in adjuvant, neoadjuvant, extended adjuvant and advanced settings. The prediction therefore reflects an established mechanism and not a new one. The trial and literature evidence below supports this.

Among the other predicted indications, only ER-positive breast cancer is similarly well supported. Others are weak. ER-negative breast cancer is mechanistically implausible and is probably a database artefact. Nipple carcinoma, bilateral breast carcinoma, breast fibrocystic disease and benign mammary dysplasia have little or no supporting evidence.

## Safety Considerations

No label-based warnings or contraindications are available in the compiled data. The following signals come from a knowledge graph and drug-interaction database, not from product labelling.

- **Knowledge-graph contraindication signals** (not label text): liver failure, cirrhosis of liver, osteoporosis, postmenopausal osteoporosis, hyperbilirubinaemia, hereditary hyperbilirubinaemia, Gilbert syndrome and cerebrovascular disorder. Hepatic impairment and bone health deserve particular attention.
- **Drug interactions**: 100 potential interactions were identified. Only aprepitant is graded Moderate. Others, including calcitriol, glimepiride, lansoprazole, omeprazole, pantoprazole, famotidine, metformin, simvastatin, prednisone and morphine, are of unknown severity and need checking against the SmPC and BNF.
- **Literature signals**: case reports describe letrozole-related maculopathy and a tumour flare/lysis syndrome.

Please refer to the SmPC and BNF for authoritative safety information. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Multiple completed Phase 3 trials and several randomised publications support letrozole-based therapy in HR+ breast cancer, giving L1 evidence. However, this is an established indication and not a new one. Most trials test combination regimens, and the licence and safety data compiled for this report are incomplete.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Confirmation of the licensed indications, dosage form and manufacturer from the MHRA register and current SmPC
- Label-based warnings and contraindications, since safety screening is currently blocked by missing data
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Restriction of use to HR+ disease, and not ER-negative, benign breast or other weakly supported predicted indications
- Review of the interaction list and bone and liver safety monitoring plans
