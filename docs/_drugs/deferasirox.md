# Deferasirox: From Beta-Thalassaemia (Iron Overload) to HIV Infectious Disease

## One-Sentence Summary

Deferasirox is an oral iron chelator, originally used for conditions involving iron overload such as beta-thalassaemia and other haemoglobinopathies.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **HIV infectious disease**, but there are currently **0 registered clinical trials** and only **2 publications** (one brief drug review and one preclinical study), so the prediction rests mainly on computation.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, deferasirox is an iron chelator. Its efficacy in managing iron overload in beta-thalassaemia is established, and mechanistically it may be relevant to HIV because iron availability influences viral replication.

A 2021 preclinical study (PMID 34550543) reported that iron within endolysosomes restricts Tat-mediated HIV-1 LTR transactivation, by promoting Tat oligomerisation and increasing β-catenin expression. This suggests a plausible but indirect link between iron homeostasis and HIV transcription.

The direction of effect is unclear. Depending on context, iron chelation could suppress or modulate viral replication, and it might even work against the protective effect of endolysosomal iron seen in that study. The high TxGNN score is a computational prediction only and should be treated as a hypothesis to test, not as evidence of benefit.

## Safety Considerations

**Contraindications suggested by the knowledge graph** (these come from knowledge graph drug-disease links, not from product label text, and must be verified against the SmPC):
- Liver and biliary: liver failure, liver disease, cirrhosis, gallbladder disease, hyperbilirubinaemia, Gilbert syndrome
- Renal: kidney disease, impaired renal function
- Haematological: thrombocytopenia, neutropenia (including congenital forms), agranulocytosis, anaemia, cyclic haematopoiesis
- Other: active peptic ulcer disease, ocular hypertension, multiple organ dysfunction syndrome, cancer

**Drug Interactions:** The interaction screen returned 100 interactions in total. Examples of those graded **Major** include:

- Amphotericin B (including lipid complex, cholesteryl sulfate and liposomal forms)

Examples graded **Moderate** include rabeprazole, pioglitazone, alosetron, aprepitant, clarithromycin, saxagliptin, aluminium hydroxide, cisapride and dexfenfluramine.

This is especially relevant to a population with HIV, who often take multiple medicines. Refer to the SmPC and BNF for full safety information. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
- The prediction has no clinical trial support and only indirect preclinical evidence. The direction of effect of iron chelation on HIV is uncertain. The knowledge graph also flags several relevant safety concerns (hepatic, renal and haematological). The evidence level is L4, suitable for generating a research question, not for clinical development.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Direct in vitro and in vivo studies of deferasirox on HIV-1 replication, to establish the direction of effect
- Mechanism of action data to support the mechanistic-link analysis
- Verified UK SmPC warnings and contraindications, since label-level safety data were not available for this report
- Review of interactions with antiretroviral regimens and of the burden of renal, hepatic and haematological toxicity in people living with HIV
