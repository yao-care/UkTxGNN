# Deferasirox: From Iron Overload to HIV Infectious Disease

## One-Sentence Summary

Deferasirox is an iron chelator; this evidence pack does not document its originally licensed indication in detail, though supporting literature within the pack references its established use in iron overload/transfusion-dependent conditions. The TxGNN model predicts potential relevance to **HIV infectious disease**, but this is currently supported only by **2 mechanistic/preclinical publications** and **no registered clinical trials**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in this evidence pack (flagged as a High-severity data gap). Based on the information that is available, deferasirox is an iron chelator, and the supporting literature identified for this prediction centres on iron metabolism rather than a direct antiviral mechanism.

The strongest mechanistic signal comes from a 2021 in vitro study showing that endolysosomal iron levels restrict HIV-1 Tat-mediated LTR transactivation by increasing Tat oligomerisation and β-catenin expression — suggesting that iron chelation could, in principle, modulate this pathway. However, this study did not test deferasirox directly as the intervention; it examined cellular iron status more broadly. The second reference (2006) is a brief new-drug-approval notice covering deferasirox alongside three unrelated drugs and contains no HIV-specific content.

Overall, the biological rationale is plausible but indirect: it rests on an inferred link between iron restriction and a step in HIV-1 transcription, rather than any direct experimental or clinical evidence of deferasirox activity against HIV. This corresponds to a mechanistic/preclinical evidence tier (L4), not a clinical signal.
