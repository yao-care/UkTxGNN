## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Clioquinol is a topical antimicrobial that is thought to act by chelating metals such as zinc and copper, which may disrupt fungal metal homeostasis. This proposed mechanism is not confirmed by the data compiled for this report.

The TxGNN model (deep learning) gives ectothrix infectious disease a score of 0.9926. This score may reflect similarities the model learned between related diseases, such as other dermatophyte infections, not biological evidence. Ectothrix infection is a dermatophyte infection in which fungal arthroconidia form on the outside of the hair shaft (for example, *Microsporum* and some *Trichophyton* species).

An important limitation is that hair and scalp infections generally need systemic therapy, because topical agents penetrate the hair shaft poorly. Topical clioquinol is therefore not expected to be sufficient on its own. A second prediction, endothrix infectious disease (score 0.9919, fungus growing inside the hair shaft), faces the same delivery problem and is also at L5 with a Hold recommendation.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests on model output and similarity to existing dermatophyte uses only. No trials or publications address ectothrix infection directly, and topical delivery to the hair shaft is a major practical barrier.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Confirmed mechanism of action data
- Any in vitro or clinical data on clioquinol against *Microsporum* and *Trichophyton* hair infections
- Assessment of whether a topical route can reach the hair shaft, compared with standard systemic therapy
- Licence label text (indications, warnings, contraindications) for the UK products
