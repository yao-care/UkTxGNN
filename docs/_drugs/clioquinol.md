## Clioquinol: From Tinea Infections to Cutaneous Candidiasis
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Clioquinol is a topical halogenated hydroxyquinoline, originally used for dermatophyte skin infections such as tinea pedis and tinea corporis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **cutaneous candidiasis**,
but there are currently **no registered clinical trials** and only **1 indirect publication** supporting this direction, so the evidence is very limited.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not currently available. Based on general knowledge of the drug class, clioquinol has topical antifungal and antibacterial activity. It is thought to act by chelating metal ions (copper, zinc, iron) and disrupting fungal cell function. This is not confirmed by the data compiled for this report.

Its use in dermatophyte infections (tinea) is established. Because metal chelation is not specific to one fungal group, the activity could plausibly extend from dermatophytes to *Candida* species, which cause cutaneous candidiasis.

The score of 99.84% is a computational prediction only. The single retrieved publication is a 1988 in vitro study on preventing occupational *Candida albicans* infection. It is indirect and is not a controlled efficacy study of clioquinol.

## Safety Considerations

- **Knowledge-graph contraindication signals** (from drug-disease relationships, not label text): adrenocortical insufficiency, Addison disease, skin atrophy, telangiectasis, capillary disease, hereditary haemorrhagic telangiectasia, peripheral vascular disease, intermittent claudication, and peripheral arterial disease. Several of these are likely to reflect the corticosteroid components of the combination products rather than clioquinol itself.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction for cutaneous candidiasis rests on a computational score and one indirect 1988 in vitro publication, with no clinical trials. Safety and mechanism data are also incomplete, so the evidence does not yet justify moving forward.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data (for example, from DrugBank)
- Approved indication text and SmPC safety information for the UK products
- In vitro susceptibility data for clioquinol against *Candida* species, followed by controlled clinical studies in cutaneous candidiasis
- A comparison against established topical antifungals (such as azoles) to show a benefit
- Review of systemic absorption and neurotoxicity concerns, particularly for use on large or inflamed skin areas
