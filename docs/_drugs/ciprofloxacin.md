# Ciprofloxacin: From Bacterial Infections to Diffuse Scleroderma

## One-Sentence Summary

Ciprofloxacin is a fluoroquinolone antibiotic, originally used for bacterial infections such as typhoid fever, salmonellosis, bacterial pneumonia and *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* infection.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **diffuse scleroderma**, but the supporting evidence is thin: **no registered clinical trials** and **2 publications** (one skin antifibrotic study and one observational study of gut bacterial overgrowth).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Ciprofloxacin is a fluoroquinolone antibacterial, and its efficacy in bacterial infections is well established. Two indirect links to diffuse scleroderma have been proposed, and neither is an established mechanism.

1. **Possible antifibrotic effect in the skin.** Scleroderma is an autoimmune connective tissue disorder marked by microvascular injury and excessive skin fibrosis. One published study explored whether oral ciprofloxacin could reduce the severity of skin fibrosis (PMID 20507401).
2. **Treating gut bacterial overgrowth.** Small bowel bacterial overgrowth (SIBO) is a recognised cause of chronic diarrhoea and weight loss in systemic sclerosis. An antibiotic active against gut bacteria could plausibly help these gastrointestinal symptoms (PMID 7728404). This would be symptom management rather than treatment of the underlying disease.

The antifibrotic mechanism remains unproven. The high TxGNN score reflects a computational association and should not be read as clinical proof.

## Safety Considerations

**Contraindications identified from the knowledge graph** (these are drug-disease links from the TxGNN knowledge graph, not label text):
- Cardiac: long QT syndrome (including familial) and torsades de pointes
- Neurological and neuromuscular: epilepsy, peripheral neuropathy and other peripheral nervous system disease, myasthenia gravis (including adult-onset) and neuromuscular junction disease
- Tendon and joint: tendinitis, periarthritis, Achilles bursitis, heel spur and exostosis
- Renal and metabolic: kidney disease, pyoureter and potassium deficiency
- Gastrointestinal and infective: *Clostridium difficile* colitis, perinatal necrotising enterocolitis and superinfection

**Drug interactions** (100 interactions were recorded; the first 20 retrieved include the following):
- **Major:** hydrocortisone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, triamcinolone and bupropion. Corticosteroids are a particular concern because scleroderma patients are often given them, and combining them with a fluoroquinolone is a recognised tendon-risk situation.
- **Moderate:** antidiabetic drugs (acarbose, metformin, pioglitazone, alogliptin, albiglutide), famotidine, loperamide, alosetron, aprepitant, acetylsalicylic acid, balsalazide, bisacodyl, budesonide and calcium phosphate.

Please refer to the SmPC and BNF for the full safety profile. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction is supported only by one exploratory skin study with no available results and one small observational study on gut bacterial overgrowth. No clinical trials are registered, and the mechanism is unproven. Ciprofloxacin also carries several recorded safety concerns (tendon, neuropathy, QT prolongation) that matter in a chronic, steroid-exposed scleroderma population.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The full results and methodology of PMID 20507401, to establish whether the antifibrotic effect was actually shown
- Mechanism of action data, including any evidence for an antifibrotic pathway
- A registered, adequately powered controlled trial in diffuse scleroderma, or a focused trial in scleroderma-associated small bowel bacterial overgrowth
- Comparison of the benefit against the safety risks in this population, particularly combined corticosteroid use, tendon risk and neuropathy
- The approved indication text and the label warnings and contraindications for the UK-authorised products, which are not in the data compiled for this report

**Note on other predictions:** Among the other predicted indications, septicemic plague has the strongest literature, including randomised trial reports in bubonic plague. It largely extends the drug's existing antibacterial use rather than being true repurposing, and it should be assessed separately.
