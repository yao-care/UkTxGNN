# Pantoprazole: From Gastro-oesophageal Reflux Disease to Active Peptic Ulcer Disease

## One-Sentence Summary

Pantoprazole is a proton pump inhibitor originally used for acid-related conditions such as erosive oesophagitis and gastro-oesophageal reflux disease. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Active Peptic Ulcer Disease**, with **3 clinical trials** and **19 publications** currently supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, a fully documented mechanism of action record is not available for pantoprazole in this evidence pack. Based on the pharmacological information captured alongside the clinical evidence, pantoprazole is an **irreversible proton pump (H+/K+-ATPase) inhibitor**. It binds to the final common step of gastric acid secretion in the parietal cell, producing sustained suppression of acid output.

Acid suppression is the pharmacological cornerstone of both the original indication (erosive oesophagitis/GORD) and the predicted new indication (active peptic ulcer disease). Peptic ulcer healing depends on raising and maintaining intragastric pH above the threshold needed for mucosal repair and, where relevant, for antibiotic-based *H. pylori* eradication — a mechanism pantoprazole already exploits in its licensed acid-suppression role.

Because active peptic ulcer disease sits within the same acid-related disease spectrum as GORD, and because pantoprazole is already a recognised comparator/backbone agent in numerous peptic ulcer and *H. pylori* eradication trials, the TxGNN prediction is mechanistically coherent rather than speculative — it reflects an established class effect rather than an unrelated repurposing signal.
