## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on general pharmacology, desogestrel is a progestogen that suppresses gonadotropin secretion and exerts a progestogenic effect on the endometrium.

In hypothalamic oligo/amenorrhoea, combined oral contraceptives containing desogestrel can provide cyclic hormone replacement and may help protect bone density, as shown in a cohort study of young women (PMID 11725730). This is symptomatic hormonal management. It does not restore the underlying ovulatory function.

The model's prediction is therefore plausible in a limited sense. However, the supporting evidence relates mainly to combined estrogen-progestogen products, not desogestrel alone. This matters because the UK product is a progestogen-only pill, which commonly causes amenorrhoea or irregular bleeding as an effect of treatment rather than as a treatment target.

## Safety Considerations

- **Conditions flagged in a knowledge-graph analysis (not label text):** hypertension, epilepsy, porphyria, liver disease, gallbladder disease, diabetes mellitus and hyperglycaemia, hypothyroidism, systemic lupus erythematosus, asthma, hypertriglyceridaemia, hypercalcaemia, female breast carcinoma, thrombophlebitis and uterine leiomyoma. These are computational associations and must be checked against the SmPC.
- **Drug interactions:** 43 interactions are recorded, all or mostly of moderate severity. They include antidiabetic agents (metformin, insulin products, GLP-1 agonists, DPP-4 and SGLT2 inhibitors, nateglinide, rosiglitazone), metreleptin, aprepitant and glycerol phenylbutyrate. The antidiabetic interactions are consistent with possible effects on glycaemic control.
- **Venous thromboembolism:** a UK database study (PMID 10652979) highlights this risk for combined oral contraceptives. It is relevant if a combined formulation is considered.

Please refer to the SmPC and BNF for full safety information. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only registered trial does not identify desogestrel as the intervention, and the literature mostly covers combined contraceptives or symptomatic hormonal management. There is no direct evidence that desogestrel alone treats amenorrhoea. It is also a progestogen-only pill, which commonly causes amenorrhoea as a side effect.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Confirmation of the intervention arms in NCT00946192
- Direct comparative studies of desogestrel (alone or combined) for amenorrhoea, with bone and endometrial outcomes
- The approved indication text and SmPC safety information for the UK product
- Mechanism of action data
- Clarification of whether the intended use is a progestogen-only or combined formulation, with a thromboembolism risk assessment
