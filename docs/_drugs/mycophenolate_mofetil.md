# Mycophenolate Mofetil: From Organ Transplant Rejection Prophylaxis to HIV Infectious Disease

## One-Sentence Summary

Mycophenolate mofetil (MMF) is an established immunosuppressant used to prevent acute rejection after renal, hepatic and cardiac transplantation. The TxGNN model predicts it may also be effective for **HIV Infectious Disease**, with **10 clinical trials** and **20 publications** currently identified, though the evidence is dated, mixed and largely inconclusive.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in this evidence pack. Based on known pharmacology, mycophenolate mofetil is the prodrug of mycophenolic acid (MPA), a reversible inhibitor of inosine monophosphate dehydrogenase (IMPDH) that blocks de novo purine (guanosine) synthesis in lymphocytes. Its efficacy in preventing organ transplant rejection has been established for decades through suppression of alloreactive T- and B-lymphocyte proliferation.

The link between transplant rejection prophylaxis and HIV infection lies in this same antiproliferative mechanism: by depleting intracellular guanosine triphosphate (GTP) pools, MPA limits proliferation of activated CD4+ T cells — the primary cellular target and reservoir for HIV replication — while also appearing to potentiate the intracellular activation of certain nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors (e.g. abacavir, didanosine). Several early pilot studies (2001–2007) explored MMF as an adjunct to antiretroviral therapy on this dual rationale.

However, mechanistic plausibility has not translated into robust clinical evidence: the relevant trials are small, mostly from the early-to-mid 2000s, and several were withdrawn, terminated, or left with unknown outcome status (e.g. the Phase 4 MAN2 study, NCT00120419). This suggests the concept has been explored but not established as a viable therapeutic strategy.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Although the IMPDH-inhibition mechanism is biologically plausible for HIV infection and several pilot trials (L2 evidence) explored this rationale, most trials are small, over 15 years old, and a number were withdrawn, terminated, or left with unknown outcomes (including the pivotal Phase 4 MAN2 study). Combined with a blocking gap in safety labelling data, the evidence does not yet support progression beyond exploratory research.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- MHRA/SmPC safety labelling data (warnings and contraindications) — currently blocking
- Confirmed mechanism of action documentation (e.g. via DrugBank)
- Updated outcome/completion data for the MAN2 study (NCT00120419) and its cardiovascular substudy (NCT00247494)
- Contemporary trial data, as most direct evidence predates 2010
- Verification of current UK marketing authorisation status for mycophenolate mofetil products
