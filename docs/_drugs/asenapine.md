## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Asenapine acts as an antagonist at serotonin, dopamine, alpha-adrenergic and histamine receptors. Detailed mechanism-of-action data were not available in the data compiled for this report. Its efficacy in schizophrenia and bipolar mania is thought to stem from this broad receptor profile, particularly D2 and 5-HT2A antagonism.

**No mechanistic link to retinal dystrophy has been identified.** Inherited retinal dystrophies arise from genetic defects in photoreceptor or retinal pigment epithelium function. Nothing in asenapine's known pharmacology addresses these pathways. The high TxGNN score (99.77%) reflects patterns in the knowledge graph rather than biological or clinical evidence. It should not be read as a sign of likely efficacy.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests solely on a computational score. There are no trials or publications, and no plausible mechanistic link between asenapine's receptor pharmacology and retinal dystrophy. Asenapine also carries cardiovascular, metabolic and haematological risk signals that would need strong justification in any new population.

Of the ten predictions in the compiled data, only the tenth (major affective disorder) has literature support. It overlaps with asenapine's existing labelled use in bipolar mania, so it is not true repurposing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A credible biological rationale linking asenapine's receptor activity to retinal dystrophy pathways, supported by preclinical (L4) data
- Mechanism-of-action data and SmPC warnings and contraindications to complete the safety review
- Confirmation of the UK authorisation details (PL number, indication, dosage form) from the MHRA register
- A risk-benefit assessment for the target population, since inherited retinal dystrophies are rare and a sedating antipsychotic with cardiometabolic risks would be a difficult fit
