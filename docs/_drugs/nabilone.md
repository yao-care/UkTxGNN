## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data was not directly supplied for nabilone (flagged as a data gap). However, supporting literature within the evidence pack describes nabilone as a synthetic analogue of delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol acting as an agonist at cannabinoid CB1 and CB2 receptors. On this basis, the TxGNN prediction rationale proposes that nabilone may act on the endocannabinoid system's modulation of the trigeminovascular pathway — a mechanism theoretically shared between its known antiemetic activity and migraine pathophysiology.

This mechanistic hypothesis is plausible but entirely unproven for migraine disorder specifically: no clinical trials or peer-reviewed publications in this evidence pack evaluate nabilone in migraine. By contrast, a related indication in the same prediction set — **headache disorder** (rank 3, evidence level L3) — is supported by an actual randomised controlled trial in medication overuse headache (PMID 23070400), suggesting the underlying mechanistic hypothesis has some real-world grounding, even though it has not yet been tested in migraine itself.

Given the complete absence of direct evidence for migraine disorder, this prediction should currently be regarded as a hypothesis-generating signal rather than a therapeutically actionable finding.
