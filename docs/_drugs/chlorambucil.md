## One-Sentence Summary

Chlorambucil is an oral nitrogen mustard alkylating agent, originally used for Hodgkin lymphoma, follicular lymphoma, mature B-cell neoplasms and related haematological cancers.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **chronic lymphocytic leukaemia/small lymphocytic lymphoma with immunoglobulin heavy chain variable-region (IGHV) gene somatic hypermutation**, a molecular subtype of a disease the drug is already used in.
No clinical trials and **no subtype-specific publications** are currently available for this top prediction, so the support is mainly model-based and indirect.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not currently available. Based on known information, chlorambucil is a DNA-alkylating nitrogen mustard. Its efficacy in B-cell malignancies is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to CLL/SLL subtypes.

The predicted indication is a molecular subset of CLL/SLL. This is therefore largely an existing use, or a subtype of one, rather than true repurposing.

The second-ranked prediction, pregerminal centre CLL/SLL (the unmutated IGHV subtype), has the same score and the same rationale. Its single supporting paper (PMID 12577769, 2003) is a review advocating risk-adapted CLL management, not subtype-specific efficacy data.

Other predictions are weaker:
- **Primary pulmonary lymphoma** (rank 4): mostly pulmonary MALT lymphoma, an indolent lymphoma where alkylators are used. It has the most literature (case reports, retrospective series, guidelines), but this is largely indirect.
- **ALK-positive large B-cell lymphoma, well-differentiated fetal adenocarcinoma of the lung, pulmonary blastoma**: model prediction only, with no trials or literature.
- **Acute lymphoblastic leukaemia**: already a listed original indication, and the supplied papers are indirect.
- **Small cell lung carcinoma**: only dated clinical studies and preclinical work, which do not support current use.

## Safety Considerations

Label-based warnings and contraindications are not available in the data compiled for this report.

- **Contraindicated conditions (knowledge graph, not label text)**: epilepsy; thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, leukopenia, lymphopenia, agranulocytosis and anaemia; nephrotic syndrome; interstitial lung disease and pulmonary fibrosis; drug-induced liver injury; and acute leukaemia (including mixed phenotype acute leukaemia). These must be checked against the SmPC.
- **Dose-related neurotoxicity**: seizures and CNS toxicity appeared at high doses in older trials (PMIDs 2825510 and 3179770).
- **Second malignancies**: the Hodgkin disease follow-up studies describe raised risks of secondary cancers, including acute leukaemia, after treatment.
- **Drug interactions** (100 recorded in total; examples):
  - **Major**: deferiprone, samarium (153Sm) lexidronam.
  - **Moderate**: amphotericin B (including lipid formulations), mercaptopurine, chloramphenicol, roflumilast, palifermin, strontium-89 chloride.
  - **Minor**: levofloxacin.
  - Several others are of unknown severity (for example warfarin, prednisolone, prednisone, omeprazole, simvastatin, mesalazine, levothyroxine).

Please refer to the SmPC and BNF for full safety information. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The top-ranked prediction is a molecular subtype of CLL/SLL, a disease where chlorambucil is already established, so it adds little new repurposing value. Its evidence is L4 (indirect, with no trials or subtype-specific publications). Chlorambucil has also been displaced as first-line therapy in older CLL patients by agents such as ibrutinib, as the RESONATE-2 data show. The other predictions rest on model scores alone or on dated and preclinical studies. Primary pulmonary lymphoma is the most plausible of them, but its evidence is case-level and retrospective.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Current MHRA SmPC and BNF text, including approved indications, warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data (for example from DrugBank)
- Subtype-specific data on chlorambucil in IGHV-mutated and unmutated CLL/SLL, and review against current NICE guidance
- A focused evidence review for primary pulmonary (MALT) lymphoma if that indication is to be pursued
- A safety monitoring plan covering myelosuppression, neurotoxicity, secondary malignancy and interaction checks
