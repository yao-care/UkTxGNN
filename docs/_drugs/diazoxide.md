## Diazoxide: KATP channel opener → hypotrichosis simplex of the scalp
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Diazoxide is a potassium channel opener used in the management of conditions of excess insulin secretion. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **hypotrichosis simplex of the scalp**, but **no clinical trials and no publications** directly study this condition, so the prediction rests on model output and a plausible mechanism alone.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

According to the Clinical Pharmacology section of the US FDA-approved prescribing information for diazoxide: "PROGLYCEM produces a dose-related increase in blood glucose, due primarily to an inhibition of insulin release from the pancreas, and also to an extrapancreatic effect. The hyperglycemic effect of PROGLYCEM begins within an hour and generally lasts no more than 8 hours in the presence of normal renal function." ([DailyMed: Proglycem (diazoxide) prescribing information, Teva Pharmaceuticals USA, Inc.](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=b16c7832-2fd9-49af-b923-1dc0d91fd6e2), label version 2026-10-06; accessed 2026-11-19.) Pharmacologically, diazoxide opens ATP-sensitive potassium (KATP) channels. Minoxidil, which is used to promote hair growth, acts through the same channel family, so a hair-growth effect is biologically plausible.

Hypertrichosis is a well-known adverse effect of systemic diazoxide, which further supports the idea that the drug can stimulate hair follicles. However, hypotrichosis simplex of the scalp is a rare inherited condition, and it is not known whether KATP modulation would help. The high score (0.9996) is a model output only and is not evidence of clinical benefit.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindication-type disease relations** (from relations recorded in the data compiled for this report, not label text): kidney disease and kidney failure, liver disease, gallbladder disease, congestive heart failure, coronary artery disease, cerebrovascular disorder, diabetes mellitus, diabetic ketoacidosis, hyperglycaemia, gout, uraemia, potassium deficiency, orthostatic hypotension, postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome and multiple system atrophy. Check these against the SmPC.
- **Drug Interactions** (100 recorded; moderate-level examples): other glucose-lowering drugs (acarbose, metformin, pioglitazone, alogliptin, albiglutide, dulaglutide, chlorpropamide, and the gliflozins canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, empagliflozin and ertugliflozin), corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, budesonide, triamcinolone), bupropion, morphine and dronabinol.
- **Hypertrichosis** is a recognised adverse effect of systemic diazoxide. Topical use for scalp hair has been studied only in animals, and systemic effects such as hyperglycaemia and fluid retention would need to be addressed in any development plan.

Please refer to the SmPC and BNF for full safety information. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction is supported only by the model score and a plausible KATP mechanism shared with minoxidil. There are no trials or publications on this disease, so the evidence level is L5.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data and evidence that KATP opening affects this specific genetic condition
- Preclinical work, ideally with a topical formulation, because systemic exposure carries significant metabolic and cardiovascular risk
- A safety review of systemic absorption, given the interactions and disease-related cautions above
- Confirmation of the SmPC warnings and contraindications for the UK products
