# Cholesterol: From No Recorded Original Indication to Homozygous Familial Hypercholesterolaemia

## One-Sentence Summary

Cholesterol is an endogenous lipid, and the data compiled for this report contain no approved indication for the single UK authorisation listed.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be associated with **homozygous familial hypercholesterolaemia (HoFH)**, but there are **no registered clinical trials** and **17 publications**, none of which tests cholesterol as a treatment.
The prediction most likely reflects a disease-association signal rather than a genuine therapeutic opportunity, since giving cholesterol would be expected to worsen this condition.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data are not currently available for cholesterol. Cholesterol is the endogenous lipid that accumulates in HoFH because LDL receptor function is defective.

The high TxGNN score most plausibly reflects the strong biological link between cholesterol and this disease, not a therapeutic role. Administering cholesterol would be expected to worsen HoFH, not treat it. The publications retrieved cover HoFH treatments (such as evinacumab, inclisiran and lerodalcibep), its genetics and its natural history. None of them examines cholesterol as an intervention.

This prediction should therefore be read as a disease-association signal, not a repurposing opportunity.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the SmPC and BNF for safety information. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

Two points from the evidence are relevant to safety. In HoFH, administering cholesterol would be expected to worsen the disease. In nonhuman primate studies, a high-fat, high-cholesterol diet accelerated SIV disease progression.

---

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction is a disease-association signal, not evidence of therapeutic benefit. No clinical trials exist, the literature concerns other HoFH treatments, and supplementing cholesterol would be expected to be harmful. The other top predictions are equally unsupported, and several are veterinary conditions outside the scope of human repurposing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Safety information (warnings and contraindications) from the SmPC and BNF
- Mechanism of action data
- Original indication and dosage form details for the UK authorisation
- A credible mechanistic rationale showing how cholesterol could treat, rather than aggravate, any proposed indication
