# Sirolimus: From Renal Transplant Rejection Prophylaxis to Liposarcoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Sirolimus (DrugBank DB00877) is an mTOR inhibitor originally developed as an immunosuppressant to prevent renal transplant rejection. The TxGNN model predicts it may also be effective for **Liposarcoma**, with **5 clinical trials** and **12 publications** currently informing this direction — though only one trial tests sirolimus itself directly in this indication.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data for sirolimus is currently a flagged data gap in this evidence pack. Based on established pharmacological knowledge, however, sirolimus is a rapamycin-class **mTOR (mechanistic target of rapamycin) inhibitor**, originally used to suppress T-cell activation and prevent rejection after kidney transplantation. This mechanism is well documented in the supporting literature — for example, studies of sirolimus in renal transplant recipients show it *reduces* rather than promotes malignancy risk compared with calcineurin inhibitors (PMID 16434506, PMID 26093731), a finding that first drew attention to its antitumour potential.

Liposarcoma, and particularly its dedifferentiated subtype, has been shown to exhibit constitutive activation of the Akt-mTOR and MAPK signalling pathways (PMID 26518767), providing a plausible mechanistic bridge between an mTOR-inhibiting immunosuppressant and this sarcoma subtype. Preclinical work combining rapamycin with the autophagy inhibitor chloroquine has also demonstrated synergistic antitumour activity in well-differentiated and dedifferentiated liposarcoma models (PMID 37400145, PMID 36309387).

It is important to note that most of the clinical evidence base for mTOR inhibition in liposarcoma involves **related rapalogues** (temsirolimus, ridaforolimus, everolimus) rather than sirolimus itself. Only one identified trial (NCT02821507) directly tests sirolimus in myxoid liposarcoma, meaning the mechanistic rationale is stronger as a drug-class argument than as sirolimus-specific evidence.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Evidence specific to sirolimus in liposarcoma is currently limited to a single completed, non-randomised Phase 2 trial (NCT02821507); most other supporting trials and literature involve related rapalogues (temsirolimus, everolimus, ridaforolimus) rather than sirolimus itself. Combined with a Blocking-severity data gap on UK/MHRA warnings and contraindications (preventing any safety pre-assessment) and the absence of any current UK marketing authorisation for sirolimus, the evidence base does not yet support proceeding.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- MHRA-sourced SmPC warnings, contraindications and drug interaction data (resolves blocking data gap DG001)
- Confirmed mechanism of action from DrugBank API (resolves DG002)
- Direct sirolimus (rather than class-analogue) trial data in liposarcoma, ideally randomised
- Clarification of UK regulatory pathway, since sirolimus currently has zero UK marketing authorisations recorded

**Note for prioritisation:** within this same evidence pack, two other predicted indications for sirolimus show materially stronger, more mature evidence — **lymphangiomyoma/lung PEComa** (L1, Phase 3 MILES trial NCT00414648 underpinning an existing regulatory approval for LAM) and **PEComa** (L2, supported by the nab-sirolimus AMPECT precedent). These may warrant evaluation ahead of liposarcoma given their more direct and robust evidentiary support.
