# Pivmecillinam: From Bacterial Infection (Original Indication Not Recorded) to Bronchitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Pivmecillinam is an amidinopenicillin antibacterial (a prodrug of mecillinam) that is currently marketed in the UK as a single-ingredient 200 mg tablet.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **bronchitis**, and **9 publications** (mostly 1977-1990 trials of a pivmecillinam/pivampicillin combination) relate to this direction, with **no registered clinical trials** identified.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, pivmecillinam is a prodrug of mecillinam, which inhibits bacterial penicillin-binding protein 2 (PBP2). It has often been combined with pivampicillin to broaden coverage.

Bacterial exacerbations of chronic bronchitis and acute bronchitis are treated with antibacterials. The link is therefore a conventional antibacterial one and not a novel repurposing mechanism. Activity against common respiratory pathogens plausibly supports use in this setting.

Important caveats: the existing studies date from 1977 to 1990 and mostly test the combination product (Miraxid), not pivmecillinam alone. Changes in antimicrobial resistance and current guidelines limit how relevant these findings are today. Other predicted indications (for example thrombotic and coagulation disorders, diabetic retinopathy, rheumatoid arthritis) have no plausible mechanism and no supporting evidence, so they are not pursued here.
