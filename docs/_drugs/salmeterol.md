# Salmeterol: From Asthma and Emphysema to Bronchitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Salmeterol is a long-acting beta2-adrenergic agonist originally used for asthma (intrinsic and allergic) and pulmonary emphysema.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **bronchitis** (essentially chronic bronchitis within COPD), with **9 clinical trials** and **19 publications** currently supporting this direction.
This is close to an on-label extension rather than true repurposing.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, salmeterol is a long-acting beta2-adrenergic agonist. It relaxes airway smooth muscle, reduces airflow obstruction, and may improve mucociliary clearance.

The predicted indication is essentially chronic bronchitis within COPD. This overlaps heavily with the original use in emphysema and obstructive airway disease. Chronic bronchitis and emphysema are the two main components of COPD, and they share the same airflow-limitation physiology that bronchodilators treat. The trials and reviews summarised below support benefit in this population.

## Safety Considerations

**Contraindication-related disease associations (from a knowledge graph, not label text):** the TxGNN knowledge graph links salmeterol to several conditions, including hypertension, long QT syndrome (including familial forms), epilepsy, hyperglycaemia, herpes simplex virus keratitis, osteoporosis, thymus gland disease, and a group of pituitary hormone deficiency and hypogonadotropic hypogonadism disorders. These associations should be checked against the SmPC before any clinical use.

**Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions were identified. Examples:
- **Major:** clarithromycin
- **Moderate:** epinephrine, famotidine, isometheptene, dolasetron, diethylpropion, and several antidiabetic agents (acarbose, alogliptin, canagliflozin, chlorpropamide, dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, albiglutide, dulaglutide)
- **Minor:** corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, triamcinolone, dexamethasone, beclometasone, betamethasone, budesonide)

**Class safety note:** Cochrane reviews of regular salmeterol in asthma report concerns about serious adverse events. In asthma, salmeterol should not be used as monotherapy and should be combined with an inhaled corticosteroid.

Please refer to the SmPC and BNF for full warnings and contraindications. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Multiple completed Phase 3 trials and meta-analyses support salmeterol (alone or with fluticasone) in COPD and chronic bronchitis, which is close to its existing use in emphysema and obstructive airway disease. Guardrails are needed because of LABA class safety concerns and the cardiovascular and metabolic associations listed above.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Licensed indication text, PL number and manufacturer details from the MHRA record
- SmPC warnings and contraindications (not available in the data compiled for this report)
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Confirmation that the disease term "bronchitis" corresponds to chronic bronchitis/COPD within the existing licensed indication
- A monitoring plan for patients with cardiovascular disease, QT prolongation, diabetes or epilepsy, and an interaction check for strong CYP3A4 inhibitors such as clarithromycin
