## Tiotropium: From Bronchitis and Asthma to Obstructive Lung Disease
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Tiotropium is a long-acting inhaled muscarinic antagonist, originally used for bronchitis, asthma and pulmonary emphysema.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **obstructive lung disease**, but this is essentially an on-label use rather than true repurposing, since the predicted indication is the parent category of its existing indications.
It is supported by **40 clinical trials** and **20 publications** in the data compiled for this report, including several large Phase 3 trials.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on its drug class, tiotropium is a long-acting muscarinic antagonist (LAMA). It blocks M3 receptors on airway smooth muscle, reducing cholinergic bronchoconstriction and mucus secretion.

"Obstructive lung disease" is the parent category of tiotropium's existing indications (bronchitis, asthma and emphysema). The high TxGNN score therefore largely reflects an established use, not a new discovery. The same reasoning applies to the closely related prediction of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), which is a core established indication.

Other predictions in the list have much weaker support. Examples include respiratory malformation, compensatory emphysema, interstitial emphysema, hyperlucent lung, tracheal stenosis, Rienhoff syndrome and a CD8alpha-related immunodeficiency. These have little or no trial or literature evidence, and the mechanistic rationale is weak, so they are not recommended for progression.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications and precautions suggested by the knowledge graph** (these are not label text and must be confirmed against the SmPC):
  - Angle-closure (closed-angle) glaucoma
  - Benign prostatic hyperplasia
  - Bladder neck obstruction
  - Impaired renal function
- **Drug Interactions**: Interaction screening returned 100 entries. The main ones are with other anticholinergic or antimuscarinic drugs, rated Moderate, with additive anticholinergic effects likely. These include hyoscyamine, atropine, glycopyrronium (including topical), clidinium, dicyclomine, trospium, mepenzolate, methscopolamine, propantheline and scopolamine. Eluxadoline, prucalopride and inhaled human insulin are also rated Moderate. Cimetidine is rated Minor.
- **Ocular safety**: An observational study (NCT06525051) examined the effect of tiotropium on anterior chamber parameters and intraocular pressure. A meta-analysis (PMID 21672999) examined mortality with the Respimat inhaler.

Please refer to the SmPC and BNF for full safety information. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Several large Phase 3 trials and meta-analyses support tiotropium in COPD and asthma, so the lead prediction is strongly supported. However, it is an established use rather than a novel repurposing opportunity. Safety guardrails are needed because of anticholinergic effects.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Full SmPC and BNF warnings and contraindications (this information was not available in the data compiled for this report)
- Confirmation of the UK marketing authorisation number, dosage form and approved indication text
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Monitoring plan for patients with glaucoma, urinary outflow obstruction or renal impairment, and for those taking other anticholinergic drugs
- No further development of the lower-ranked predictions (respiratory malformation, compensatory and interstitial emphysema, hyperlucent lung, tracheal stenosis, Rienhoff syndrome and the CD8alpha-related immunodeficiency) unless new supporting evidence emerges
