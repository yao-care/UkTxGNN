## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general knowledge, dornase alfa is a recombinant human DNase I that cleaves extracellular DNA to reduce the viscosity of airway sputum. Its efficacy in cystic fibrosis relates to this action on DNA-rich airway secretions.

For Fryns Smeets Thiry syndrome, no identifiable mechanistic link exists. Fryns-type syndromes are rare congenital malformation syndromes, and there is no known pathology driven by extracellular DNA. A score of 0.5 carries essentially no predictive information, so this prediction should be regarded as a model output rather than a credible repurposing signal.

The same applies to the other nine predicted indications reviewed (all scored 0.5, all L5, all Hold):

- **Fryns Fabry Remans syndrome, frontonasal dysplasia (phocomelic upper limbs and Klippel-Feil types), frontonasal malformation with cloacal exstrophy:** congenital structural malformations with no plausible role for DNase.
- **Congenital nervous system disorder and central nervous system origin vertigo:** the first is too heterogeneous to define a mechanism, and inhaled dornase alfa has minimal CNS exposure.
- **Flinders Island spotted fever:** a rickettsial infection treated with antibiotics; dornase alfa has no antirickettsial activity.
- **Sensory peripheral neuropathy:** neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) have been speculatively implicated, but this is unsupported clinically, and inhaled delivery would not reach peripheral nerves.
- **Galactorrhoea-hyperprolactinaemia:** a disorder of pituitary and dopaminergic prolactin control, unrelated to DNase activity.
