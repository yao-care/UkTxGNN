## Frovatriptan: From Migraine to Migraine with Brainstem Aura
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Frovatriptan is a triptan (5-HT1B/1D receptor agonist) originally used for the acute treatment of migraine with or without aura.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **migraine with brainstem aura**, but there are currently **no registered clinical trials** and the **18 publications** compiled are mostly reviews of migraine in general rather than of this subtype specifically.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Frovatriptan is a second-generation triptan with a long elimination half-life (about 26 hours). Its mechanism is thought to arise from agonism at serotonin 5-HT1B and 5-HT1D receptors, which inhibits intracranial and extracerebral artery vasodilation and may also reduce neurogenic inflammation. Structured mechanism-of-action data were not available in the data compiled for this report, but this description is consistent with the published reviews.

Migraine with brainstem aura is a subtype of migraine and shares core pathophysiology with the original indication, including trigeminovascular activation, CGRP release and cranial vasodilation. This overlap is consistent with the high TxGNN score.

There is an important caveat. The available literature covers migraine with aura in general (for example, a subgroup analysis comparing frovatriptan with zolmitriptan), not brainstem aura specifically. Triptans have traditionally been used cautiously or avoided in brainstem aura (formerly basilar-type migraine) because of theoretical concerns about vasoconstriction in the posterior circulation. A dedicated safety evaluation is therefore needed before any recommendation.

The other two predictions (atrophoderma vermiculata and ulerythema ophryogenesis) have no supporting trials or literature and no evident mechanistic link to 5-HT1B/1D agonism. They are likely knowledge-graph artefacts and are not taken forward here.

## Safety Considerations

**Contraindications flagged in the knowledge graph** (derived from drug-disease relationships, not from label text, so they must be confirmed against the SmPC):
- Cardiovascular and cerebrovascular: coronary artery disease, myocardial ischaemia, Prinzmetal angina, coronary vasospasm, hypertension, stroke, cerebral infarction, transient ischaemic attack, vertebrobasilar insufficiency, sudden cardiac arrest, heart conduction disease
- Vascular: peripheral vascular disease, peripheral arterial disease, Raynaud disease, ischaemic bowel disease
- Migraine subtypes: familial or sporadic hemiplegic migraine
- Metabolic: diabetes mellitus, diabetic ketoacidosis, obesity
- Other: serotonin syndrome

Vertebrobasilar insufficiency and hemiplegic migraine are particularly relevant to the brainstem aura question.

**Drug interactions** (31 recorded; the main ones are listed):
- **Major:** dolasetron, palonosetron, ondansetron, granisetron (5-HT3 antagonists), lorcaserin, fenfluramine, dexfenfluramine, sibutramine and methylene blue. Many of these relate to serotonergic effects.
- **Moderate:** morphine, codeine, hydrocodone and deferasirox.

Please refer to the SmPC and BNF for full safety information. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction is biologically plausible, since brainstem aura is a migraine subtype, but evidence is at model-prediction and general-migraine level only (L4). There are no trials and no subtype-specific data. Triptans have traditionally been used with caution in brainstem aura, and the knowledge graph flags vertebrobasilar insufficiency and hemiplegic migraine as contraindications.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A dedicated safety review of triptan use in brainstem aura, including posterior circulation risk, against current UK guidance and the SmPC
- Subtype-specific clinical or observational data on frovatriptan in brainstem aura
- Confirmed SmPC warnings and contraindications, and the MHRA authorisation details (PL number, dosage form, approved indication)
- Structured mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
