# Sertraline: Evaluating Repurposing Potential for Agoraphobia

## One-Sentence Summary

Sertraline is a selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor (SSRI) with an established evidence base — reflected in completed Phase 4 trials — in panic disorder and social phobia. The TxGNN model predicts additional efficacy in **Agoraphobia**, a condition that is clinically highly comorbid with panic disorder, and this is currently the best-supported of eight TxGNN-predicted candidates, with **4 clinical trials** (including a completed Phase 4 head-to-head RCT) and **20 publications**, several at systematic review/meta-analysis level.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data was not provided in this evidence pack. Based on the information available, sertraline is an SSRI; its serotonergic mechanism underlies established efficacy in panic disorder and social phobia, evidenced here by a completed Phase 4 RCT (NCT00677352, sertraline vs paroxetine, n=321).

Agoraphobia and panic disorder are closely linked conditions — agoraphobia is clinically highly comorbid with panic disorder, and SSRIs are already first-line pharmacotherapy for that combined presentation. This mechanistic and clinical overlap is the basis for the TxGNN prediction: rather than an unrelated new target, agoraphobia sits directly adjacent to sertraline's existing anxiety-disorder evidence base, which is reflected in the systematic reviews and network meta-analyses of "panic disorder with or without agoraphobia" identified below.

It is worth noting that TxGNN generated seven further candidate indications for sertraline in this evidence pack (mostly personality disorders), but these lack direct mechanistic rationale or supporting evidence and are not recommended to proceed (see table at the end of this report).

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Agoraphobia is closely linked to panic disorder, for which sertraline already has completed Phase 4 RCT and systematic review/meta-analysis level evidence. However, no identified trial enrols agoraphobia as the primary diagnosis independent of panic disorder, so the evidence remains indirect.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Product label safety data (key warnings, contraindications) — currently a blocking data gap
- Detailed mechanism of action documentation for sertraline
- Confirmation of UK marketing authorisation pathway, given current "Not marketed" status
- A trial or evidence synthesis with agoraphobia as the primary (rather than comorbid) endpoint
