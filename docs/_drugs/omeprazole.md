# Omeprazole: From Gastro-oesophageal Reflux and Peptic Ulcer Disease to Duodenogastric Reflux

## One-Sentence Summary

Omeprazole is a proton pump inhibitor originally used for acid-related upper gastrointestinal conditions such as gastric and duodenal ulcers, gastro-oesophageal reflux disease and Zollinger-Ellison syndrome.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **duodenogastric reflux**, but the supporting evidence is limited: **1 registered clinical trial (not relevant to efficacy)** and **15 publications**, mostly small human studies and animal work.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, omeprazole is a proton pump inhibitor that suppresses gastric acid secretion. Its efficacy in acid-related disease is well established, and mechanistically it may be relevant to conditions that coexist with acid injury.

Duodenogastric reflux involves bile and duodenal contents flowing back into the stomach. It often occurs alongside acid-related disease such as gastro-oesophageal reflux disease and Barrett's oesophagus. Small human studies in Barrett's oesophagus and in children with oesophageal bile reflux suggest omeprazole may reduce symptoms and acid-related mucosal injury.

The link is, however, indirect. Omeprazole does not directly treat bile reflux, and some data suggest it does not reduce reflux. Rat studies also raise a safety concern: acid blockade combined with duodenogastric reflux promoted gastric neoplasia. The high TxGNN score is a model prediction, not clinical evidence.

A second candidate, **duodenal obstruction** (score 99.64%), was also generated. It is a mechanical condition needing endoscopic or surgical relief, and omeprazole would not resolve an established obstruction. The retrieved literature consists of case reports and does not test omeprazole for this purpose. The evidence level is L5 and the recommendation is **Hold**. The score likely reflects network proximity to peptic ulcer disease rather than a distinct therapeutic effect.

## Safety Considerations

- **Conditions linked to contraindication or caution in the knowledge graph** (not taken from label text): liver failure, liver disease, gallbladder disease, congestive heart failure, osteoporosis, postmenopausal osteoporosis, bone fracture and chronic atrophic gastritis.
- **Drug interactions:** the interaction query returned 100 interactions, 20 of which are listed in the data. Major-severity examples are **acalabrutinib** and **atazanavir**. Moderate examples include hydrochlorothiazide, alprazolam, atorvastatin, fenofibrate, apalutamide, armodafinil, amphetamine, anagrelide, and several amphotericin B and aminoglycoside (amikacin) products. Minor examples include aspirin, nifedipine, lidocaine and ambrisentan.
- **Preclinical signal:** two rat studies suggest acid suppression combined with duodenogastric reflux may promote gastric neoplasia. The relevance to humans is unknown.

Please refer to the SmPC and BNF for full safety information. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold (research question only)**

**Rationale:**
The TxGNN score is very high, but the human evidence is limited to small studies in Barrett's oesophagus and paediatric bile reflux. There is no trial testing omeprazole for duodenogastric reflux. Omeprazole also does not directly address bile reflux, and the rat data raise a carcinogenesis concern. Duodenal obstruction should remain on hold, as omeprazole cannot relieve a mechanical obstruction.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data to clarify the link with bile reflux
- Controlled human studies of omeprazole in duodenogastric reflux, with objective measurement of bile exposure
- Clarification of the long-term gastric neoplasia signal in the setting of reflux plus acid suppression
- SmPC warnings, contraindications and indication text for the UK authorisation, as these are not available in the compiled data
