## Acrivastine: From Allergic Rhinitis to Allergic Urticaria
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Acrivastine is a short-acting H1-receptor antagonist (antihistamine), originally used for allergic and vasomotor rhinitis and related upper-airway and eye conditions.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **allergic urticaria**,
with **no registered clinical trials** but **7 publications** (mostly reviews, plus one small double-blind study) currently supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, acrivastine is a second-generation H1-receptor antagonist (a side-chain-reduced metabolite of triprolidine). Its efficacy in allergic rhinitis has been shown, and mechanistically it may be applicable to allergic urticaria.

Histamine released from mast cells is the central mediator of wheal-and-flare in urticaria, so H1 blockade is mechanistically well supported. Non-sedating antihistamines are the standard first-line therapy for urticaria, and a 1991 review reports that double-blind trials found acrivastine (usually 8 mg three times daily) effective and well tolerated in chronic urticaria.

Caution is needed: much of this support is class-level evidence for antihistamines in general. The prediction largely extends an existing, well-understood drug class to a closely related allergic condition. Other predictions (for example nasal cavity disease and acute laryngopharyngitis) mostly overlap with existing indications, and others (recalcitrant atopic dermatitis, atopic IgE responsiveness) are poorly supported.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The H1-antagonist mechanism is plausible for urticaria, but the evidence for allergic urticaria is limited to older reviews and one small pharmacodynamic study, with no registered clinical trials (L4). Cold urticaria has a small 1988 double-blind crossover trial (18 patients) that looks promising, but it is old and unconfirmed. Other predicted indications overlap with existing uses or lack support.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Confirm the approved indication text and authorisation details for the UK product, including whether urticaria is already covered
- Mechanism of action data (currently unavailable)
- Full SmPC warnings and contraindications (currently missing), and verification of the knowledge-graph contraindications
- Modern, adequately powered randomised trials in chronic urticaria, and ideally cold urticaria, with comparison against current first-line non-sedating antihistamines
- Comparison with the current UK guideline position, since urticaria is already managed with licensed antihistamines
