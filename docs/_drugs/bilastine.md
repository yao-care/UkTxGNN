# Bilastine: From Allergic Rhinitis and Urticaria to Duodenal Obstruction

## One-Sentence Summary

Bilastine is a second-generation antihistamine, originally used for allergic rhinitis, urticaria and angioedema. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **duodenal obstruction**, but **no clinical trials and no publications** currently support this prediction, and no biologically plausible mechanism has been identified.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Bilastine is known to act on the histamine H1 receptor (HRH1), where it behaves as a selective peripheral H1 antihistamine with minimal central nervous system penetration. Its efficacy in allergic rhinitis and urticaria is well established.

The link to duodenal obstruction is weak. Duodenal obstruction is usually mechanical or functional, for example from strictures, tumours or motility disorders. H1 receptor blockade has no recognised role in these conditions. The high score most likely reflects associations in the knowledge graph, such as shared gastrointestinal or histamine-receptor-class connections, rather than a real pharmacological rationale. This prediction should be regarded as a hypothesis only.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests only on a model score, with no trials, no literature and no plausible mechanism linking H1 antagonism to duodenal obstruction. It is not a priority for further development.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A credible mechanistic hypothesis for H1 receptor involvement in duodenal obstruction, supported by preclinical data
- Any supporting clinical or observational evidence, which does not currently exist
- Confirmed UK authorisation details and approved indications (SmPC and BNF)
- Warnings, contraindications and interaction data from the SmPC and BNF

**Note on other predictions for this drug:** Lower-ranked predictions, such as cold urticaria and allergic urticaria, do have supporting evidence. This includes a completed Phase 2/3 crossover trial in cold urticaria and a Phase 3 randomised controlled trial and meta-analyses in chronic urticaria. These conditions lie within or close to the existing urticaria indication, so they are better described as extensions of current use than as true repurposing.
