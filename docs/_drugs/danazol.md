# Danazol: From Endometriosis to Amenorrhea

## One-Sentence Summary

Danazol is a synthetic attenuated androgen historically used for endometriosis, benign fibrocystic breast disease and hereditary angioedema. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **amenorrhea (disease)**, with **0 registered clinical trials** and **20 supporting publications**, several of which describe its established amenorrhoea-inducing effect in clinical use.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data for Danazol is not recorded in this evidence pack (structured MOA field is a data gap). However, the supporting literature consistently describes it as a synthetic steroid derived from 17-alpha-ethinyltestosterone that acts centrally to inhibit pituitary gonadotropin (LH/FSH) secretion, thereby suppressing ovarian steroidogenesis and gametogenesis (PMID 2404115).

This antigonadotropic mechanism is not incidental to Danazol's established uses — it is the mechanism by which the drug produces its therapeutic effect in endometriosis: suppression of ovarian function induces a hypo-oestrogenic, amenorrhoeic state that causes regression of ectopic endometrial tissue (PMID 16280355). Several publications in this pack directly document Danazol being used specifically *to induce* amenorrhoea, including for menstrual suppression in transgender individuals (PMID 39051650) and in a paper explicitly titled "The induction of amenorrhoea" (PMID 1533675).

The TxGNN prediction therefore aligns closely with well-documented pharmacology: amenorrhoea is not a distant new indication but an established, mechanistically direct effect of Danazol that underlies several of its recognised uses. This strengthens biological plausibility, though clinical evidence remains observational/narrative rather than trial-based.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
- SmPC warnings/contraindications data for Danazol are marked as a **Blocking** data gap in this pack, which prevents even a preliminary (S1) safety assessment; combined with Danazol currently having no UK marketing authorisation (0 licences, "Not marketed"), progression cannot be responsibly recommended at this stage despite mechanistically plausible and literature-supported evidence for its amenorrhoea-inducing effect.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- SmPC/PIL safety data (warnings, contraindications, drug interactions) sourced from MHRA or an equivalent regulator
- Confirmation of UK market access route (licensing, import, or special licence supply) given current "Not marketed" status
- Formal study-type classification and relevance grading of the cited literature (currently unclassified/"pending")
- Assessment of whether "induced amenorrhoea" as a treatment goal (vs. amenorrhoea as a primary disease) is the appropriate framing for further evaluation
