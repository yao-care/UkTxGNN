## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, diazepam is a positive allosteric modulator of GABA-A receptors. It enhances inhibitory neurotransmission and produces sedative, anxiolytic, anticonvulsant and muscle-relaxant effects.

Insomnia is often comorbid with anxiety, and anxiolysis and sedation are plausible routes to shorter sleep latency. Benzodiazepines as a class are used clinically as short-term hypnotics, so the prediction is mechanistically reasonable.

However, diazepam is not a first-line hypnotic. Its long-acting active metabolite (nordiazepam) causes residual daytime sedation, and tolerance, dependence and disruption of sleep architecture limit long-term use. The retrieved literature is mostly reviews, herbal-medicine studies and discussions of pharmacokinetics or safety. It contains almost no diazepam-specific efficacy evidence.

## Safety Considerations

**Contraindications / cautions flagged by the knowledge graph** (drug-disease edges, not label text):
- Hepatic: liver failure, liver disease, gallbladder disease
- Respiratory: respiratory failure, sleep apnoea syndromes (obstructive and central), congenital central hypoventilation syndrome, lung disease
- Eye: open-angle and angle-closure glaucoma
- Neuromuscular: myasthenia gravis and neuromuscular junction disease
- Psychiatric: anxiety disorder, neurotic disorder, dysthymic disorder, substance-related disorder and substance abuse/dependence

Sleep apnoea and respiratory disease are particularly relevant to a hypnotic use.

**Drug interactions** (100 recorded; examples shown):
- **Major:** morphine (additive CNS and respiratory depression)
- **Moderate:** bupropion, aprepitant, omeprazole, esomeprazole, cimetidine, clarithromycin, cisapride, clotrimazole, miconazole, dronabinol, metoclopramide
- **Minor:** dexamethasone, betamethasone, antacids (magnesium oxide, aluminium hydroxide, calcium carbonate)

**Other signals from the literature:** dependence, tolerance, withdrawal, abuse potential, cognitive decline and falls in older adults with long-term benzodiazepine use.

Please refer to the SmPC and BNF for complete safety information. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanism is plausible and benzodiazepines are used as short-term hypnotics, but there are no registered trials and no diazepam-specific efficacy evidence. Diazepam is not a first-line hypnotic because of residual sedation, dependence and tolerance. The prediction therefore remains a research question rather than an actionable repurposing opportunity.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data (currently unavailable) and a clear comparison with established short-acting hypnotics
- Diazepam-specific controlled evidence for insomnia, or a justified niche (for example insomnia with comorbid anxiety)
- Full SmPC and BNF warnings and contraindications, which are missing from the data compiled for this report, to allow safety screening
- Verification of the UK authorisation details (dosage form, indication, MHRA licence number)
- A risk assessment for dependence, respiratory depression, sleep apnoea, interactions with opioids, and use in older adults
