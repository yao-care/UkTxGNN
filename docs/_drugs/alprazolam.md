# Alprazolam: From Anxiety Disorder to Insomnia

## One-Sentence Summary

Alprazolam is a benzodiazepine originally used to treat anxiety disorders, including mixed anxiety and depressive disorder.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **insomnia**, but there are **no registered clinical trials** and the **18 publications** retrieved are almost all indirect (no controlled trial of alprazolam for primary insomnia).
The prediction is therefore best viewed as a hypothesis, not a supported indication.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general class pharmacology, alprazolam is a benzodiazepine that enhances GABA-A receptor signalling, producing anxiolytic, sedative and hypnotic effects. Its efficacy in anxiety is established, and mechanistically it could be applicable to insomnia, particularly insomnia accompanied by anxiety.

The link between the original and predicted indications is plausible: anxiety and insomnia frequently co-occur, and sedation is a known effect of the drug class. However, the retrieved literature does not test this link directly. It consists of indirect items such as a panic disorder review, a post-COVID insomnia survey, benzodiazepine utilisation and economic studies, and a combined acupuncture/alprazolam study in which the alprazolam effect cannot be isolated.

The high TxGNN score is a computational prediction, not clinical evidence. Known benzodiazepine risks (dependence, falls, cognitive impairment) and a heavy interaction burden argue against active repurposing without direct trial data.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications (knowledge-graph associations, not label text):** The TxGNN knowledge graph links alprazolam with contraindications in kidney disease, liver disease, gallbladder disease, open-angle and angle-closure glaucoma, obstructive sleep apnoea and other sleep apnoea syndromes, congenital central hypoventilation syndrome, obesity disorders, and substance-related disorders and dependence. These associations should be verified against the SmPC.
- **Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions are recorded; the 20 shown include:
  - **Major:** morphine, including liposomal morphine (additive central nervous system and respiratory depression).
  - **Moderate:** aprepitant, bupropion, cimetidine, clarithromycin, clotrimazole, dronabinol, eliglustat, glycerol phenylbutyrate, metoclopramide, miconazole.
  - **Minor:** antacids (aluminium hydroxide, calcium carbonate, magaldrate, magnesium salts) and corticosteroids (betamethasone, dexamethasone).
- **Class concerns noted in the literature:** dependence, withdrawal and rebound symptoms, falls, memory impairment, and risk in cirrhosis.

Please refer to the SmPC and BNF for full safety information. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The insomnia prediction rests on a model score and general class pharmacology, with no registered trials and no direct controlled evidence. Benzodiazepine dependence and fall risks, plus the large interaction burden, argue against pursuing this without better data.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Randomised, controlled evidence of alprazolam in primary insomnia, or insomnia with comorbid anxiety, compared with established hypnotics and non-drug therapy
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Confirmation of the UK-licensed indications and SmPC warnings and contraindications
- A risk-benefit assessment covering dependence, withdrawal, falls and interactions, particularly in older adults

For context, the same data identified **agoraphobia** (score 99.56%) as a prediction with more direct evidence. It is supported by multiple controlled studies of alprazolam in panic disorder with agoraphobia, though mostly from 1985–1994. It sits close to the existing anxiety and panic use, so it is better seen as an extension of current use than as true repurposing. Current guidance also favours other first-line agents, so it would merit separate evaluation.
