## Alprazolam: Insomnia (disease)

## One-Sentence Summary

Alprazolam is a benzodiazepine that enhances GABA-A receptor signalling, producing sedative and anxiolytic effects. The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **insomnia**, with a prediction score of 0.9981. Supporting evidence is limited: **no registered clinical trials** and **one retrospective observational study** (PMID 39183410) in which alprazolam was given to patients with coronary heart disease and insomnia, placing the evidence at **L3**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general class pharmacology, alprazolam is a benzodiazepine that enhances GABA-A receptor signalling, which produces sedative and hypnotic effects. This gives a plausible mechanistic basis for use in insomnia, particularly where insomnia occurs alongside anxiety.

However, the supporting literature is largely indirect. The retrieved items include a panic disorder review, a post-COVID insomnia cross-sectional survey, benzodiazepine utilisation and economic modelling, a zolpidem deprescribing cohort and a pharmacokinetics review. The only study in which alprazolam was given to patients with insomnia is a retrospective observational study in coronary heart disease, where alprazolam was the control treatment and the comparison arm received moxibustion and ear acupuncture. No randomised controlled trial of alprazolam for insomnia was retrieved.

The high TxGNN score is a computational prediction, not clinical evidence.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions are recorded. Examples of a **major** interaction are morphine and liposomal morphine (additive central nervous system and respiratory depression). **Moderate** interactions include aprepitant, bupropion, cimetidine, clarithromycin, clotrimazole, dronabinol, eliglustat, glycerol phenylbutyrate, metoclopramide and miconazole. **Minor** interactions include several antacids (aluminium hydroxide, calcium carbonate, magnesium salts) and corticosteroids (betamethasone, dexamethasone).
- **Class-level risks from the literature**: long-term benzodiazepine use is associated with dependence, falls, memory impairment and cognitive effects.

Please refer to the SmPC and BNF for full warnings and contraindications. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanism is plausible and one retrospective observational study involved alprazolam in insomnia, but there is no randomised trial and no registered clinical trial. Benzodiazepine risks (dependence, falls, cognitive impairment) and a heavy interaction burden argue against active repurposing without direct evidence. The evidence level is L3.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A randomised controlled trial of alprazolam in insomnia, with comparison against current guideline-recommended treatments
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- A safety and dependence-risk assessment for hypnotic use, including limits on duration and in older patients
- Full SmPC and BNF warnings and contraindications review
