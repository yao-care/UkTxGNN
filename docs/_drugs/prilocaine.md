## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, prilocaine is an amide local anaesthetic that blocks voltage-gated sodium channels in nerve fibres, reducing the generation and conduction of pain signals. Its original indications (premature ejaculation, psychologic dyspareunia) fit a topical or local desensitising effect.

Neuralgia, particularly postherpetic neuralgia, involves abnormal firing of peripheral nociceptive fibres. Blocking sodium channels in these fibres is a plausible way to reduce pain, and topical local anaesthetics are already used in neuropathic pain care. Early reports from 1989 describe lidocaine/prilocaine (EMLA) cream in postherpetic neuralgia, with efficacy and pharmacokinetic data.

Important caveat: most evidence concerns the EMLA eutectic mixture (lidocaine 2.5% plus prilocaine 2.5%), so benefit cannot be attributed to prilocaine alone.

Other top-10 predictions (for example papillary conjunctivitis, manic bipolar disorder, bronchitis, rosacea conjunctivitis) rest on model score only. Others, such as atopic eczema and allergic asthma, are linked to literature that describes safety or allergy problems rather than benefit. These are not recommended for further work at this stage.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications suggested by the knowledge graph** (derived from drug-disease links, not from label text): hypertension, epilepsy, kidney disease, liver disease, diabetes mellitus and diabetic ketoacidosis, coronary artery disease and myocardial infarction, heart conduction disease and atrioventricular block, sudden cardiac arrest, respiratory failure, angle-closure glaucoma, myasthenia gravis and other neuromuscular junction disease, butyrylcholinesterase deficiency, hyperthyroidism/thyrotoxicosis, hemoglobinopathy, and gallbladder disease. These must be confirmed against the SmPC.
- **Drug interactions:** 22 interactions were recorded, most rated Major. Examples of Major interactions include:
  - Other local anaesthetics: lidocaine (including topical), benzocaine (topical) and cinchocaine (topical)
  - Agents associated with methaemoglobinaemia: dapsone (including topical), primaquine, tafenoquine, chloroquine, quinine, rasburicase, sulfasalazine, amyl nitrite, nitric oxide, nitrous acid, silver nitrate (topical) and silver sulfadiazine (topical)
  - Metoclopramide
  
  Moderate: liposomal morphine. Minor: hyaluronidase.
- **Safety signals in the literature:** a case of seizures and methaemoglobinaemia after topical EMLA in a 3.5-year-old child with atopic dermatitis (PMID 27397059); increased systemic absorption through barrier-compromised skin (PMID 40519459); allergic contact dermatitis and purpura after EMLA (PMIDs 7897165, 16354264); and delayed-type hypersensitivity to prilocaine (PMID 21507228).

Please refer to the SmPC and BNF for full safety information. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Neuralgia is the best-supported prediction (evidence level L3), but the evidence is small, mostly from 1989 to 1996, often uncontrolled, and confounded because it concerns the lidocaine/prilocaine combination. The only registered trial (NCT00916942) tested procedural pre-treatment, not neuralgia efficacy. The other nine top predictions have no direct supporting evidence or show safety concerns. The marketing authorisation on record is an injection and does not match the topical cream used in the neuralgia literature.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data, and the SmPC warnings and contraindications for the UK product (the safety data gap is currently blocking)
- Verification of the UK authorisation record, including the approved indication, dosage form and route
- Route compatibility assessment: the evidence concerns topical EMLA cream, whereas the listed product is an injection
- A controlled trial or systematic review of topical lidocaine/prilocaine in postherpetic or other neuralgia, ideally separating the contribution of prilocaine
- A safety plan covering methaemoglobinaemia risk, use in children, and barrier-compromised skin
