# Ticagrelor: From Acute Coronary Syndrome to Intracranial Arteriosclerosis

## One-Sentence Summary

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank has not been obtained (data gap DG002). Based on information available within the evidence pack, ticagrelor is a reversible P2Y12 receptor antagonist that inhibits ADP-induced platelet activation and aggregation. This mechanism underlies its established role in acute coronary syndrome and broader atherothrombotic disease, where large Phase 3 trials (PLATO, THEMIS, EUCLID) have confirmed efficacy and safety of ticagrelor-based antiplatelet regimens.

Intracranial arteriosclerosis (intracranial atherosclerotic disease, ICAD) shares the same underlying pathology — atherosclerotic plaque causing thrombotic vessel narrowing — as coronary and peripheral artery disease, just in a different vascular bed. Dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT) with clopidogrel and aspirin is already the standard short-term treatment for symptomatic ICAD, and several ongoing trials (e.g. CAPTIVA, DREAM-PRIDE) are now testing whether ticagrelor-containing regimens can outperform standard clopidogrel-based DAPT in this population, since recurrent stroke risk remains high even with current therapy.

Mechanistically this is a logical extension of ticagrelor's established antiplatelet action rather than a disease-specific mechanism — the rationale rests on the shared atherothrombotic biology across vascular beds, supported directly by active Phase 3 clinical development in ICAD.
