# Celecoxib: From Rheumatoid Arthritis to Inflammatory Spondylopathy

## One-Sentence Summary

Celecoxib is a selective COX-2 inhibitor NSAID, originally used for rheumatoid arthritis, osteoarthritis, ankylosing spondylitis and juvenile idiopathic arthritis.
The TxGNN model's highest-ranked prediction with real supporting evidence is **Inflammatory Spondylopathy** (ankylosing spondylitis / axial spondyloarthritis), with **11 clinical trials** and **20 publications** currently supporting this direction.
This is largely confirmation of an existing indication rather than novel repurposing.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, celecoxib is a selective COX-2 inhibitor. It reduces prostaglandin E2-driven inflammation and pain.

Inflammatory spondylopathy (ankylosing spondylitis and axial spondyloarthritis) is already an established celecoxib indication, and NSAIDs are first-line therapy for these conditions. The prediction therefore fits closely with the drug's existing use in inflammatory joint disease.

Published work also suggests celecoxib may inhibit radiographic bone progression in spondyloarthritis, possibly through effects on bone metabolism and sacroiliac joint inflammation. This goes beyond symptom relief, but it needs confirmation.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications and comorbidities flagged in the knowledge graph** (these are graph-derived associations, not label text): hypertension, kidney disease, liver disease, congestive heart failure, active peptic ulcer disease, asthma, stroke and cerebrovascular disorders, myocardial infarction, cardiovascular and heart disease, anaemia, gallbladder disease, and substance abuse/dependence.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were recorded. The one flagged Major is **eliglustat**. Moderate interactions include hydrocortisone, metformin, sulfonylureas (glimepiride, glipizide, glyburide, chlorpropamide, acetohexamide), nateglinide, exenatide, cimetidine, levofloxacin, metronidazole, naltrexone, and the aminosalicylates (mesalazine, balsalazide, olsalazine). Anticoagulants, ACE inhibitors and CYP2C9 inhibitors also warrant attention.
- Cardiovascular and gastrointestinal risk is the main safety theme in the literature, though one 2025 cohort study found these risks comparable to those of non-selective NSAIDs in ankylosing spondylitis.

Please refer to the SmPC and BNF for full safety information. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Two completed Phase 3 RCTs in ankylosing spondylitis (NCT00648141, NCT00762463), together with several Phase 4 trials and a recent RCT on radiographic progression, give strong evidence. However, this is an extension of an existing indication rather than true repurposing, and the cardiovascular and GI risk profile needs active management.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Confirmation of the approved indication wording on the UK marketing authorisation, as it was not available in the data compiled for this report
- Mechanism of action data and SmPC warnings and contraindications
- Cardiovascular and GI risk assessment, use of the lowest effective dose for the shortest duration, and review of interacting medicines
- Further confirmation that celecoxib specifically slows radiographic progression, since the existing evidence is mixed and partly observational

The remaining predictions, including the top-scoring rare skeletal dysplasias and WHIM syndrome, should stay on **Hold** because there is no supporting evidence or plausible mechanism.
