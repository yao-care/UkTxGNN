## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed, verified mechanism of action data was not available in this evidence pack (flagged as a High-severity data gap). Based on the pharmacological rationale accompanying the prediction, leflunomide is understood to act as a DHODH inhibitor, blocking the de novo pyrimidine synthesis pathway and producing anti-proliferative and immunomodulatory effects — a mechanism associated with treatment of autoimmune conditions such as rheumatoid arthritis.

Brachydactyly-syndactyly syndrome is a congenital skeletal malformation syndrome, with pathology typically driven by disrupted embryonic limb-patterning genes (e.g. HOX and BMP signalling pathways) rather than by inflammatory or immune-mediated processes. There is no established mechanistic bridge between DHODH-mediated pyrimidine synthesis inhibition and the developmental gene pathways implicated in this syndrome.

Consequently, the biological plausibility of this prediction is low. The link identified by TxGNN appears to be a statistical association within the knowledge graph rather than one supported by known pharmacology, and should be interpreted with substantial caution.
