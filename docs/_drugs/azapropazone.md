# Azapropazone: From Rheumatoid Arthritis to Acromesomelic Dysplasia, Hunter-Thompson Type

## One-Sentence Summary

Azapropazone is a non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) originally used for inflammatory and degenerative joint conditions such as rheumatoid arthritis, ankylosing spondylitis, gout and osteoarthritis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **acromesomelic dysplasia, Hunter-Thompson type**, a rare genetic skeletal disorder, but **no clinical trials and no publications** currently support this prediction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, azapropazone is an NSAID, its use in inflammatory and degenerative joint disease is established, and it would be expected to act by reducing prostaglandin-mediated inflammation and pain.

On review, there is **no credible mechanistic link** to the predicted condition. Hunter-Thompson type acromesomelic dysplasia is a genetic skeletal disorder caused by loss of function in *CDMP1/GDF5*. It is a developmental bone-growth defect, not an inflammatory disease, and an NSAID would not address the underlying defect.

The high TxGNN score most likely reflects a knowledge-graph artefact, driven by shared musculoskeletal phenotype associations rather than a shared disease mechanism. At best, an NSAID might offer symptomatic pain relief for secondary joint problems, which would not be disease-modifying.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction is computational only (L5), with no trials, no literature and no plausible mechanistic link between an NSAID and a genetic skeletal dysplasia. The high score is probably an artefact of shared phenotype associations.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A credible mechanistic hypothesis linking azapropazone to *GDF5*-related bone development, supported by preclinical data
- Mechanism of action data for azapropazone
- Safety information from the SmPC and BNF, including warnings, contraindications and drug interactions
- Confirmation of the licensed indication text and current supply status

**Note on other predictions:** Among the other predicted indications, **inflammatory spondylopathy** (rank 10, L3) has some published evidence. This includes controlled comparisons against indomethacin in ankylosing spondylitis from 1974 and 1980. It is largely a relabelling of an existing indication, and the evidence is dated. If further evaluation is warranted, it would be a more productive research question than the top-ranked prediction. Azapropazone's safety profile, including interaction concerns, should be re-evaluated against current alternatives such as other NSAIDs and TNF or IL-17 inhibitors.
