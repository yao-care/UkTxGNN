Note: the page language is English, so the main prediction is written once, as "bronchitis".

## One-Sentence Summary

Omalizumab is an anti-IgE monoclonal antibody that binds free IgE and is used in allergic airway and skin disease.
Only one of these trials, in persistent eosinophilic bronchitis, bears directly on the disease, giving an evidence level of **L2**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Omalizumab binds free IgE and lowers FcεRI-mediated activation of mast cells and basophils. This reduces allergic airway inflammation. Detailed mechanism-of-action data were not available in the data compiled for this report, but the IgE-binding action is well characterised.

Eosinophilic bronchitis is a form of bronchitis with eosinophilic airway inflammation. A randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase 2/3 trial examined whether omalizumab allows prednisone dose reduction in patients with asthma and persistent eosinophilic bronchitis. This gives a plausible mechanistic route for the prediction.

The rationale is limited, however. Most supporting literature concerns allergic asthma and asthma-COPD overlap rather than bronchitis itself. Omalizumab has no established mechanistic role in non-allergic, infectious or smoking-related chronic bronchitis. The prediction should be read as applying to an IgE/eosinophil-driven subgroup at most.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions** (8 recorded): the one Moderate-level interaction is with omacetaxine mepesuccinate. Minor-level interactions are recorded with zinc sulfate, zinc acetate, zinc gluconate, zinc chloride, echinacea and vitamin E. A further entry reflects the pharmacological target, immunoglobulin heavy constant epsilon (IgE).

Please refer to the SmPC and BNF for warnings and contraindications. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
A completed randomised, placebo-controlled Phase 2/3 trial in persistent eosinophilic bronchitis supports an evidence level of L2, but it enrolled only 11 patients and the remaining evidence is indirect, mostly from asthma. The benefit is plausible only in an IgE- or eosinophil-driven subgroup, so further work should stay within a defined research setting.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Published results and effect sizes from NCT02049294, and a larger adequately powered trial in eosinophilic bronchitis
- Clear definition of the target bronchitis phenotype (allergic or eosinophilic), excluding infectious and smoking-related forms
- Review of the SmPC warnings and contraindications, which were not available in the data compiled for this report
- Confirmation of the approved indication and dosage form for the UK authorisation
