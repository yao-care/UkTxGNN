## Omalizumab: From Asthma to Bronchitis
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Omalizumab is an anti-IgE monoclonal antibody, originally used for allergic asthma and chronic idiopathic urticaria.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **bronchitis** (top-ranked prediction), but this link is indirect: **2 clinical trials** and **7 publications** are listed, and none shows efficacy in bronchitis itself. The evidence is stronger for related conditions such as atopic eczema and obstructive lung disease.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data was not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, omalizumab is a humanised monoclonal antibody that binds free IgE. This lowers FcεRI-mediated activation of mast cells and basophils and may reduce allergic airway inflammation.

Its efficacy in allergic asthma is established, and bronchitis is closely related to asthma in the airway disease network. The bronchitis label probably reflects the overlap between asthma and chronic bronchitis. The available evidence comes mostly from allergic asthma and asthma-COPD overlap, not from bronchitis. Benefit in non-allergic, infectious or smoking-related chronic bronchitis has no established mechanism.

**Other predictions in the ranking:**
- **Obstructive lung disease:** Strongest evidence base (several completed Phase 3 trials), but almost entirely in allergic asthma, an approved indication. For COPD, benefit is plausible only in allergen-sensitised, high-IgE patients. The Phase 2 COPD-OMA trial is still recruiting and an earlier COPD study was withdrawn with no participants.
- **Atopic eczema and dermatitis:** Several small trials, including a Phase 4 study in children. Results are mixed, and agents targeting IL-4/IL-13 (such as dupilumab) are better supported. Omalizumab may help only in selected high-IgE patients. The generic "dermatitis" term does not support a wider claim.
- **Bronchial neoplasm, acne keloid, acrodermatitis chronica atrophicans, familial hydroa vacciniforme, neonatal dermatomyositis, and childhood connective-tissue-disease-associated interstitial lung disease:** No supported mechanism and no meaningful clinical evidence; these are computational predictions only and should be held.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The high TxGNN score for bronchitis is not supported by direct evidence. The only related trial is a very small asthma study (n=11), and the literature concerns asthma and asthma-COPD overlap, which is already covered by the approved indication. Of the other predictions, obstructive lung disease (the COPD component) and atopic eczema warrant further research, but with no confirmatory trial data they remain research questions, and the remaining predictions have no supporting evidence.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism-of-action data and a defined bronchitis phenotype (for example, allergic or eosinophilic) that could plausibly respond to anti-IgE therapy
- Results from the COPD-OMA trial (NCT07059091) before considering any obstructive lung disease repurposing
- Controlled trials in high-IgE atopic eczema subgroups, compared against IL-4/IL-13-targeted therapies
- Full UK licence details (approved indications, dosage forms) and SmPC warnings and contraindications to complete the safety review
