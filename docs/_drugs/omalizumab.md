# Omalizumab: From Severe Allergic Asthma to Bronchitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Omalizumab is a recombinant humanised anti-IgE monoclonal antibody, most widely used to treat moderate-to-severe persistent allergic asthma and chronic spontaneous urticaria. The TxGNN model predicts it may also be effective for **bronchitis** (specifically eosinophilic bronchitis), with **2 clinical trials** and **8 publications** currently identified in support of this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in this evidence pack (flagged as data gap DG002). Based on well-established pharmacological knowledge, omalizumab is a humanised monoclonal antibody that binds the constant (Cε3) region of circulating IgE, preventing it from binding to the high-affinity FcεRI receptor on mast cells and basophils. This reduces free serum IgE, downregulates FcεRI expression, and dampens the downstream allergic inflammatory cascade — effects that translate clinically into reduced airway inflammation.

Allergic asthma and eosinophilic bronchitis share substantial pathophysiological overlap: both are Type-2/eosinophil-driven airway inflammatory conditions, often distinguished mainly by the absence of airflow obstruction/hyperresponsiveness in eosinophilic bronchitis. Because omalizumab's mechanism targets the IgE-mediated inflammatory pathway rather than airway smooth muscle or obstruction per se, it is mechanistically plausible that it could benefit patients with eosinophilic bronchitis who share the same underlying immunological driver.

This is further supported by literature identified in the evidence pack — including reviews on omalizumab's post-approval experience and its relevance to smoking-induced and COPD-overlap airway disease — which reflect ongoing clinical interest in extending anti-IgE therapy beyond classic allergic asthma into related airway inflammatory phenotypes.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Evidence for the bronchitis indication currently rests on one small completed Phase 2/3 trial (n=11) in eosinophilic bronchitis plus supportive but largely indirect literature. More importantly, essential safety data (SmPC-level warnings and contraindications) required for the S1 safety screen is a **blocking** data gap, so this candidate cannot yet proceed to formal safety evaluation.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- SmPC-level warnings and contraindications for omalizumab (DG001, blocking — required before S1 safety review)
- Confirmed mechanism of action documentation from DrugBank/SmPC (DG002)
- UK marketing authorisation and licensing records for omalizumab
- Larger, adequately powered controlled trials specifically in eosinophilic/allergic bronchitis populations, beyond the single n=11 pilot study
