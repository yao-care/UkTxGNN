## Omalizumab: Bronchitis
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Omalizumab is a humanised monoclonal antibody that binds immunoglobulin E (IgE). However, none of the **2 registered trials** and none of the **publications** retrieved studies omalizumab in bronchitis itself, so the evidence level is L5.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Omalizumab targets the constant region of the IgE heavy chain (gene *IGHE*). It lowers free IgE and reduces FcεRI-mediated activation of mast cells and basophils. This is a plausible way to dampen allergic airway inflammation.

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available beyond this target information. The supporting literature concerns allergic airway disease in people who also have chronic bronchitis features, not bronchitis as a stand-alone condition. The mechanism is not established for infectious, non-allergic or smoking-related chronic bronchitis. The high score probably reflects proximity to allergic airway disease rather than direct evidence in bronchitis.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
- No published study supports omalizumab in bronchitis. The one closely related trial was tiny, enrolled a severe-asthma-type population and suggested possible inadequacy. The remaining items are reviews or unrelated studies, so the evidence level is L5 despite the high model score.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A clear definition of the target bronchitis phenotype (for example eosinophilic or allergic), since a mechanism is plausible at best for IgE-driven disease.
- Controlled studies of omalizumab in that phenotype, with adequate sample size.
- Complete mechanism-of-action data.
- Safety information from the SmPC, including label warnings and contraindications.
