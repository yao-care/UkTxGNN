# Diethylstilbestrol: From Unspecified Original Indication to Amenorrhoea

## One-Sentence Summary

Diethylstilbestrol (DES) is a synthetic non-steroidal oestrogen, and no approved indication text is available for its UK licence in the data compiled for this report.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **amenorrhoea**, but there are **no registered clinical trials** and only **14 publications**, mostly historical reviews and case reports.
The prediction is essentially model-derived, and the literature raises significant safety concerns.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not currently available. Based on known information, diethylstilbestrol is a synthetic non-steroidal oestrogen. Oestrogen replacement is a biologically plausible approach to hypo-oestrogenic amenorrhoea, such as primary ovarian failure, premature ovarian insufficiency, and gonadal dysgenesis (Turner syndrome).

The retrieved literature is historical and descriptive. It includes reviews on ovarian failure and case reports of endometrial carcinoma after oestrogen therapy in gonadal dysgenesis and Turner syndrome. These point to a significant safety signal for unopposed oestrogen.

The high TxGNN score most likely reflects the drug's oestrogenic class effect and is not clinical evidence of efficacy. DES also has well-documented harms, including clear-cell adenocarcinoma and reproductive tract abnormalities after in utero exposure. Safer oestradiol-based options are available.

## Safety Considerations

- **Disease-level cautions (from a knowledge graph, not label text):** The TxGNN knowledge graph links DES to contraindication with many conditions, including hypertension, congestive heart failure, thrombophlebitis, liver disease, gallbladder disease, kidney disease, epilepsy, asthma, diabetes mellitus, diabetic ketoacidosis, hypothyroidism, systemic lupus erythematosus, hypertriglyceridaemia, hypercalcaemia, benign prostatic hyperplasia, uterine leiomyoma and female breast carcinoma.
- **Literature safety signals:** Endometrial carcinoma with unopposed oestrogen in Turner syndrome; clear-cell adenocarcinoma and reproductive tract abnormalities after in utero exposure; increased breast cancer risk in exposed women.
- **Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions were identified (DDInter). Most listed are Moderate, and one is Minor (cimetidine). Main examples:
  - **Antidiabetic agents** (may reduce glycaemic control): acarbose, metformin, chlorpropamide, alogliptin, saxagliptin, albiglutide, dulaglutide, canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, empagliflozin
  - **Corticosteroids:** hydrocortisone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, triamcinolone, budesonide
  - **Others:** aprepitant, clarithromycin, calcitriol (topical), chenodeoxycholic acid

Please refer to the SmPC and BNF for authoritative warnings and contraindications. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests on a plausible oestrogen-replacement mechanism but is supported only by historical reviews and case reports, with no clinical trials. The available literature highlights serious risks (endometrial carcinoma, in utero harms), and safer oestradiol-based alternatives exist.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Approved indication text, dosage form and manufacturer details for the UK licence
- Mechanism of action data and SmPC warnings and contraindications
- Comparative evidence against established oestradiol-based therapies in hypo-oestrogenic amenorrhoea
- A clear risk-benefit case addressing carcinogenicity and thromboembolic risk, including any need for progestogen opposition
