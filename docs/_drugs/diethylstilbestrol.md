## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available for this candidate. Based on known pharmacology, diethylstilbestrol is a synthetic non-steroidal oestrogen; oestrogen replacement can, in principle, induce endometrial proliferation and withdrawal bleeding, which offers a plausible physiological rationale for use in hypo-oestrogenic amenorrhoea (e.g. premature ovarian failure).

However, the literature retrieved for this candidate is overwhelmingly composed of retrospective reviews and case reports describing the *consequences* of oestrogen exposure — most notably endometrial carcinoma following long-term cyclical oestrogen-progestogen therapy, and premature ovarian failure/amenorrhoea arising as a *complication* of in-utero DES exposure — rather than prospective evidence of DES being used to treat amenorrhoea. In other words, the mechanistic direction is plausible, but the retrieved evidence base points more strongly to a safety signal than to a therapeutic effect.

No clinical trials (registered or ICTRP) were identified for this drug-disease pair, and no original indication or MOA data could be confirmed from the source registries used to build this evidence pack.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
- The mechanistic rationale for oestrogen replacement in hypo-oestrogenic amenorrhoea is plausible, but the retrieved evidence base (L4, no clinical trials, predominantly older reviews and case reports) predominantly documents oestrogen-related harms — notably endometrial carcinoma — rather than therapeutic benefit, and safety/warning data needed for a formal S1 evaluation is currently missing (blocking data gap).

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- SmPC-equivalent warnings and contraindications (currently a blocking data gap for safety evaluation)

- Confirmation of the drug's original approved indication(s), which are not currently recorded
