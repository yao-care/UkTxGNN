# Rifampicin: From Tuberculosis to Conjunctivitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Rifampicin is a rifamycin-class antibiotic originally developed as a first-line treatment for tuberculosis and other mycobacterial infections. The TxGNN model predicts it may also be effective for **Conjunctivitis**, but this is currently supported only by **20 publications** — largely historical case reports, susceptibility studies and one 1970s controlled trial — with **no registered clinical trials**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in this evidence pack (flagged as a High-severity data gap). Based on established pharmacology, rifampicin is a rifamycin that inhibits bacterial DNA-dependent RNA polymerase, giving it a broad antibacterial spectrum extending beyond *Mycobacterium* species to include organisms such as *Chlamydia trachomatis* and *Neisseria meningitidis*.

Tuberculosis and conjunctivitis are not anatomically related, but the TxGNN link appears to be pathogen-driven rather than organ-driven: several of the organisms rifampicin acts against — notably *C. trachomatis* (the cause of trachoma, a severe conjunctival infection) and *N. meningitidis* — are also recognised causes of conjunctivitis.

This mechanistic plausibility is reinforced by historical clinical use: topical rifampicin was trialled for endemic trachoma in the 1970s with results comparable to tetracycline, and systemic rifampicin has been used as adjunctive/prophylactic therapy in cases of primary meningococcal conjunctivitis. However, this body of evidence predates modern trial registries and has not been revisited with contemporary clinical trial methodology.

- Verification of current UK marketing/licensing status for rifampicin-containing products
- A contemporary clinical evaluation (the only comparative trial data for conjunctivitis/trachoma is >45 years old)
