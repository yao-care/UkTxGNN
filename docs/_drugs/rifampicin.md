## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not currently available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, rifampicin is a broad-spectrum antibiotic that inhibits bacterial RNA polymerase. Its efficacy in tuberculosis is well established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to bacterial eye infections.

Rifampicin is active against Gram-positive bacteria and *Chlamydia trachomatis*, which are recognised causes of infectious conjunctivitis and trachoma. This gives a plausible antibacterial rationale. Older studies tested rifampicin eye ointment in chlamydial eye infection and trachoma.

However, rifampicin is not a standard conjunctivitis therapy, and well-established alternatives exist. The evidence is largely indirect (susceptibility surveys and case reports), so the prediction should be regarded as hypothesis-generating.

Other predictions in the model output were considered. Hidradenitis suppurativa/acne (rifampicin plus clindamycin is a guideline-listed option), rheumatoid arthritis (small, older studies with modest results) and HIV (a co-infection and drug interaction signal rather than an anti-HIV effect) have some literature. Several others (multiple endocrine neoplasia, a rare neurodevelopmental disorder, and the feline and simian immunodeficiency entries) are computational predictions only, with no plausible mechanistic link.

## Safety Considerations

**Contraindications and risk conditions (from knowledge graph disease associations, not label text):** The knowledge graph links rifampicin with the following conditions as contraindications or cautions. Examples include:
- Liver disease, liver failure, acute liver failure and hepatitis
- Porphyria (including inherited porphyria)
- Blood disorders: thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, agranulocytosis, anaemia, pancytopenia, aplastic anaemia
- Diabetes mellitus and diabetic ketoacidosis
- Gout
- Peripheral neuropathy
- Gallbladder disease
- Substance abuse/dependence
- Slow acetylator status

**Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions were identified; those listed below are moderate-level. Rifampicin is a strong enzyme inducer (CYP3A4 and P-gp), so many co-medications may have reduced exposure. Examples include:
- Corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, triamcinolone)
- Antidiabetics (metformin, pioglitazone, canagliflozin, chlorpropamide)
- Antibiotics (doxycycline, clarithromycin, metronidazole)
- Proton pump inhibitors (omeprazole, rabeprazole)
- Others: bupropion, aprepitant, morphine, vitamin D analogues (cholecalciferol, calcifediol, calcitriol), picosulfuric acid

Other safety points: ocular toxicity has been reported with rifampicin (PMIDs 1247775 and 1260294). Please refer to the SmPC and BNF for full safety information, and report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is high, but the supporting evidence is indirect (susceptibility surveys, case reports and two older topical studies in chlamydial/trachoma infection), there are no registered trials, and established conjunctivitis treatments exist. Rifampicin's enzyme induction and safety profile add further concerns.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data to support the mechanistic-link analysis
- Label-based warnings and contraindications from the SmPC and BNF (currently missing, which blocks safety screening)
- Confirmation of whether any ophthalmic rifampicin formulation is available or feasible in the UK
- Modern, controlled clinical evidence in a defined subtype (for example chlamydial conjunctivitis), compared with current UK standard-of-care antibiotics
- A review of antimicrobial stewardship implications, given that rifampicin is a key antituberculosis agent and resistance is a concern
