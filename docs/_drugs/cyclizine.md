## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, cyclizine is a first-generation H1-antihistamine. Urticaria is a histamine-mediated condition, so blocking H1 receptors is biologically plausible as a way to reduce itch and wheals.

The proposed use sits close to the allergic conditions for which antihistamines are generally used. However, the support comes only from the TxGNN prediction. In clinical practice, non-sedating second-generation antihistamines are generally preferred for urticaria, and no cyclizine-specific data were found, so the prediction remains a hypothesis.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction has a high model score (0.9998), and H1 blockade is mechanistically plausible for histamine-mediated urticaria. However, no trials or publications address allergic urticaria directly, so the evidence level is L5 and the case rests on model output alone.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Clinical or observational data on cyclizine in allergic urticaria, including comparison with second-generation antihistamines
- Mechanism of action data
- Label-based warnings and contraindications from the SmPC and BNF
- Verification of the MHRA authorisation number, dosage form and approved indication text
- A review of interaction risks, especially additive anticholinergic and sedative effects
