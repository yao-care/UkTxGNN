## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. Hyaluronidase is known to degrade hyaluronan, a major component of the extracellular matrix and the vitreous gel.

In the eye, this is the basis of "pharmacologic vitreolysis". The enzyme liquefies the vitreous and promotes posterior vitreous detachment. This may improve diffusion of oxygen and nutrients and help clear vitreous haemorrhage. It may also reduce vitreous-driven VEGF/HIF-1α signalling, which contributes to diabetic retinopathy progression. Animal studies support this (diabetic rats, enzyme-induced vitreolysis acting through the HIF-1α pathway, and retinal endothelial glycocalyx work in mice). A 2026 study of dysregulated hyaluronan metabolism in proliferative diabetic retinopathy has renewed mechanistic interest.

Important caveats:
- The Phase 3 trials studied **vitreous haemorrhage**, not diabetic retinopathy as such.
- Ovine hyaluronidase (Vitrase) did not reach FDA approval for this use.
- Neither the original indication nor its relationship to the new one could be assessed, because no approved indication text was available.

Other predictions are weak. Esotropia, amenorrhoea, renal tubular acidosis, dermatitis, acrodermatitis chronica atrophicans, diabetic cataract, neonatal dermatomyositis and non-syndromic oesophageal malformation have no trials and no supporting mechanism, or only indirect literature. The esotropia literature describes strabismus as a complication of local anaesthesia, not a treatment target.

## Safety Considerations

- **Allergic reactions:** A 2024 review of hyaluronidase injection describes allergic complications and notes that hyaluronidase allergy is frequently misdiagnosed. Risk factors and management are covered there.
- **Ocular context:** A 1978 double-blind study reported cystoid macular oedema in 25% of aphakic patients receiving hyaluronidase mixed with local anaesthetic, versus none in the control group. This is historical, but relevant to any ocular use.
- **Drug interactions:** 63 interactions were recorded, all of those listed being minor. Examples include corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, dexamethasone, prednisolone, triamcinolone, betamethasone, budesonide), aspirin, antihistamines (promethazine, chlorphenamine, cetirizine, loratadine), oestrogens (oestradiol, ethinylestradiol, conjugated oestrogens) and local anaesthetics (lidocaine, articaine).

Formal warnings and contraindications were not available. Please refer to the SmPC and BNF for full safety information. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold (research question only)**

**Rationale:**
Diabetic retinopathy is the only prediction with substantial evidence: two completed Phase 3 trials and supportive preclinical work. However, those trials addressed vitreous haemorrhage, results are not available here, the product did not gain FDA approval for this use, and the Phase 2 diabetic retinopathy study was very small and uncontrolled. All other predictions lack clinical support and should not be pursued on the model score alone.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Published results and regulatory outcome of NCT00198497 and NCT00198510
- Approved indication text and SmPC safety information (warnings, contraindications) for the UK product
- Mechanism of action data
- Assessment of route compatibility, since intravitreal use differs from the existing injection licence
- Clarification of whether the evidence applies to diabetic retinopathy itself or only to vitreous haemorrhage
