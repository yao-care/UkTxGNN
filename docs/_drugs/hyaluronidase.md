## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available for hyaluronidase in this evidence pack, and no original indication or UK licence could be identified to anchor a mechanistic comparison.

The sole supporting publication (PMID 16934027) describes persistent diplopia and strabismus as a **complication** of retrobulbar local anaesthesia during cataract surgery — hyaluronidase is a common additive in such anaesthetic mixtures. This is a case-report description of an adverse ocular motility outcome associated with the drug's use, not evidence of a therapeutic effect on esotropia.

As a result, the TxGNN score for this candidate more plausibly reflects a **co-occurrence between hyaluronidase exposure and strabismus/diplopia as an adverse event** rather than a genuine repurposing opportunity. The mechanistic direction is, if anything, a risk signal rather than a treatment rationale, and should not be interpreted as supportive evidence without further scrutiny.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The top-ranked prediction (esotropia) is supported by a single case report describing an adverse surgical complication rather than a therapeutic effect, with no clinical trials and no mechanism-of-action data available to build a repurposing rationale. Evidence is insufficient to proceed.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Confirmed original indication(s) and mechanism of action for hyaluronidase (currently both are data gaps)

**Note on other candidates in this evidence pack:** among the 10 TxGNN-ranked predictions for this drug, **diabetic retinopathy** (rank 6) has materially stronger evidence — two completed Phase 3 trials (NCT00198510, n=750; NCT00198497, n=510) of intravitreal hyaluronidase (Vitrase) for vitreous haemorrhage clearance, plus a supporting Phase 2 study and 20 literature references — and may warrant separate evaluation.
