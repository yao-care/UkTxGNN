# Duloxetine: From Depression and Anxiety Disorders to Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Duloxetine is a serotonin-norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor (SNRI) established in clinical practice for major depressive disorder, generalised anxiety disorder, diabetic peripheral neuropathic pain and fibromyalgia. The TxGNN model's top-scoring candidates span ten different diseases, but only two carry meaningful supporting evidence: **Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder** (99.84% score, one completed Phase 4 trial) and **Agoraphobia/panic disorder spectrum** (99.84% score, three supporting publications). The remaining eight candidates (including the single highest-scoring hit, benign paroxysmal torticollis of infancy) have no clinical trials or literature support and are best treated as unvalidated model signal.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data for this candidate record is not available. Based on well-established public pharmacological knowledge, duloxetine is a serotonin-norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor (SNRI), and its efficacy in major depressive disorder and generalised anxiety disorder is well proven. Serotonergic dysregulation is central to the pathophysiology of obsessive-compulsive disorder, and selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs) are the established first-line pharmacological treatment for OCD.

Since duloxetine shares the serotonergic reuptake-inhibition mechanism with SSRIs, and OCD frequently co-occurs with, and shares neurobiological overlap with, anxiety and mood disorders that duloxetine already treats, a mechanistic rationale for extending its use to OCD is plausible. This is supported by an existing, if small, dedicated clinical trial (below) rather than purely theoretical reasoning.

The same serotonergic/noradrenergic mechanism also underlies the report's secondary candidate, agoraphobia (part of the panic disorder spectrum), for which duloxetine has direct open-label clinical evidence — see the supplementary section below.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Only one small (n=20), non-comparative Phase 4 trial directly supports duloxetine in OCD, and no literature confirms it; agoraphobia is similarly supported by only an open-label study and a case report. Neither reaches the L1/L2 threshold needed to proceed, and this candidate has a **Blocking** data gap on UK/manufacturer safety labelling (warnings, contraindications) that must be resolved before any S1 safety evaluation.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- MHRA product labelling (SmPC) — key warnings, contraindications and drug interactions (currently a Blocking data gap)
- Confirmed mechanism-of-action documentation from DrugBank or an equivalent source
- A larger, comparator-controlled trial of duloxetine in OCD to replace the existing 20-patient pilot
- Dedicated agoraphobia-specific trial data, since current literature addresses panic disorder more broadly
- UK marketing authorisation and market-status confirmation, as none is currently on file for this candidate
