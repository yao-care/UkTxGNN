## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Capecitabine is an oral prodrug of 5-fluorouracil (5-FU). It is converted to 5-FU by thymidine phosphorylase, an enzyme enriched in tumour tissue. 5-FU inhibits thymidylate synthase and disrupts DNA synthesis in rapidly dividing adenocarcinoma cells. Detailed mechanism-of-action data were not available in the drug record compiled for this report; the description above comes from the repurposing rationale for this prediction.

Gastric tubular adenocarcinoma is a common histological pattern of gastric adenocarcinoma, the same disease family as the original gastric cancer indication. The mechanism is therefore expected to apply directly. Fluoropyrimidine-based regimens, including capecitabine with oxaliplatin or cisplatin, are widely used in gastric and gastro-oesophageal junction adenocarcinoma, as the trials below illustrate.

This prediction is largely a histological refinement of an existing indication rather than a wholly new disease area. The publications are mostly in gastric or gastro-oesophageal junction adenocarcinoma generally, not specifically the tubular subtype.

## Safety Considerations

No label-based warnings or contraindications were available in the data compiled for this report. The knowledge graph does link capecitabine to the following conditions as contraindications (these are graph associations, not label text, and should be checked against the SmPC):

- **Haematological:** neutropenia, agranulocytosis, thrombocytopenia, anaemia, macrocytic anaemia
- **Hepatic and biliary:** liver disease, gallbladder disease, hyperbilirubinaemia, hereditary hyperbilirubinaemia, Gilbert syndrome
- **Renal:** impaired renal function
- **Cardiac:** coronary artery disease, sudden cardiac arrest, heart conduction disease
- **Other:** infectious disease, stomatitis, necrotizing enterocolitis
- **Genetic:** dihydropyrimidine dehydrogenase (DPD) deficiency, a key fluoropyrimidine toxicity risk, for which pre-treatment testing is standard UK practice

**Drug interactions:** 100 interactions were recorded, and the first 20 were available for review. The main ones with a stated severity are:
- **Moderate:** metronidazole, tinidazole
- **Minor:** aluminium hydroxide, magaldrate, magnesium carbonate/hydroxide/oxide, levofloxacin
- **Severity not classified:** pantoprazole, omeprazole, lansoprazole, glimepiride, metformin, doxycycline, morphine, atropine, prednisone, hydrocortisone, simvastatin, dolasetron

The well-recognised warfarin and phenytoin interactions, and the contraindication with other fluoropyrimidines, were not in the reviewed portion. Please refer to the SmPC and BNF. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Several Phase 3 RCTs (notably CLASSIC and RESOLVE) support capecitabine-based chemotherapy in gastric adenocarcinoma, the disease family that includes the tubular subtype, and the mechanism is the same as in the original indication. The evidence is not specific to tubular histology, and no trial is registered for this subtype. The L1 grade reflects the gastric adenocarcinoma evidence base, not tubular-specific data.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Confirmation of the authorised UK indications from the current MHRA record and SmPC. The licence entry in the data has no indication text, and the original indication is taken from the knowledge graph.
- Confirmation that the evidence applies to the tubular subtype specifically, for example through subgroup analyses of the RCTs above.
- A safety review against the SmPC, including DPD deficiency testing, renal and hepatic function checks, and a full interaction review.
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank.
- A note on other predictions: signet ring cell, cardia and gastric body carcinoma have supporting data, whereas gastric adenocarcinoma with proximal polyposis, microinvasive gastric cancer, gastric pylorus carcinoma, salivary gland-type stomach carcinoma and malignant gastric granular cell tumour have little or none and are held.
