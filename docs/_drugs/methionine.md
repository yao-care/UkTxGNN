## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available for methionine in this evidence pack. Based on known pharmacology, methionine is an essential amino acid and precursor in the homocysteine/S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) and transsulfuration pathway, which also feeds into glutathione (GSH) biosynthesis. No confirmed original indication or UK licence exists for this compound, which limits the extent to which mechanistic continuity with the predicted new use can be assessed.

For the top-ranked prediction, acne, the supporting literature does not describe methionine as a treatment. Instead, it shows that isotretinoin therapy for cystic acne is associated with a **rise** in plasma homocysteine as a side effect, and two older papers describe neutrophil function in inflammatory skin conditions without any direct link to methionine supplementation. This means the mechanistic direction is unclear and may even run counter to a therapeutic rationale — elevated homocysteine is generally regarded as an adverse metabolic effect, not a marker of efficacy.

Overall, the link between methionine's known biology and acne is mechanistically plausible only in the loosest sense (shared metabolic pathway), but the available evidence does not demonstrate — and in one instance arguably contradicts — a therapeutic effect. This should be treated as a hypothesis-generating signal only.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The evidence for methionine in acne consists solely of preclinical/observational and case-level literature (L4) with no clinical trials, and part of the available evidence points toward homocysteine elevation as an adverse effect rather than a therapeutic mechanism. Combined with the absence of any UK marketing authorisation and missing regulatory safety documentation, the evidence base does not currently support progression.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- TFDA/MHRA-equivalent product labelling (warnings and contraindications) — currently a blocking data gap
- Confirmed mechanism of action and an established original indication for methionine, to properly assess mechanistic continuity
- Targeted, controlled studies testing methionine (not homocysteine as a biomarker of another drug's toxicity) as an intervention in acne
- Clarification of the direction of the homocysteine/methionine relationship in dermatological conditions before further mechanistic claims are made
