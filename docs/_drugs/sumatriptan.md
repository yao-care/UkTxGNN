## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data for Sumatriptan is not available in the structured Evidence Pack. Based on established pharmacological knowledge, Sumatriptan is a selective 5-HT1B/1D receptor agonist ("triptan"), long used for acute migraine and cluster headache treatment. It works through cranial vasoconstriction and inhibition of inflammatory neuropeptide release from the trigeminovascular system.

Migraine with brainstem aura (formerly known as basilar-type migraine) is a rare migraine subtype in which aura symptoms arise from transient dysfunction of the posterior (vertebrobasilar) circulation, rather than the cortical mechanisms implicated in typical migraine. Although this subtype falls within the same broad "migraine" disease family that Sumatriptan already treats, its distinct vascular territory means the same vasoconstrictive mechanism that makes triptans effective in common migraine carries a theoretical risk of provoking brainstem or posterior-circulation ischaemia in this specific subtype.

For this reason, the International Headache Society (ICHD) and most clinical guidelines classify migraine with brainstem aura as a **relative or absolute contraindication** for triptan-class drugs, rather than a candidate for indication expansion. The TxGNN model has likely generated this prediction based on surface-level disease-family similarity (both are migraine subtypes) without capturing this safety-critical distinction. This prediction should therefore be interpreted as a **safety signal flag**, not a genuine repurposing opportunity.

## Conclusion and Next Steps
