## Anagrelide: From Thrombocythaemia to Reactive Thrombocytosis
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Anagrelide is a platelet-lowering drug, originally used to treat thrombocythaemia (including familial thrombocytosis).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **reactive thrombocytosis**, but there are **no registered clinical trials** and only **10 publications**, none of which directly tests anagrelide in this condition.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. The literature describes anagrelide as lowering platelet counts by interfering with megakaryocyte maturation, with PDE3 inhibition contributing to its antiplatelet and cardiovascular effects. This is plausibly relevant to any condition with raised platelet counts.

The link to the new indication is weak, however. Reactive (secondary) thrombocytosis is driven by an underlying cause such as inflammation, infection, iron deficiency, malignancy or splenectomy. It is usually managed by treating that cause, and thrombotic risk is typically low unless other risk factors are present. The reviews in the data state that reactive thrombocytosis often needs no therapy, whereas clonal thrombocytosis may need cytoreduction. The very high TxGNN score most likely reflects the semantic closeness to the approved thrombocythaemia indications rather than independent evidence.

## Clinical Trial Evidence

## Cytotoxicity

Anagrelide is a platelet-lowering agent used in myeloproliferative disorders. It is not a conventional cytotoxic chemotherapy agent, so no cytotoxicity classification is given here. Please refer to the SmPC for monitoring and handling requirements.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
No clinical trials exist for anagrelide in reactive thrombocytosis, and the literature does not directly support its use. Reactive thrombocytosis is normally managed by treating the underlying cause, with low thrombotic risk and little rationale for cytoreduction. The high TxGNN score reflects proximity to the approved indications rather than independent evidence. The knowledge graph also flags many cardiovascular and organ-function contraindications.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data (for example from DrugBank)
- SmPC and BNF warnings and contraindications, to complete the safety screening
- Clinical evidence in reactive thrombocytosis, or a defined high-risk subgroup (for example, post-splenectomy with thrombotic risk), showing a benefit over treating the underlying cause
- A cardiovascular risk assessment given the interaction and contraindication signals
- Verification of the UK authorisation record, including the licence number, dosage form and approved indication

*Note: The second predicted indication, inverse Klippel-Trenaunay syndrome (TxGNN score 0.9959), has no supporting trials or literature and no plausible mechanistic link (evidence level L5, Hold). It is not evaluated further.*
