## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in this evidence pack (data gap). Based on known pharmacology, prednisone is a systemic glucocorticoid that suppresses T-cell-mediated immune activity — the central pathological mechanism believed to drive follicular autoimmune attack in alopecia areata (AA). Corticosteroids of this class have long been used empirically for autoimmune and inflammatory conditions generally, and this immunosuppressive action provides a direct, biologically plausible rationale for use in AA.

Clinically, systemic corticosteroids (including prednisone) are already an established adjunct or rescue option for moderate-to-severe AA in real-world dermatology practice, predating this prediction. This is reflected in a substantial body of literature spanning from the 1950s to 2024, including a 2023 double-blind randomised trial combining low-dose prednisone with methotrexate for the most severe AA subtypes (alopecia totalis/universalis).

However, the strongest RCT evidence available tests prednisone as a **low-dose adjunct to methotrexate**, not as monotherapy, and most retrieved clinical trials (predominantly in systemic lupus erythematosus, lymphoma and prostate cancer) reflect prednisone's ubiquitous use as background standard-of-care rather than a direct test of efficacy in AA. This combination of a genuine but narrow evidence base is why the evidence level is rated L3 rather than higher.

## UK Market Information

No UK marketing authorisation currently exists for prednisone within this evidence pack (0 licences on record; market status: Not Marketed).

For context, prednisone requires hepatic conversion to its active metabolite, prednisolone. In UK clinical practice, prednisolone (rather than prednisone) is the corticosteroid routinely marketed and prescribed for equivalent indications — this may explain the absence of a UK licence for prednisone itself.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
There is a genuine, mechanistically coherent, and long-standing body of clinical evidence (including one completed Phase 3 RCT and multiple decades of cohort experience) supporting glucocorticoid use in moderate-to-severe alopecia areata, but the strongest trial evidence tests prednisone only as a low-dose adjunct to methotrexate rather than as monotherapy, and formal safety/regulatory data are currently absent.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- TFDA/UK SmPC-equivalent warnings, contraindications and drug interaction data (Blocking gap — required before any S1 safety assessment)
- Detailed mechanism of action characterisation (High-priority gap)
- Clarification of the UK regulatory/access pathway, given prednisone itself holds no UK marketing authorisation (unlicensed use or prednisolone-based equivalence would need to be considered)
- A dosing/regimen review distinguishing monotherapy use from the combination (prednisone + methotrexate) regimen actually tested in the pivotal RCT
