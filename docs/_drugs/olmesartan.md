## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data for olmesartan is not available in this evidence pack (a flagged high-severity data gap). Based on known pharmacology, olmesartan is an angiotensin II type-1 (AT1) receptor antagonist that blocks the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS), an effect it shares with the rest of the ARB class. Its established efficacy in hypertension stems from this RAAS blockade.

The link to migraine is biologically plausible: RAAS activity also contributes to cerebrovascular tone regulation and neuro-inflammation, mechanisms implicated in migraine pathophysiology. Notably, another ARB in the same class, candesartan, already has randomised controlled trial evidence supporting its use in migraine prophylaxis. This class-level precedent strengthens the case that olmesartan's high TxGNN score is not purely a knowledge-graph artefact.

It is worth noting that TxGNN's single highest-ranked prediction for this drug, Prinzmetal angina, was explicitly assessed as lacking mechanistic support (coronary vasospasm is not a RAAS-driven process) and carries no supporting evidence. Migraine disorder was therefore selected for this report as the most defensible candidate among the ten predictions reviewed, on the basis of both existing human data and class-level analogy.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold (Research Question)**

**Rationale:**
A single completed trial and supportive narrative/systematic reviews suggest ARBs, including olmesartan, may reduce migraine frequency in hypertensive patients, but no dedicated migraine-specific RCT exists and olmesartan is not currently marketed in the UK. This is a hypothesis worth formal investigation rather than a basis for clinical use at this stage.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A dedicated RCT evaluating olmesartan for migraine prophylaxis, ideally in normotensive patients, to separate the effect from blood-pressure lowering
- SmPC-level warnings, contraindications and full drug interaction data (currently a blocking data gap)
- Confirmed mechanism of action data linking RAAS blockade to neurovascular/migraine pathways beyond blood pressure effects
- UK marketing authorisation status review, should development proceed
