## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Haloperidol is generally understood to be a dopamine D2 receptor antagonist, and this underlies its use in psychosis and Tourette syndrome.

No mechanistic link has been identified between D2 antagonism and the fucosylation pathways that are defective in this rare inherited metabolic condition. The very high TxGNN score (0.9991) comes from the TxGNN model (deep learning) and may reflect similarities the model learned between related diseases, not biological or clinical evidence. On current information the prediction is not plausible enough to act on without further mechanistic work.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests on a model score alone, with no trials, no literature and no plausible mechanistic link between dopamine antagonism and defective fucosylation. Haloperidol also carries significant safety considerations, such as QT prolongation and extrapyramidal effects, so exposing patients to it without a rationale is hard to justify.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A credible mechanistic hypothesis, supported by preclinical data in glycosylation-disorder models
- Detailed mechanism of action data for haloperidol
- Review of the SmPC and BNF safety information against this rare-disease population

It has completed Phase 3 trials, including a placebo- and haloperidol-controlled olanzapine trial (NCT00129220, n=224), and network meta-analyses (PMIDs 34642461, 39191865). That use is close to haloperidol's existing psychiatric indications rather than true repurposing, and it is rated "Proceed with Guardrails". Guardrails would include monitoring for extrapyramidal symptoms, QTc prolongation and tardive dyskinesia, and reviewing interactions before co-prescribing. The remaining predictions (retinal dystrophy, myopia variants and various congenital malformation syndromes) are rated Hold, mostly with no clinical evidence.
