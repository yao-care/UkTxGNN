## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data for trifluoperazine is not available in the Evidence Pack (`original_moa: [Data Gap]`). Based on general pharmacological knowledge, trifluoperazine belongs to the phenothiazine class of typical (first-generation) antipsychotics, and its principal known action is potent antagonism of D2 dopamine receptors — a mechanism shared across the antipsychotic drug class.

The dopaminergic hypothesis of mania (PMID 970489) proposes that dopaminergic overactivity underlies manic episodes, and that D2-receptor blockade produces antimanic effects. This provides a plausible mechanistic bridge between an antipsychotic's established use in psychotic disorders and its potential utility in bipolar mania — a rationale that is reinforced by the fact that other D2-antagonist antipsychotics (e.g. haloperidol, clotiapine, risperidone) are already used clinically for acute mania, as reflected in the literature evidence below.

However, because the drug-specific MOA data and UK regulatory/safety documentation are both currently gaps (DG001, DG002), the mechanistic argument here rests on class-level pharmacology rather than confirmed trifluoperazine-specific data, which weakens translatability until these gaps are closed.
