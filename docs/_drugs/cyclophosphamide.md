## Cyclophosphamide: From Mycosis Fungoides and Other Lymphoid and Solid Cancers to Myeloid Leukaemia
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Cyclophosphamide is an alkylating chemotherapy agent, originally used for lymphomas, plasma cell myeloma, neuroblastoma, breast carcinoma and retinoblastoma.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **myeloid leukaemia**, with **33 registered clinical trials** and **20 publications** currently supporting this direction. Most of this evidence relates to its use in transplant conditioning and graft-versus-host disease (GVHD) prophylaxis, not as a stand-alone treatment.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, cyclophosphamide is an alkylating agent with cytotoxic and potent lymphodepleting and immunosuppressive activity. Its efficacy in lymphoid and other haematological malignancies is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to myeloid leukaemia.

In myeloid leukaemia, cyclophosphamide is already used in conditioning regimens before stem cell transplantation (for example busulfan–cyclophosphamide and cyclophosphamide/fludarabine). It is also used as post-transplant cyclophosphamide (PTCy) for GVHD prophylaxis in haploidentical transplantation. It selectively depletes alloreactive T cells while sparing haematopoietic stem cells, which gives a plausible and clinically exploited mechanism.

This prediction is therefore best seen as an extension to myeloid disease that is largely established in transplant practice, not a novel use. The highest-phase trial (NCT00003187) is a small Phase 2/3 transplant study (n=19), so direct evidence for cyclophosphamide on its own is limited.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Cyclophosphamide is already embedded in myeloid leukaemia transplant practice (Bu/Cy conditioning, PTCy), and multiple Phase 2 and 3 trials and cohort studies support this use. However, the evidence is for combination regimens, so cyclophosphamide's own contribution is not isolated. The Phase 2/3 trial is very small (n=19). The label safety data needed for formal screening are also missing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Product label warnings and contraindications from the current SmPC and BNF
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Confirmation of the UK authorisation details (the product record lacks an approved indication, dosage form and manufacturer)
- Trials or analyses that isolate cyclophosphamide's contribution within conditioning and PTCy regimens
- A monitoring plan for myelosuppression, haemorrhagic cystitis, cardiotoxicity (especially with anthracyclines) and secondary malignancy
