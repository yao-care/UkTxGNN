## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, minocycline is a tetracycline antibiotic with established efficacy in bacterial infections and acne, and mechanistically it may be applicable to inflammatory eye surface disease.

Tetracyclines, including minocycline, are known to have anti-inflammatory activity and to inhibit matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs). Related drugs of this class are used in ocular surface conditions such as meibomian gland dysfunction and blepharitis. That is a reasonable basis for the model's prediction. However, this is an inference from drug class only. The data compiled for this report contain no supporting trials or publications for punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications and conditions of concern (not taken from label text):** kidney disease, liver disease, gallbladder disease, *Clostridium difficile* colitis, pyoureter, perinatal necrotising enterocolitis, pseudotumour cerebri (idiopathic intracranial hypertension), intracranial hypertension and oesophageal dyskinesia. These should be checked against the SmPC.
- **Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions were identified in total; the first 20 are available for review.
  - **Major:** isotretinoin and acitretin. Retinoids combined with tetracyclines carry a risk of raised intracranial pressure.
  - **Moderate:** ethinylestradiol, amoxicillin, ampicillin, aminolevulinic acid (topical), sodium citrate, calcium phosphate, calcium acetate, bismuth subcitrate potassium, balsalazide, asparaginase products, brentuximab vedotin, calaspargase pegol and cannabidiol.
  - **Minor:** acetazolamide, hydrochlorothiazide, amiloride and bumetanide.

Please refer to the SmPC and BNF for full safety information. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction has a high model score (0.9963) but is Level L5: there are no trials, no publications and no mechanism data. The tetracycline class rationale is plausible but unsupported by evidence here.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Literature review of tetracycline use (including minocycline) in punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis and related ocular surface disease
- Mechanism of action data for minocycline
- Approved indication text, dosage form and PL number from the MHRA record
- Assessment of route and formulation suitability, as only an oral tablet is listed and an ocular indication may need topical or other delivery
- Review of safety against the full SmPC, including intracranial hypertension and retinoid interactions
