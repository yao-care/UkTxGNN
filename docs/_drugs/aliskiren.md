## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on established knowledge, aliskiren directly inhibits renin, the first and rate-limiting step of the renin-angiotensin system (RAS). Its efficacy in hypertension is established.

For pulmonary hypertension, a plausible link is that RAS activation contributes to pulmonary vascular remodelling and tone, so blocking renin could be relevant. This link has not been tested for this indication in the data supplied, and the TxGNN prediction rests on knowledge-graph patterns alone.

The related prediction of pulmonary hypertension owing to lung disease and/or hypoxia has indirect support only. Rat studies link intermittent hypoxia to angiotensin II-mediated sympathoexcitation, and one study suggests aliskiren may attenuate hypoxia-induced atrial remodelling. None of this addresses pulmonary hypertension outcomes in humans.

## Safety Considerations

**Contraindications (knowledge-graph associations, not label text):** the TxGNN knowledge graph links aliskiren to contraindications in the following groups. These should be checked against the SmPC.
- **Angioedema:** including hereditary, acquired and RAAS-blocker-induced forms
- **Liver and biliary:** liver failure, liver disease, hepatic encephalopathy and coma, gallbladder disease, pancreatitis, neonatal jaundice
- **Renal:** impaired renal function, anuria, renal artery obstruction
- **Metabolic and electrolyte:** diabetes mellitus, diabetic ketoacidosis, potassium deficiency, hypercalcaemia, phosphorus metabolism disorders, nephrocalcinosis, hyperparathyroidism, osteitis fibrosa, gout, hyperuricaemia
- **Other:** systemic lupus erythematosus, neutropenia, agranulocytosis, hypotension

**Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions were identified, and the 20 shown in the data compiled for this report are all of moderate severity. Main groups:
- Corticosteroids: hydrocortisone, prednisolone, prednisone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, budesonide
- SGLT2 inhibitors: canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, ertugliflozin
- Potassium preparations (potassium chloride, bicarbonate, citrate, gluconate): hyperkalaemia risk
- Azole antifungals and macrolides: clotrimazole, miconazole, clarithromycin
- Others: aspirin, aprepitant, eliglustat

**Evidence-based cautions from the literature:**
- ALTITUDE (PMID 26188211, 23418282) raised concerns about hypotension and ischaemic stroke in high-risk diabetic patients taking aliskiren with ACE inhibitor or ARB.
- In bilateral renal artery stenosis, renal function and hyperkalaemia need consideration.

Please refer to the SmPC and BNF for full safety information. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The pulmonary hypertension prediction has a very high TxGNN score but no trials or literature (L5). The other predictions have only indirect or preclinical support. Cerebrovascular disorder has the most evidence (a terminated, underpowered Phase 2/3 surrogate-endpoint trial), but efficacy in humans is unproven and ALTITUDE raised safety signals.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data to support the mechanistic-link analysis
- Verified SmPC and BNF warnings and contraindications, as the label-based safety data are incomplete
- Preclinical or early clinical studies of aliskiren in pulmonary hypertension models or patients
- A review of the UK marketing authorisation details (product form, indication, PL number)
- For cerebrovascular disorder, a properly powered outcome study with a safety plan for diabetic and renal-impaired patients and for co-prescription with ACE inhibitors or ARBs
