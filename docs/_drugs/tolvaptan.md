## Tolvaptan: From Inappropriate ADH Syndrome to Polycystic Kidney Disease 3 (With or Without Polycystic Liver Disease)
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Tolvaptan is a selective vasopressin V2 receptor antagonist, originally used for syndrome of inappropriate antidiuretic hormone secretion (SIADH), nephrogenic syndrome of inappropriate antidiuresis and polycystic kidney disease.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **polycystic kidney disease 3 with or without polycystic liver disease**,
with **no registered clinical trials** but **20 publications** (including two pivotal RCTs and a Cochrane systematic review) supporting this direction, largely through its established use in autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease (ADPKD).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, tolvaptan is a selective vasopressin V2 receptor antagonist. In polycystic kidney disease, vasopressin-driven cAMP signalling promotes cyst growth and fluid secretion. Blocking the V2 receptor lowers cAMP in renal tubular cells, which is the mechanism behind its use in ADPKD.

PKD3 (GANAB-related) is a close genetic and phenotypic variant of ADPKD, so the evidence extends by mechanism and disease class. This is effectively an on-label or near-label indication rather than true repurposing.

An important caveat is that the pivotal trials (TEMPO 3:4 and REPRISE) enrolled mainly PKD1/PKD2 patients, not GANAB-specific cohorts. Direct evidence in PKD3 is therefore lacking.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications suggested by the knowledge graph (not label text):** liver failure, cirrhosis of the liver, substance abuse/dependence, active peptic ulcer disease and demyelinating disease.
- **Hepatotoxicity:** The pivotal ADPKD trials reported more elevations in aminotransferase and bilirubin levels, so liver function monitoring is needed.
- **Aquaresis-related adverse effects** (such as thirst and polyuria) are expected from the mechanism.
- **Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions were identified. Notable examples include:
  - **Major:** clarithromycin
  - **Moderate:** aprepitant, dexamethasone, rolapitant, miconazole, empagliflozin, loperamide, naltrexone, rifaximin, and several potassium salts (citrate, bicarbonate, chloride, gluconate)
  - **Minor:** fidaxomicin, naloxegol, prucalopride

Please refer to the SmPC and BNF for full safety information. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Two RCTs and a Cochrane systematic review support tolvaptan in ADPKD, and PKD3 is a close variant of the same disease class. However, no PKD3/GANAB-specific data exist, and the hepatotoxicity risk requires structured monitoring.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Confirmation of the approved indication text, dosage form and PL number for the UK authorisation
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Label-based warnings and contraindications from the SmPC and BNF
- Evidence or registry data in GANAB-related PKD3 patients
- A liver function monitoring plan and review of interacting medicines, particularly strong CYP3A inhibitors such as clarithromycin
