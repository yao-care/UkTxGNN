## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Bromfenac is a topical NSAID that inhibits cyclooxygenase (COX-1 and COX-2) and so reduces prostaglandin-mediated inflammation and pain in the eye. Detailed mechanism of action data were not available in the compiled data, so this description reflects the established pharmacology of the drug class.

Several ocular conditions share this prostaglandin pathway, including post-cataract inflammation, cystoid macular oedema, dry eye and VEGF-driven maculopathies. This makes an anti-inflammatory eye drop a plausible candidate across them.

"Eye disease" is a very broad label. The strongest evidence is for postoperative inflammation and pain after cataract surgery, which is already an established use. Evidence for other sub-indications, such as dry eye and macular oedema, is less consistent and should be judged separately.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications flagged in the knowledge graph** (drug-disease links, not label text): rheumatoid arthritis, diabetes mellitus and diabetic ketoacidosis. These should be checked against the SmPC.
- **Drug interactions**: 100 interactions were identified. Most of those listed are of moderate severity, with a few minor. Examples include:
  - Corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, triamcinolone, budesonide)
  - Acetylsalicylic acid
  - Mesalazine and balsalazide
  - Metformin, glimepiride and chlorpropamide
  - Vancomycin and kanamycin
- **Reports in the literature**: corneal melting and perforation (including in Stevens-Johnson syndrome and undiagnosed Sjögren's syndrome), and a case of toxic epidermal necrolysis after bromfenac eye drops. Systemic bromfenac was withdrawn in the past because of severe liver injury. This is a caution for any ocular use, particularly in people with ocular surface disease.

Please refer to the SmPC and BNF for full safety information. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Multiple completed Phase 3 trials support bromfenac in ocular inflammation and pain after cataract surgery, with some support for dry eye and macular oedema. "Eye disease" is too broad to treat as a single indication, so conclusions should be made per sub-indication.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Approved indication text, dosage form and manufacturer for the UK licence
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Label warnings and contraindications from the SmPC
- A defined target sub-indication (for example dry eye or macular oedema) with dedicated efficacy evidence
- Monitoring for corneal adverse events, especially in patients with ocular surface disease

**Other predictions in the data (not recommended for progression):**
Optic papillitis has only indirect evidence, and the remaining predictions (including von Hippel anomaly, seborrheic keratosis and lagophthalmos) have no supporting evidence. All are rated Hold.
