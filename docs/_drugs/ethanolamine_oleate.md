## Ethanolamine Oleate: From Variceal Sclerotherapy to Oesophageal Disease
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Ethanolamine oleate is an injectable sclerosing agent, used in the UK as a 5% solution for injection.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **oesophageal disease**, but the literature supplied for this prediction mostly describes its established use in oesophageal varices. There are **no registered clinical trials** and **19 publications** (of which 3 are RCTs).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Ethanolamine oleate is known to be a sclerosing agent: it damages the vascular endothelium, causing inflammation, thrombosis and fibrosis that obliterate the treated vessel.

The literature shows it is already used in oesophageal varices, in the form of endoscopic injection sclerotherapy. The "oesophageal disease" prediction therefore largely reflects an established use rather than a true repurposing signal. Evidence for non-variceal oesophageal conditions is limited to small studies and reviews in idiopathic achalasia, where injection into the lower oesophageal sphincter has been explored in patients resistant to standard treatments.

The other nine predictions (for example platelet disorders, Glanzmann thrombasthenia, diabetic retinopathy and neonatal thrombocytopenia) have no clinical trials or supporting literature and no plausible mechanistic link. They are not recommended for further consideration.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the SmPC and BNF for safety information. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

Points relevant to this review: a 1985 in vitro study of a related sclerosant (sodium morrhuate, PMID 4056566) showed damage to red cells and endothelial cells and granulocyte stimulation, which is a concern for any use outside local sclerotherapy, particularly in neonates.

---

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The predicted "oesophageal disease" indication largely reflects existing use in oesophageal varices, so it is not a true repurposing finding. Evidence for genuinely new indications is limited to small achalasia studies, and the other predictions have no supporting evidence.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The approved indication text from the SmPC, to confirm whether variceal use is already licensed in the UK
- Mechanism of action data and safety information (warnings, contraindications, interactions)
- Clarification of the specific oesophageal condition to be targeted (for example achalasia) and a review of NICE guidance and current UK practice
- Registered, controlled trials in any non-variceal oesophageal indication, with a safety plan addressing endothelial and haemolytic toxicity
