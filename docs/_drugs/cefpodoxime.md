## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, cefpodoxime is a third-generation cephalosporin that inhibits bacterial cell wall synthesis by binding penicillin-binding proteins. Its efficacy lies in treating bacterial infections, and there is no established mechanistic route to osteoarthritis.

"Osteoarthritis susceptibility" describes a genetic risk concept rather than a treatable condition. The high TxGNN score most likely reflects the structure of the knowledge graph, not a real pharmacological relationship.

The other top-ranked predictions (osteoarthritis, rheumatoid arthritis, hemoglobinopathy and several rare skeletal dysplasias) show the same pattern: no plausible mechanism, no trials and no literature. For osteoarthritis and rheumatoid arthritis, an antibacterial has no known anti-inflammatory, chondroprotective or immunomodulatory action. In hemoglobinopathies, cephalosporins serve only a supportive role in treating infection.
