# Chlorthalidone: From Hypertension to Primary Hereditary Glaucoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Chlorthalidone is a thiazide-like diuretic, originally used to treat hypertension and congestive heart failure.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **primary hereditary glaucoma**, but there are currently **0 clinical trials** and **0 publications** supporting this prediction.
The prediction is model-based only and should be treated as a hypothesis.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, chlorthalidone is a thiazide-like diuretic. Its efficacy in hypertension and heart failure is established, but a mechanistic link to primary hereditary glaucoma cannot be drawn from the data compiled for this report.

Thiazide-like diuretics are not an established glaucoma therapy. Chlorthalidone has weak carbonic anhydrase inhibitory activity, and carbonic anhydrase inhibitors lower intraocular pressure. This is a speculative link and is not supported by the data compiled here. The high TxGNN score therefore remains a model prediction only.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

The TxGNN score for primary hereditary glaucoma is high, but no clinical trials or publications support it, and the mechanistic link is speculative. The evidence level is L5 (model prediction only).
