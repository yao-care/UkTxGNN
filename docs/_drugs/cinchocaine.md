## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Cinchocaine is known as an amide-type local anaesthetic, a class that blocks voltage-gated sodium channels and so dampens nerve signalling at the site of application.

A local anaesthetic effect could plausibly reduce airway irritation or the cough reflex. However, there is no evidence that it treats the infectious or inflammatory process underlying bronchitis. The TxGNN score of 0.9977 is a computational output only, and no trials or literature were found to support it. The mechanistic link should therefore be regarded as speculative.
