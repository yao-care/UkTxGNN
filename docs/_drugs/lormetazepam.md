Lormetazepam is a short-acting benzodiazepine hypnotic that is already marketed in the UK as a 500 microgram tablet.

The more genuinely new predictions (such as anxiety and anxiety disorder) rest only on reviews and pharmacology literature.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, lormetazepam is a benzodiazepine that positively modulates GABA-A receptors, producing sedative-hypnotic effects. This is the established mechanism for treating insomnia.

Insomnia is therefore not a new indication: it is the drug's existing, marketed use. The high TxGNN score mainly confirms that the model recognises this known association.

Other predictions have weaker support. Anxiety and anxiety disorder follow the same GABA-A anxiolytic rationale, but lormetazepam is mainly positioned as a hypnotic and no efficacy RCT for anxiety was identified. Alcohol withdrawal delirium and barbiturate abuse have class-level rationale only (benzodiazepines are used in withdrawal and show cross-tolerance with barbiturates), with no lormetazepam-specific evidence. Several other predictions (antidepressant type abuse, hallucinogen abuse, cauda equina syndrome, benign paroxysmal torticollis of infancy) have no plausible therapeutic link, and the only literature for two of them is a wastewater analysis study.

## Safety Considerations

Specific warnings, contraindications and drug interaction data were not available in the data compiled for this report. Please refer to the SmPC and BNF for safety information. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

The published literature points to these themes for guardrails:
- **Dependence and rebound insomnia**: rebound insomnia after abrupt withdrawal is documented for lormetazepam, and its dependence liability has been reviewed.
- **Residual next-day effects**: reviews describe morning hangover at higher doses (for example 2 mg), with no residual sedation reported at 1 mg.
- **Memory impairment**: anterograde amnesia has been shown in healthy volunteers.
- **Older patients**: a terminated pharmacovigilance study highlighted the higher adverse reaction risk in this group.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails** (for insomnia only)

**Rationale:**
Insomnia is an established, marketed use, supported by a completed Phase 3 comparator trial and several RCTs, so this is confirmation of an existing indication rather than repurposing. The other predicted indications lack lormetazepam-specific efficacy evidence: anxiety and anxiety disorder are research questions (L4), and the remainder should be held (L5).

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Licence indication text and PL number from the MHRA record or SmPC, since neither was available.
- SmPC and BNF warnings, contraindications and interaction data, which were missing.
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank.
- Guardrails: short-term use only, monitoring for dependence and rebound insomnia, and caution in older patients.
- For anxiety, a controlled efficacy trial of lormetazepam specifically.
- Consolidation of the "insomnia" and "sleep disorder, initiating and maintaining sleep" entries, which describe the same condition.
