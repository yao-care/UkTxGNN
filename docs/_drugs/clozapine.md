## Clozapine: Manic bipolar affective disorder
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Clozapine is an atypical antipsychotic with a broad receptor-binding profile. The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **manic bipolar affective disorder**. Support is **1 completed Phase 2 trial** in treatment-resistant mania, plus a systematic review and meta-analysis of clozapine in bipolar disorder; there are no Phase 3 trials for this specific indication.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not currently available in the data compiled for this report. Based on clozapine's known pharmacology, it antagonises dopamine D2 and serotonin 5-HT2A receptors and also acts at muscarinic, adrenergic and histaminergic receptors. This broad profile provides a plausible antimanic and mood-stabilising mechanism, especially in treatment-resistant illness.

An important caveat is that the available evidence concerns bipolar disorder broadly rather than the manic subtype specifically. The completed Phase 2 trial was in treatment-resistant mania, which is the closest direct evidence.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were recorded. Examples classified as Major include bupropion, morphine and potassium citrate. Moderate examples include famotidine, epinephrine, metformin, pioglitazone, omeprazole, atropine, loperamide, glycopyrronium and several antidiabetic agents.
- **Conditions of concern**: Conditions linked to clozapine as contraindications or cautions include neutropenia, agranulocytosis, leukopenia, epilepsy, liver disease, diabetes mellitus and diabetic ketoacidosis, coronary artery disease, orthostatic hypotension, angle-closure glaucoma, paralytic ileus, respiratory failure, and benign prostatic hyperplasia. These come from relationship data, not label text.
- **Published safety concerns**: The literature raises metabolic, haematological and cardiac risks. Metabolic, haematological and cardiac monitoring is required.

Please refer to the SmPC and BNF for full safety information. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
A completed Phase 2 trial in treatment-resistant mania and a systematic review and meta-analysis in bipolar disorder support the prediction, giving an evidence level of L2. There are no Phase 3 trials, and the evidence applies to bipolar disorder broadly rather than the manic subtype alone. Clozapine's serious haematological, cardiac and metabolic risks require strict guardrails.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Phase 3 evidence specific to acute mania, or confirmation from the existing bipolar disorder data
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Clozapine blood monitoring (neutrophil counts) plus metabolic and cardiac monitoring plans
- Interaction review for patients taking antidiabetic, anticholinergic or other interacting medicines
- Review of the SmPC warnings, as labelled safety text was not available in the data compiled for this report
