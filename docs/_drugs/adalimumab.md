## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Adalimumab is known to neutralise TNF-α, a cytokine that drives inflammation in several immune-mediated conditions.

For this prediction, the mechanistic link is weak. Hypermobility of the coccyx is generally a mechanical or post-traumatic problem of the lowest part of the spine, not a condition with an established TNF-driven inflammatory basis. The high TxGNN score (0.9977) reflects a pattern found by the model and is not evidence that TNF blockade would help. Until a plausible biological pathway is shown, the prediction should be treated as a hypothesis only.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests only on a model score. No trials or publications address hypermobility of the coccyx, and the condition has no recognised TNF-mediated mechanism. The evidence level is L5.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A plausible biological rationale linking TNF inhibition to coccygeal hypermobility, supported by mechanism of action data
- Preclinical or observational data in this condition
- A review of safety and interaction data in the SmPC and BNF, including the immunosuppression-related interactions above
