# Adalimumab: From Crohn's Disease to Rheumatoid Vasculitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Adalimumab is an anti-TNF-alpha monoclonal antibody, originally used for inflammatory conditions such as Crohn's disease, rheumatoid arthritis, psoriatic arthritis, ankylosing spondylitis and non-infectious uveitis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **rheumatoid vasculitis** (top-ranked prediction), but the supporting evidence is limited to **case reports and reviews**, with **1 registered trial** that does not test adalimumab for this condition.
Of the other high-scoring predictions, only those that overlap with existing approved uses (inflammatory spondylopathy and polyarticular juvenile arthritis) have strong trial evidence.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, adalimumab is a fully human monoclonal antibody that neutralises TNF-alpha, a central cytokine in rheumatoid arthritis and in the vascular inflammation seen in rheumatoid vasculitis. Its efficacy in rheumatoid arthritis is established, and rheumatoid vasculitis is a severe extra-articular complication of the same disease, so the mechanism is plausible.

However, the supporting evidence is thin. One case report describes digital vasculitis in a patient with rheumatoid arthritis who responded well to adalimumab. Another describes an acute pulmonary hypertension crisis in a patient with rheumatoid vasculitis eight months after adalimumab dose reduction. Conversely, anti-TNF agents have also been reported to *induce* vasculitis, antiphospholipid syndrome and ANCA-associated nephritis, so the benefit-risk balance is unclear.

The other top predictions fall into three groups:
- **Inflammatory spondylopathy** and **polyarticular juvenile rheumatoid arthritis**: these largely overlap with adalimumab's existing approved uses (ankylosing spondylitis/spondyloarthropathy and juvenile idiopathic arthritis), so they are not true repurposing findings.
- **Vertebral disease**: a broad term. The literature concerns spinal involvement in axial spondyloarthritis, so it should be narrowed to a specific diagnosis before any decision.
- **Hypermobility of coccyx** and **Kummell disease**: high scores are computational only. These are mechanical or post-traumatic conditions with no established TNF-driven mechanism, and no trials or literature were found.

## Safety Considerations

**Drug Interactions** (100 interactions recorded; main ones shown):
- **Major**: corticosteroids (betamethasone, budesonide, dexamethasone, hydrocortisone, prednisolone, prednisone, triamcinolone), deferiprone, and radiopharmaceuticals/antibody agents (ibritumomab tiuxetan, iobenguane I-131, tositumomab, tositumomab I-131)
- **Moderate**: metronidazole, tinidazole, rosuvastatin, simvastatin
- **Minor**: zinc salts (acetate, gluconate, sulfate)

**Class safety signals from the literature**: infection risk including tuberculosis reactivation, herpes zoster, and rare immune-mediated events such as drug-induced lupus, vasculitis, antiphospholipid syndrome and ANCA-associated nephritis.

Please refer to the SmPC and BNF for warnings and contraindications. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
For rheumatoid vasculitis, evidence is limited to case reports (including conflicting reports of anti-TNF-induced vasculitis), and the one registered trial does not test adalimumab for this condition. The strongest-supported predictions (inflammatory spondylopathy and polyarticular juvenile arthritis, both L1 and "Proceed with Guardrails") already fall within existing approved uses, so they are not new repurposing opportunities. Hypermobility of coccyx and Kummell disease are computational predictions only (L5, Hold).

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Controlled or at least larger observational studies of adalimumab in rheumatoid vasculitis, with safety assessment given the reports of anti-TNF-induced vasculitis
- Mechanism of action data to be added to the record
- Confirmation of licensed indications and dosage form from the SmPC/BNF, as the UK licence record lacks this information
- For on-label overlapping indications, guardrails: infection and tuberculosis screening, weight-based paediatric dosing, uveitis monitoring, and long-term safety follow-up
- Narrowing "vertebral disease" to a specific diagnosis (e.g. axial spondyloarthritis) before any decision
