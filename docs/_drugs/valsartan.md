## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Valsartan belongs to the angiotensin II type 1 (AT1) receptor blocker class. Its effect on blood pressure is well established.

Hypertensive kidney injury involves activation of the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS). Blocking the AT1 receptor is therefore mechanistically reasonable in this setting.

However, this is a theoretical link only. No study in the compiled data tests valsartan in malignant hypertensive renal disease. The prediction rests essentially on the model score.

## Safety Considerations

**Drug Interactions** (100 interactions were recorded; the main ones are listed):
- **Major:** potassium bicarbonate and potassium chloride. Combining them with valsartan risks raised serum potassium.
- **Moderate:** insulin products (including human, aspart, degludec, detemir, glargine, glulisine and lispro), exenatide, hydrocortisone, eluxadoline and polyethylene glycol 3350 with electrolytes.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
- The evidence level is L5. There are no trials, and the only cited publication is a preclinical study of a different drug. Support rests on the model prediction alone.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Studies that directly evaluate valsartan in malignant hypertensive renal disease, starting with preclinical or observational data and then controlled clinical studies
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Label-based warnings and contraindications for safety screening, particularly renal function and potassium monitoring in severe kidney disease
