## Rifabutin: HIV infectious disease

## One-Sentence Summary

Rifabutin is a rifamycin antibiotic that inhibits bacterial DNA-dependent RNA polymerase. The TxGNN model predicts it may be relevant to **HIV infectious disease**. Although 35 registered trials and 20 publications were retrieved, they study mycobacterial infections (tuberculosis, *Mycobacterium avium* complex) in people with HIV, or interactions with antiretrovirals. None studies HIV infection itself, so the evidence level is **L5**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Rifabutin inhibits the bacterial RNA polymerase (rpoB) and has no direct anti-HIV activity. The model's high score is therefore best read as an association with HIV-related care, not as evidence of antiviral action.

In people with HIV, rifabutin is used alongside antiretroviral therapy to treat and prevent opportunistic mycobacterial infections. It induces CYP3A less strongly than rifampicin, which is why it is often preferred when a protease inhibitor is needed. The clinically meaningful link is this interaction profile and its use in HIV-associated mycobacterial disease, not any effect on HIV itself.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were identified. The main ones are:
  - Major: eliglustat.
  - Moderate: clarithromycin, metronidazole, doxycycline, corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, triamcinolone), morphine, aprepitant, pioglitazone, saxagliptin, linagliptin, and several sulfonylureas (chlorpropamide, acetohexamide).
  - Rifabutin also interacts with antiretrovirals, notably protease inhibitors, dolutegravir and long-acting cabotegravir/rilpivirine. These interactions need specialist review.
- **Haematological toxicity**: Severe neutropenia led to early termination of a paediatric study, and treatment-limiting neutropenia has been reported in children on protease inhibitors. Blood counts should be monitored.

Please also refer to the SmPC and BNF for full safety information. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
- No retrieved study treats HIV infection itself, and rifabutin has no direct antiviral activity.
- The retrieved studies concern opportunistic mycobacterial disease and antiretroviral interactions, so the evidence level is L5.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A clear definition of the intended use. HIV-associated mycobacterial disease is a different question from HIV infection and would need to be evaluated as such.
- Any claim of anti-HIV benefit would need supporting preclinical or clinical data. None was found.
- A drug-interaction management plan covering antiretrovirals, and a neutropenia monitoring plan, particularly for children.
- Licence indication and label safety text from the SmPC, which are not recorded in the local data.
