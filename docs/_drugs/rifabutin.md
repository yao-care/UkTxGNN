# Rifabutin: From Tuberculosis/MAC Infection to HIV Infectious Disease

## One-Sentence Summary

Rifabutin is a rifamycin-class antimycobacterial, established for treating and preventing tuberculosis (TB) and *Mycobacterium avium* complex (MAC) infection. The TxGNN model predicts it may be relevant to **HIV Infectious Disease**, with **39 clinical trials** and **20 publications** currently supporting this direction — though, as detailed below, this evidence largely reflects rifabutin's role in managing HIV-associated mycobacterial co-infections rather than a direct antiretroviral effect.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available for this drug in the evidence pack. Based on known pharmacology, Rifabutin belongs to the rifamycin class of antimycobacterial antibiotics, structurally related to rifampicin, and its efficacy against tuberculosis and MAC infection is well established. Rifabutin was specifically developed as a rifampicin alternative because it is a substantially weaker inducer of cytochrome P450 3A4 (CYP3A4) — a property that makes it far more compatible with antiretroviral regimens (protease inhibitors, NNRTIs, integrase inhibitors) than rifampicin.

The relationship between the original indication and the "new" prediction is not that rifabutin treats HIV itself, but that TB and MAC are the two most significant mycobacterial co-infections in people living with HIV. The overwhelming majority of the supporting trial and literature evidence relates to rifabutin's use in HIV/TB co-treatment, MAC prophylaxis in advanced HIV/AIDS, and — very heavily — pharmacokinetic drug–drug interaction studies with antiretroviral agents (protease inhibitors, dolutegravir, cabotegravir, maraviroc, etc.), reflecting its critical role as the rifamycin of choice in HIV-positive patients requiring TB or MAC therapy.

Mechanistically, this makes the prediction reasonable as an **indication of clinical relevance to the HIV population** rather than a genuine antiretroviral repurposing signal. This distinction should be made explicit in any downstream decision material, since "effective for HIV Infectious Disease" could otherwise be misread as anti-HIV activity.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The evidence base is strong (L1 — multiple completed Phase 3 trials plus a Cochrane systematic review) and rifabutin's role in managing HIV-associated TB/MAC co-infection is well documented. However, a **Blocking** data gap on essential safety information (SmPC warnings/contraindications) prevents entry into the S1 safety review stage, and the drug has no current UK marketing authorisation.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Resolve DG001: obtain MHRA-approved SmPC/PIL (warnings, contraindications, DDI profile)
- Resolve DG002: source original MOA documentation from DrugBank to strengthen the mechanistic rationale
- Confirm whether any UK access route exists (e.g. named-patient/unlicensed import), given the pack shows zero UK licences
- Reframe the indication precisely as "management of HIV-associated mycobacterial co-infection (TB/MAC)" rather than "HIV Infectious Disease" treatment, to avoid implying direct antiretroviral activity
- Given the extensive documented CYP3A4-mediated interactions with antiretrovirals, commission a full DDI review against the current UK ART formulary before any clinical-use recommendation
