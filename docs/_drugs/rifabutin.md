## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Rifabutin inhibits bacterial DNA-dependent RNA polymerase (rpoB). It has no direct anti-HIV activity, so the mechanism does not support activity against the virus itself.

Its relevance in HIV is indirect. It is used in people with HIV who have opportunistic mycobacterial infections, such as *Mycobacterium avium* complex (MAC) and tuberculosis. It is a weaker CYP3A inducer than rifampicin, which makes it a preferred rifamycin alongside protease inhibitors. The high TxGNN score probably reflects this close association between rifabutin and the HIV population. It should not be read as evidence that rifabutin treats HIV infection.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug interactions:** The interaction screen returned 100 interactions in total. Examples include a major interaction with eliglustat, and moderate interactions with clarithromycin, doxycycline, metronidazole, dexamethasone, hydrocortisone, pioglitazone and aprepitant. Rifabutin is a CYP3A inducer, and combinations with antiretrovirals (protease inhibitors, dolutegravir, cabotegravir/rilpivirine) need careful dose and interaction review.
- **Haematological toxicity:** Paediatric TB/HIV studies with lopinavir/ritonavir reported treatment-limiting neutropenia. Full blood count monitoring is advisable.

Please also refer to the SmPC and BNF for full safety information. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
No study in the compiled data treats HIV infection itself, so the evidence level is L5. Rifabutin has no direct antiviral activity. Its established role is in mycobacterial disease in people with HIV, and the high prediction score should be read in that light.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Evidence from studies that directly assess HIV infection as an outcome, or a clear reframing of the question around HIV-associated opportunistic mycobacterial disease.
- UK licence indication text and SmPC safety information (warnings and contraindications), which are not recorded in the local data.
- A structured interaction-management plan for antiretroviral combinations.
- Haematological monitoring guidance, particularly for children and for patients on protease inhibitors.
