## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed, structured mechanism-of-action data for Fexofenadine is not available in this evidence pack (Data Gap DG002). However, the evidence pack's own repurposing rationale identifies Fexofenadine as a second-generation, selective H1 histamine receptor antagonist that acts peripherally and does not readily cross the blood–brain barrier.

The proposed link to rosacea conjunctivitis rests on the fact that allergic conjunctivitis is a classic IgE/histamine-mediated condition, and the erythema/flushing component of rosacea is associated with neurovascular inflammation and mast-cell degranulation (histamine release). On this basis, H1 blockade has a plausible — but indirect — mechanistic connection to a "rosacea with conjunctivitis" phenotype.

This link should be treated cautiously: the primary pathophysiology of rosacea (vascular dysregulation, *Demodex* mites, inflammatory mediators such as cathelicidin/TLR2) is not principally histamine-driven. The TxGNN score of 99.85% reflects the strength of association within the knowledge graph only, and does not constitute clinical evidence of efficacy.
