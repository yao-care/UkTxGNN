## Mercaptopurine: Myeloid leukemia
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Mercaptopurine is a purine antimetabolite used in cytotoxic chemotherapy. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **myeloid leukemia**, with a prediction score of 0.9994. Evidence is limited to **L3**: the supporting studies are mainly cohort and pilot studies of oral mercaptopurine maintenance, usually within multi-agent regimens, and no randomised trial tests mercaptopurine itself in this disease.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Mercaptopurine is a purine antimetabolite. Its thioguanine nucleotide (TGN) metabolites are incorporated into DNA and inhibit de novo purine synthesis, so it can act on rapidly dividing leukaemic blasts.

Myeloid leukaemia is also a malignancy of proliferating haematopoietic cells, so an antimetabolite mechanism is plausible. In myeloid disease, however, mercaptopurine has mostly been used as part of multi-agent regimens. These include oral mercaptopurine/methotrexate maintenance in acute myeloid leukaemia (AML), ALL-like consolidation in childhood AML, and maintenance in acute promyelocytic leukaemia. The contribution of mercaptopurine itself cannot be separated from the rest of the regimen.

## Safety Considerations

- **Conditions recorded as cautions or contraindications**: kidney disease, pancreatitis, liver disease, gallbladder disease, thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, agranulocytosis, anaemia (including macrocytic anaemia), hyperbilirubinaemia (including hereditary forms and Gilbert syndrome), cholestasis (including benign recurrent and familial intrahepatic forms), leukopenia, and hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma. These are not taken from label text, so check them against the SmPC.
- **Drug interactions** (100 recorded; examples): moderate interactions with mesalazine, balsalazide, olsalazine, sulfasalazine and naltrexone; a minor interaction with levofloxacin. Several others (for example prednisone, omeprazole, metformin) have an unknown severity level.

Please refer to the SmPC and BNF for full safety information. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanism is plausible and the TxGNN score is high (0.9994). The clinical evidence, however, consists mainly of cohort and pilot studies and multi-agent regimen trials. No randomised trial isolates mercaptopurine's contribution in myeloid leukaemia, so the evidence level is L3.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Prospective, ideally randomised, data isolating mercaptopurine's effect in AML maintenance
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Label-based warnings and contraindications from the SmPC and BNF
- A haematological and pharmacogenetic (TPMT/NUDT15) monitoring plan
