## Pimecrolimus: Seborrheic dermatitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Pimecrolimus is a topical calcineurin inhibitor, originally used to treat atopic eczema (atopic dermatitis).
with **1 clinical trial** (Phase 2, completed) and **18 publications** (including two systematic reviews of randomised controlled trials) currently supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not formally recorded in the data compiled for this report. However, the published literature describes pimecrolimus as a topical calcineurin inhibitor. It blocks T-cell activation and inhibits release of inflammatory cytokines (IL-2, IL-4, interferon-gamma and TNF-alpha). It also inhibits mast cell degranulation.

Atopic eczema is a T-cell-driven inflammatory skin disease. Seborrhoeic dermatitis is also an inflammatory skin condition, driven in part by an inflammatory response to *Malassezia* yeast on sebum-rich skin. A non-steroidal anti-inflammatory agent is therefore mechanistically plausible. It may also be attractive because long-term topical corticosteroid use on the face carries risks such as skin thinning.

The very high TxGNN score is consistent with this reasoning. Two systematic reviews of randomised trials suggest pimecrolimus 1% cream is well tolerated and effective in facial seborrhoeic dermatitis.

## Cytotoxicity

Pimecrolimus is not an antineoplastic drug, so this section does not apply.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications recorded in the knowledge graph (not label text):** the TxGNN knowledge graph links pimecrolimus with the following conditions, most of which are viral infections: common wart, epidermodysplasia verruciformis, anogenital human papillomavirus infection, herpes simplex infection, herpes simplex dermatitis, herpes zoster, chickenpox, infectious mononucleosis, chronic Epstein-Barr virus infection syndrome, lymphatic system disease, Netherton syndrome and ichthyosis linearis circumflexa. These should be checked against the current SmPC.
- **Drug interactions:** an interaction query returned 100 records, but none has a severity grading. Examples include pantoprazole, omeprazole, simvastatin, metformin, doxycycline, prednisone and hydrocortisone. Clinical significance has not been established from these data.
- **Long-term safety:** a systematic review and meta-analysis of cancer risk with topical calcineurin inhibitors (PMID 36370744) and an ongoing 8,000-patient paediatric registry (NCT00568997, estimated completion 2028) address the long-term malignancy question. Boxed-warning considerations, particularly use in infants, apply.
- **Diagnostic caution:** topical calcineurin inhibitors can modify the appearance of fungal skin infections (tinea incognito), which can mimic seborrhoeic dermatitis.

Please also refer to the SmPC and BNF for full safety information. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
One completed Phase 2 randomised, double-blind trial and two systematic reviews of RCTs support pimecrolimus in facial seborrhoeic dermatitis, and the mechanism is plausible. There is no Phase 3 evidence, so the evidence level is L2 and use should remain cautious and well monitored.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Phase 3 confirmatory RCTs in seborrhoeic dermatitis, with longer follow-up than the 4-week Phase 2 trial
- Review of the current UK SmPC (warnings, contraindications, and approved indication text), which is missing from the data compiled for this report
- Formal mechanism of action data and graded interaction data
- A plan to address long-term safety (malignancy surveillance, infant use) and to exclude fungal and viral skin infections before treatment
- Verification of the UK authorisation record and NICE/BNF positioning

- Evidence is strong (multiple Phase 3/4 trials, a 5-year randomised safety trial, network meta-analyses and guidelines), but varies by subtype (e.g. perioral dermatitis).
- **Exanthem (score 0.9932, L2, Research Question):** A non-specific label. The trials cover oral and genital lichen planus or lichen sclerosus, and EGFR-inhibitor rash, all small pilots. No single conclusion can be drawn.
- **Acrodermatitis chronica atrophicans (score 0.9901, L5, Hold):** Prediction score only, with no trials or literature. This is a late manifestation of Lyme disease, treated with antibiotics, and an immunosuppressant has no clear rationale and could be counterproductive.
