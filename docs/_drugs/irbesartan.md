## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, Irbesartan is part of the angiotensin II receptor blocker (ARB) class, and its efficacy in hypertension has been proven; mechanistically it may be applicable to malignant renovascular hypertension because both conditions involve the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS).

Malignant renovascular hypertension is driven by activation of the RAAS pathway, and ARBs act directly on this pathway by blocking the AT1 receptor. This gives the prediction pharmacological plausibility. However, malignant hypertension is a hypertensive emergency that is conventionally managed with intravenous antihypertensives for rapid blood pressure control; oral ARBs such as irbesartan are more appropriate for chronic management once the acute crisis has resolved, rather than as first-line emergency treatment.

It is important to note that this rationale is based on generic ARB-class pharmacology rather than drug-specific mechanistic data, since the original MOA field for irbesartan is currently a data gap. No clinical trial or literature evidence specific to this drug-disease pairing has been identified to corroborate the mechanistic hypothesis.
