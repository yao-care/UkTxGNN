## Clobetasol: From Topical Corticosteroid-Responsive Skin Conditions to Primary Cutaneous T-Cell Lymphoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Clobetasol is a super-potent topical corticosteroid, here represented by a clobetasol/neomycin/nystatin ointment product marketed in the UK.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **primary cutaneous T-cell lymphoma** (mainly early-stage mycosis fungoides),

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. Clobetasol propionate is a super-potent topical glucocorticoid. Glucocorticoid receptor activation is known to induce apoptosis in malignant T cells and to suppress local inflammatory and cytokine signalling in the skin.

This is the rationale for using topical corticosteroids in early-stage (patch/plaque) mycosis fungoides, the most common form of primary cutaneous T-cell lymphoma. The high TxGNN score is consistent with this known pharmacology.

The effect is expected to be limited to skin-directed treatment of early-stage disease. It is not expected to help in advanced or systemic disease such as Sézary syndrome or angioimmunoblastic T-cell lymphoma.

Examples include other corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, prednisolone, prednisone, triamcinolone), proton pump inhibitors (omeprazole, pantoprazole, lansoprazole), doxycycline, metronidazole, vancomycin, clotrimazole, morphine, calcitriol and vitamin A. ## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
The pharmacological rationale is well established, and observational data (a clinical series of about 200 patients and two cohort studies) support clobetasol in early-stage mycosis fungoides. However, there are no registered trials or RCTs, and the evidence is limited to skin-directed treatment of early-stage disease.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Prospective or controlled trial data in early-stage mycosis fungoides
- Confirmation of the UK approved indication, dosage form and SmPC safety information (warnings and contraindications) for the specific product
- Clarification of whether the evidence for single-agent clobetasol applies to the clobetasol/neomycin/nystatin combination
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- A monitoring plan for local skin adverse effects (e.g. atrophy) and systemic absorption with prolonged use
- Restriction to early-stage disease, with specialist dermatology or haemato-oncology oversight
