Clobetasol is a super-potent topical corticosteroid, listed in the UK BNF prescribing codes in many forms, including a clobetasol/neomycin/nystatin ointment product.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **primary cutaneous T-cell lymphoma** (mainly early-stage mycosis fungoides),

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

According to the Mechanism of Action section of the US FDA-approved prescribing information for clobetasol: "Like other topical corticosteroids, clobetasol propionate has anti-inflammatory, antipruritic, and vasoconstrictive properties. The mechanism of the anti-inflammatory activity of the topical steroids, in general, is unclear." ([DailyMed: Clobetasol Propionate (Emollient) (clobetasol propionate) prescribing information, The J. Molner Company LLC](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=26ccb1d8-c466-e238-e063-6394a90a0069), label version 2026-11-10; accessed 2026-11-19.) Clobetasol propionate is a super-potent topical glucocorticoid. Glucocorticoid receptor activation is known to induce apoptosis in malignant T cells and to suppress local inflammatory and cytokine signalling in the skin.

This is the rationale for using topical corticosteroids in early-stage (patch/plaque) mycosis fungoides, the most common form of primary cutaneous T-cell lymphoma. The high TxGNN score is consistent with this known pharmacology.

The effect is expected to be limited to skin-directed treatment of early-stage disease. It is not expected to help in advanced or systemic disease such as Sézary syndrome or angioimmunoblastic T-cell lymphoma.

## Safety Considerations

**Drug Interactions:** 175 potential interactions were recorded, but none has a severity grading. Examples include other corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, prednisolone, prednisone, triamcinolone), proton pump inhibitors (omeprazole, pantoprazole, lansoprazole), doxycycline, metronidazole, vancomycin, clotrimazole, morphine, calcitriol and vitamin A. Clinical significance should be checked in the BNF.

Please refer to the SmPC and BNF for warnings and contraindications. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
The pharmacological rationale is well established, and observational data (a clinical series of about 200 patients and two cohort studies) support clobetasol in early-stage mycosis fungoides. However, there are no registered trials or RCTs, and the evidence is limited to skin-directed treatment of early-stage disease.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Prospective or controlled trial data in early-stage mycosis fungoides
- Confirmation of the UK approved indication, dosage form and SmPC safety information (warnings and contraindications) for the specific product
- Clarification of whether the evidence for single-agent clobetasol applies to the clobetasol/neomycin/nystatin combination
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- A monitoring plan for local skin adverse effects (e.g. atrophy) and systemic absorption with prolonged use
- Restriction to early-stage disease, with specialist dermatology or haemato-oncology oversight
