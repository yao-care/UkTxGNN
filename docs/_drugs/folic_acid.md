# Folic Acid: From Vitamin Supplementation to Biotin Metabolic Disease

## One-Sentence Summary

Folic acid (vitamin B9) is a well-established vitamin supplement, generally used to treat and prevent folate deficiency. The TxGNN model predicts a possible role in **biotin metabolic disease**, but this is currently supported only by indirect, mechanism-level literature and general micronutrient trials, with **13 clinical trials** and **20 publications** identified — none of which directly test folic acid in this indication.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in this evidence pack (data gap DG002). Based on general pharmacological knowledge, folic acid is a water-soluble B-vitamin (vitamin B9) that acts as a cofactor in one-carbon metabolism, supporting DNA synthesis and methylation reactions; its efficacy in treating folate deficiency and preventing neural tube defects is well established.

Biotin metabolic disease (e.g. biotinidase deficiency and related biotin-responsive disorders) belongs to a broader group of "vitamin-responsive inborn errors of metabolism," in which several B-vitamins — including folate, cobalamin (B12), and biotin — act as coenzymes for related metabolic pathways. This shared classification, rather than a direct pharmacological mechanism, is the most plausible basis for the TxGNN association.

The supporting literature (e.g. PMID 23622402, "Vitamin-responsive disorders: cobalamin, folate, biotin, vitamins B1 and E") discusses folate and biotin together as cofactor-dependent conditions, but no publication or trial identified here directly evaluates folic acid as a treatment for biotin metabolic disease specifically. The prediction should therefore be regarded as mechanistically plausible in a general "vitamin cofactor" sense, rather than as evidence of a direct therapeutic effect.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The link between folic acid and biotin metabolic disease is currently supported only by general reviews grouping folate and biotin as related vitamin cofactors, with no dedicated trial or study evaluating this specific drug-disease pair. Combined with a blocking safety data gap (DG001) and no confirmed UK marketing authorisation on record, the evidence is insufficient to proceed further at this time.
