## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not currently available for oxytetracycline in this evidence pack. Based on known pharmacology, oxytetracycline belongs to the tetracycline class of antibiotics, whose antibacterial efficacy in respiratory and other bacterial infections is well established through decades of clinical use, even though it currently lacks a UK marketing authorisation.

The rationale linking oxytetracycline to chronic rhinosinusitis is indirect. Chronic rhinosinusitis frequently involves bacterial colonisation and mucosal inflammation, and a related tetracycline, doxycycline, has published support for an anti-inflammatory effect (matrix metalloproteinase inhibition) independent of its antibacterial action. By class analogy, a similar mechanism is biologically plausible for oxytetracycline. However, this is an extrapolation from a related molecule rather than direct evidence — no clinical trials or literature specific to oxytetracycline in chronic rhinosinusitis currently exist.

Given the complete absence of direct supporting data, this candidate should be regarded as a research hypothesis generated by the knowledge graph rather than a clinically actionable signal at this stage.
