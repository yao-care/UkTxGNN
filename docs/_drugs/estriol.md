# Estriol: From Menopausal Vasomotor Symptoms to Amenorrhoea

## One-Sentence Summary

Estriol is a weak, short-acting oestrogen that has been marketed in Europe and Asia for many years, mainly for post-menopausal hot flushes.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **amenorrhoea**, particularly functional hypothalamic amenorrhoea (FHA).
Support is limited: **no registered clinical trials** and **6 publications**, of which only 2 are directly relevant to estriol and FHA (one small human physiological study and one review).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Pharmacology records show that estriol acts on oestrogen receptor alpha (ESR1) and oestrogen receptor beta (ESR2). It is generally described as a weak oestrogen with preferential receptor beta activity.

In functional hypothalamic amenorrhoea, stress, low energy availability or other stressors suppress pulsatile GnRH secretion from the hypothalamus. This lowers LH and FSH release, reduces ovarian function and leaves the woman hypo-oestrogenic. Replacing or modulating oestrogen signalling is therefore biologically plausible.

A 2012 study (PMID 22137494) reported that estriol modulated LH secretion in women with FHA. A 2023 review (PMID 37371858) proposes low-dose oestrogens as neuroendocrine modulators in FHA. These findings support the mechanism, but neither shows that estriol restores menses or ovulation. The high TxGNN score is a computational prediction, not clinical evidence.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the SmPC and BNF for safety information. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

The pharmacology data confirm that estriol binds oestrogen receptors alpha and beta, so the usual class considerations for oestrogens apply. These should be checked against the product SmPC.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The evidence consists of one small physiological study and one review in FHA, with no registered trials and no data on restoring menses or ovulation. The TxGNN score alone is not clinical evidence, and the UK product data lack indication and safety details.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- UK SmPC and BNF warnings and contraindications for the authorised product, and its approved indication and dosage form
- Mechanism of action data confirmed from DrugBank or the SmPC
- Clinical efficacy data (menstrual recovery, ovulation) from controlled studies in FHA
- Assessment of route and formulation suitability, since the only UK product identified is a vaginal cream
- A comparison with established FHA management (lifestyle and nutritional correction, and standard hormone therapy)
