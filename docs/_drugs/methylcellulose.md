## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available for methylcellulose (data gap). Based on the literature captured in this evidence pack, methylcellulose is a cellulose-derived polymer used almost exclusively as a **pharmaceutical excipient** — a thickening agent, bulk-forming laxative, ophthalmic lubricant, and, most relevantly here, a thermosensitive hydrogel/scaffold carrier. It is not described anywhere in the supporting literature as a pharmacologically active anti-inflammatory or chondroprotective agent in its own right.

Nearly all 18 associated publications feature methylcellulose (or the closely related carboxymethylcellulose) as an **inert delivery vehicle** for genuinely active agents — nanozymes, TIMP-3 protein, mesenchymal stromal cell spheroids, celecoxib, and meloxicam — used to treat osteoarthritis. This suggests the TxGNN knowledge-graph association may be capturing a "drug-delivery-carrier ↔ disease" relationship rather than a true drug-treats-disease relationship, which substantially weakens the pharmacological plausibility of this prediction.

Consequently, the reasoning linking methylcellulose to a direct therapeutic effect in osteoarthritis is speculative and should not be interpreted as evidence of intrinsic disease-modifying activity.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The evidence base is limited to preclinical formulation studies (Evidence Level L4) in which methylcellulose functions as an inert delivery vehicle rather than an active therapeutic agent, with no clinical trials and no mechanism-of-action data available. The prediction likely reflects a delivery-carrier association in the knowledge graph rather than a genuine pharmacological signal.
