# Ketamine: From Anaesthesia to Headache Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Ketamine is an NMDA-receptor antagonist established internationally as a general anaesthetic and, in its S-enantiomer form (esketamine), licensed for treatment-resistant depression. The TxGNN model predicts it may also be effective for **Headache Disorder**, with **41 clinical trials** and **20 publications** currently identified as related evidence, though only a minority directly and specifically test ketamine in headache populations.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Ketamine is an NMDA-receptor antagonist. Blocking NMDA receptors interrupts central sensitisation and the abnormal synaptic plasticity that underlies several difficult-to-treat headache disorders, including chronic migraine, cluster headache and trigeminal neuralgia. This gives a plausible pharmacological rationale for repurposing.

Currently, detailed original-indication data is not available in this evidence pack. Based on known pharmacology, ketamine's established use is as a general anaesthetic (and, as esketamine, for treatment-resistant depression), and its central NMDA-antagonist mechanism may mechanistically extend to refractory headache syndromes.

Low-dose ("sub-dissociative") ketamine has already been used off-label in emergency departments and headache clinics for acute migraine, tension-type headache, cluster headache and chronic daily headache, generally in small case series or single-centre trials. One completed Phase 3 RCT (the THINK trial, NCT03081416) and a retrospective cohort study (PMID 35356451) provide the most direct supporting evidence, but a large proportion of the identified trials/literature relate to ketamine's use in depression, sickle-cell pain or perioperative analgesia rather than headache disorder specifically — this is reflected in the many "C" or "pending" relevance grades in the evidence pack.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Only one completed Phase 3 RCT (THINK trial) and one retrospective cohort study directly and robustly support ketamine's use in headache disorder; the majority of identified trials/literature relate to other indications (depression, sickle-cell pain, perioperative analgesia) or remain unrated ("pending") for relevance. Combined with the absence of UK marketing authorisation and a Blocking safety data gap, the evidence is not yet sufficient to proceed.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- MHRA/SmPC-equivalent warnings, contraindications and controlled-drug handling requirements (currently a Blocking data gap)
- Confirmed DrugBank mechanism-of-action record to formally support the mechanistic rationale
- Full-text review and outcome data from the completed THINK (NCT03081416) and CHECK (NCT02657031) trials
- Completion and publication of ongoing Phase 3 trials (KetHead, NCT05306899) specifically in chronic headache populations
- A structured relevance re-grading of the "pending" trial/literature entries to separate headache-specific evidence from general pain/depression evidence
