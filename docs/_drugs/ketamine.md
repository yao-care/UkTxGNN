# Ketamine: From an Unspecified Original Indication to Headache Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Ketamine is an NMDA receptor antagonist with one oral solution authorisation recorded on the UK market. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **headache disorder**, with **9 clinical trials** and **19 publications** compiled for this direction. These include several small randomised trials, mostly in emergency department settings, and one Phase 3 trial that is still recruiting.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data for ketamine were not available in the data compiled for this report. The following rests on general pharmacology. Ketamine is an NMDA receptor antagonist. NMDA-mediated glutamatergic signalling contributes to central sensitisation, cortical spreading depression and trigeminovascular pain transmission. These processes are implicated in migraine, cluster headache and other refractory headache disorders.

Blocking NMDA receptors at sub-dissociative doses may reduce this sensitisation and produce an analgesic effect. This offers a plausible mechanistic link between ketamine's known pharmacology and headache disorders. The high TxGNN score is a computational prediction and supports prioritisation only. It is not proof of efficacy.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Several randomised and comparator trials in acute and chronic headache, a plausible NMDA-related mechanism and a high model score support further work. However, the trials are small, results are not available for the key Phase 3 studies, and the UK licence record, original indication and safety label data are incomplete.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Published results of THINK (NCT03081416) and KetHead (NCT05306899), including effect sizes and adverse events
- The authorised indication, dosage form and a verified MHRA licence record for the UK product
- Official warnings and contraindications from the SmPC and BNF, and a review of the knowledge-graph contraindications (especially cardiovascular, raised intracranial pressure and substance dependence) when screening patients
- Confirmed mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Assessment of route compatibility: the UK product is an oral solution, whereas the trials mainly used intranasal and intravenous routes
- A safety monitoring and controlled-substance governance plan for any off-label use in headache
