# Pregabalin: From Neuropathic Pain to Tendinitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Pregabalin is an α2δ calcium-channel modulator whose established literature base relates to central and peripheral neuropathic pain pathways, though formal original-indication and mechanism-of-action data are missing from this evidence pack. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Tendinitis**, but this is currently supported by **0 clinical trials** and only **6 publications**, most of which are indirect (postoperative pain adjuvant use, case reports, or preclinical work unrelated to pregabalin itself).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data for pregabalin is not available in this evidence pack (flagged as a High-severity data gap, DG002). Based on the information that is available, pregabalin's literature footprint centres on modulation of central and peripheral neuropathic pain pathways — this is the mechanistic frame used within the evidence pack itself when assessing the tendinitis prediction.

Tendinitis, however, is fundamentally a mechanical and inflammatory soft-tissue injury rather than a neuropathic pain condition. The evidence pack's own rationale for this candidate explicitly notes that the mechanistic link is **weak and indirect**: available literature largely concerns pregabalin's use as a perioperative opioid-sparing analgesic adjunct after tendon-adjacent surgery (e.g. rotator cuff repair), not as a treatment directed at tendon inflammation or repair itself. No studies in this pack test pregabalin as a primary therapy for tendinitis.

Given this, the high TxGNN similarity score should be interpreted as a knowledge-graph structural signal rather than a validated pharmacological rationale — it identifies a candidate worth flagging, not one with an established biological case.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
No clinical trials support pregabalin for tendinitis, and the available literature is indirect — largely perioperative opioid-sparing studies or unrelated case reports — with the evidence pack's own analysis rating the mechanistic link as weak. This does not meet the bar for further evaluation at this time.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- TFDA/MHRA product label data (warnings, contraindications) — currently a Blocking gap (DG001)
- Verified mechanism-of-action data — currently a High-severity gap (DG002)
- Disease-specific preclinical or clinical evidence directly testing pregabalin in tendinitis (rather than adjunctive perioperative pain control)
- Formal documentation of pregabalin's original licensed indication(s) for baseline comparison

**Note:** This evidence pack also contains other predicted indications for pregabalin with substantially stronger evidence — notably **migraine disorder** (Evidence Level L2, decision stage S2, "Research Question"), which is supported by completed RCTs, a Cochrane systematic review, and mechanistic/imaging studies on cortical spreading depression. If prioritising a candidate from this pack, migraine disorder is a considerably stronger starting point than tendinitis.
