## One-Sentence Summary

Entecavir is an oral guanosine nucleoside analogue that inhibits hepatitis B virus (HBV) polymerase. The TxGNN model predicts it may be relevant to **chronic hepatitis C virus infection**. The available evidence, **2 clinical trials** and **20 publications**, concerns patients co-infected with HBV and HCV and does not show that entecavir treats HCV itself.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Entecavir inhibits HBV polymerase. Detailed mechanism of action data is not otherwise available in the data compiled for this report.

There is no known direct activity against HCV. HCV is an RNA virus whose replication depends on a different polymerase (NS5B). The only plausible link is indirect. In patients infected with both HBV and HCV, entecavir suppresses HBV replication, including HBV reactivation that can occur when direct-acting antivirals (DAAs) clear HCV. This is management of the co-infecting HBV, not treatment of HCV. The model's high score therefore probably reflects the two infections occurring together and being studied together, not a demonstrated anti-HCV effect.

Because no cited study tests entecavir as a treatment for HCV itself, the evidence level is capped at L5, although the co-infection literature is relevant clinical context.

## Safety Considerations

- Acid suppressants: famotidine, ranitidine, cimetidine
- Antidiabetic: metformin
- Nephrotoxic or aminoglycoside-type antimicrobials: amphotericin B (including lipid and liposomal forms), kanamycin, neomycin, vancomycin, polymyxin B
- Other antibiotics: amoxicillin, levofloxacin

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
- Entecavir has no known direct activity against HCV.
- The two registered trials and the literature concern HBV reactivation prophylaxis in co-infected patients, not HCV treatment.
- HCV is now treated with direct-acting antivirals, so there is little rationale for entecavir as an HCV therapy.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Preclinical evidence of anti-HCV activity (for example replicon or cell-culture data)
- Clinical data in which HCV outcomes, such as sustained virological response, are measured with entecavir
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- If the aim is co-infection management, the question should be framed as HBV prophylaxis during DAA therapy, not as a new HCV indication
