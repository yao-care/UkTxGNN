## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data for Nepafenac is not available in the evidence pack (data gap DG002). However, the clinical trial evidence itself consistently describes Nepafenac as a topical ophthalmic non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) — for example, multiple trials directly compare it against other topical NSAIDs (diclofenac, ketorolac, bromfenac) for control of ocular inflammation, and describe its role in inhibiting prostaglandin-mediated (COX pathway) inflammatory processes in the eye (e.g. NCT03597867, which measured vitreous PGE2 levels after nepafenac administration).

The predicted new indication, "Eye Disease," is a broad category rather than a single novel condition. Given that the overwhelming majority of supporting trials already relate to established ocular inflammatory conditions — post-cataract surgery inflammation and pain, diabetic macular oedema, post-vitrectomy/retinal detachment inflammation, and post-intravitreal injection discomfort — this prediction largely reflects reinforcement of Nepafenac's known pharmacological class action (topical anti-inflammatory) rather than an entirely mechanistically novel repurposing signal.

Mechanistically, this is plausible: ocular inflammatory conditions across different anatomical sub-sites (anterior segment post-surgical inflammation, posterior segment macular oedema, vitreoretinal inflammation) share overlapping prostaglandin-driven inflammatory pathways, which a topical COX-inhibiting NSAID could plausibly address. This is consistent with the trial evidence pack, which shows active investigation of Nepafenac across several distinct ocular inflammatory presentations beyond the primary cataract-surgery indication.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Although the predicted indication (Eye Disease) is well supported by a large body of completed Phase 3 clinical trial evidence (L1), largely reflecting Nepafenac's established pharmacology as a topical ocular NSAID, two blocking issues prevent progression: (1) the drug currently holds **no UK marketing authorisation**, and (2) core safety data (warnings, contraindications) is a **recorded blocking gap**, meaning initial safety screening cannot be completed.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- SmPC-equivalent warnings and contraindications (DG001, Blocking) — required before any S1 safety assessment
- Confirmed mechanism of action documentation (DG002, High) from DrugBank or equivalent source
- Clarification of UK regulatory pathway, given current "Not Marketed" status and zero licenses
- PubMed literature search specific to the "Eye Disease" prediction, as none is currently indexed
- Clarification of what "Eye Disease" specifically denotes as a target indication, given its breadth relative to the existing cataract-surgery evidence base
