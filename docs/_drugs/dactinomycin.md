## Dactinomycin: From Ewing Sarcoma and Other Paediatric and Germ Cell Tumours to Relapsing-Remitting Multiple Sclerosis
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Dactinomycin is a cytotoxic antibiotic originally used for Ewing sarcoma, rhabdomyosarcoma, Wilms tumour, gestational trophoblastic neoplasm and testicular germ cell tumours.
The TxGNN model's top-ranked prediction is **relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis**, but there are **no clinical trials and no publications** supporting it. The better-supported predictions are rhabdomyosarcoma-family and soft tissue sarcoma sites, which largely fall within existing indications.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Dactinomycin is known to be a DNA-intercalating, transcription-inhibiting cytotoxic agent. Its efficacy in rhabdomyosarcoma, Ewing sarcoma and Wilms tumour is well established, mainly within multi-agent regimens such as VAC (vincristine, dactinomycin, cyclophosphamide).

**Relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis (rank 1):** No plausible immunomodulatory rationale is supported by the supplied data. Dactinomycin is strongly myelosuppressive and hepatotoxic. The very high score is a purely computational output and cannot be checked against a known mechanism.

**Rhabdomyosarcoma-family predictions (ranks 2–6):** These are biologically coherent because dactinomycin is a backbone agent in rhabdomyosarcoma. The predicted entities (vaginal botryoid-type, extrahepatic bile duct, parameningeal and prostate embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma) are anatomical or histological subtypes of an existing indication rather than genuinely new uses.

**Liver sarcoma, upper aerodigestive tract neoplasm and head and neck cancer (ranks 7–9):** The linked literature mainly concerns paediatric sarcomas (for example, undifferentiated embryonal sarcoma of the liver and head and neck rhabdomyosarcoma). The signal does not extend to epithelial carcinomas such as squamous cell carcinoma of the head and neck.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindication and warning signals (knowledge graph, not label text):** liver disease, gallbladder disease, thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, agranulocytosis, leukopenia, anaemia (including macrocytic and aplastic anaemia), varicella zoster infection and herpes zoster, hepatic vein thrombosis and Budd-Chiari syndrome.
- **Hepatotoxicity:** Age is a risk factor for hepatopathy with VAC regimens (PMID 15143082), which is relevant to the liver sarcoma prediction.
- **Drug interactions:** 100 interactions were identified. Those graded **Major** are deferiprone and samarium (153Sm) lexidronam (both likely to add to myelosuppression). Those graded **Moderate** include palifermin, strontium chloride Sr-89, fostamatinib, mercaptopurine, roflumilast and chloramphenicol. Levofloxacin is graded Minor. Several others (for example, ondansetron, granisetron, metoclopramide, dexamethasone, leucovorin and folic acid) are of unknown severity.

Label-based warnings and contraindications were not available. Please refer to the SmPC and BNF, and report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold** (relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis and the other L5 predictions). Rhabdomyosarcoma-family and sarcoma-site predictions are **Research Questions** at L3.

**Rationale:**
- The top-ranked prediction, multiple sclerosis, has no trials or publications and no plausible mechanism, and dactinomycin carries significant myelosuppressive and hepatotoxic risk. It should not be pursued on the current data.
- The L3 predictions mostly reflect existing rhabdomyosarcoma practice rather than true repurposing. The head and neck and upper aerodigestive tract signals come from sarcomas, not carcinomas, and should not be generalised.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data and a mechanistic assessment for any immune-mediated indication
- The MHRA SmPC indication text, warnings and contraindications to confirm which predicted sarcoma sites are already covered
- Dactinomycin-specific outcome data, as distinct from combination-regimen data, for the sarcoma subtypes
- A hepatic and haematological monitoring plan, especially for liver involvement and adult patients
- Confirmation of the licence record, including the PL number and dosage form
