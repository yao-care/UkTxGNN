# Temozolomide: From Anaplastic Astrocytoma and Glioblastoma to Adult Astrocytic Tumour

## One-Sentence Summary

Temozolomide is an oral alkylating chemotherapy agent, originally used to treat anaplastic astrocytoma, astrocytoma (excluding glioblastoma) and glioblastoma.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **adult astrocytic tumour**, supported by **2 clinical trials** and **20 publications**. However, this prediction largely overlaps with the drug's existing approved uses, so it confirms established use rather than identifying a new one.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data was not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, temozolomide is an oral alkylating agent that methylates DNA, notably at the O6 position of guanine. This produces cytotoxic lesions and leads to tumour cell death. Its activity is greatest in tumours with a methylated MGMT promoter, where DNA repair capacity is reduced.

Adult astrocytic tumours include anaplastic astrocytoma and glioblastoma, which are already the drug's established uses. The prediction is therefore consistent with the approved use rather than a true repurposing signal. The high TxGNN score reflects this overlap.

A second predicted indication, **cauda equina neoplasm** (most commonly myxopapillary ependymoma), has a plausible mechanistic link because temozolomide crosses the blood-brain barrier and is active in other CNS tumours. Its response depends on factors such as MGMT status, which are not established for these tumours. Clinical support is limited to a single case report, giving evidence level L4 and a recommendation of **Hold**.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
A completed Phase 3 randomised trial and multiple published RCTs support temozolomide in astrocytic tumours, giving evidence level L1. However, this largely confirms existing approved uses rather than a new indication. The second predicted indication, cauda equina neoplasm, rests on a single case report (L4) and should be held.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Confirmation of the licensed indications, dosage form and PL number from the MHRA register and SmPC, as these were missing from the compiled data
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Warnings and contraindications from the SmPC and BNF, confirming the knowledge graph findings
- A clear definition of what is new compared with the current licensed indications, since the prediction overlaps with them
- A haematological monitoring plan, given the myelosuppression-related conditions flagged
- For cauda equina neoplasm: prospective clinical evidence and MGMT status data before reconsidering
