# Clotrimazole: From Superficial Fungal Infections to Vulvovaginitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Clotrimazole is a topical azole antifungal, originally used for skin and mucosal fungal infections such as tinea, cutaneous candidiasis and vulvovaginal candidiasis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **vulvovaginitis**, the highest-ranked prediction with substantive evidence,
with **12 clinical trials** and **22 publications** currently supporting this direction. Because candidal vulvovaginitis is already an established use, this is closer to confirmation of an existing indication than true repurposing.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, clotrimazole is an azole antifungal that inhibits fungal lanosterol 14-alpha-demethylase and disrupts ergosterol synthesis, which impairs fungal cell membranes and inhibits growth.

Its efficacy in superficial candidiasis and dermatophyte infections is established. Candidal vulvovaginitis is caused by the same Candida species, so the mechanism applies directly. The prediction largely reflects an existing use rather than a new one.

Other predictions are less convincing. Acne has only a suspended trial of a triple-combination cream, so clotrimazole's contribution cannot be isolated. Postmenopausal atrophic vaginitis is driven by oestrogen deficiency, so there is no clear mechanistic link. Trichomonal vulvovaginitis is treated first-line with nitroimidazoles. Deep or hair-associated dermatophyte infections (tinea profunda, Majocchi granuloma, endothrix and ectothrix infections, scalp or beard dermatophytosis) usually need systemic therapy because topical agents penetrate poorly.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications (from disease-association data, not label text)**: Clotrimazole is linked to adrenocortical insufficiency, Addison disease, skin atrophy, telangiectasis, capillary disease, hereditary haemorrhagic telangiectasia, peripheral vascular disease, intermittent claudication and peripheral arterial disease. Treat these as signals to verify against the SmPC.
- **Drug Interactions**: The interaction screen returned 100 entries; the first 20 are summarised here. Major interactions were listed with fentanyl, alfentanil, hydrocodone and amiodarone. Moderate interactions include abemaciclib, paclitaxel, acalabrutinib, nifedipine, amlodipine, alfuzosin, aliskiren, apixaban, aprepitant, alprazolam, estradiol, levonorgestrel and norethisterone. Minor interactions include dolutegravir and amphotericin B. These are likely most relevant with systemic exposure, so check the full list.
- **Pregnancy**: First-trimester topical antifungal exposure has been studied in a large claims database (PMID 41350226). An in vitro placental study found that azole antifungals affect steroidogenic activity (PMID 40886781).

Please refer to the SmPC and BNF for full safety information. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails** (vulvovaginitis, candidal)

**Rationale:**
Phase 3 trials and several randomised studies support clotrimazole in candidal vulvovaginitis, and it is already an established use, so the evidence is strong. The other predictions, including acne, postmenopausal atrophic vaginitis, trichomonal vulvovaginitis and deep or hair-associated dermatophyte infections, should remain on **Hold** because evidence is weak or absent, or topical therapy is mechanistically inadequate. Superficial mycosis is a **Research Question** that overlaps heavily with existing indications.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The SmPC warnings and contraindications for the UK product, which were not available in the data compiled for this report
- The UK licence indication text and PL number
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Confirmation of the causative organism (Candida vs bacterial or trichomonal) before use
- Review of first-trimester safety data and awareness of azole-resistant Candida species
- Results from the ongoing NCT06835361 trial
