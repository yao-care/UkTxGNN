## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available for Clotrimazole in this evidence pack (flagged as a High-severity data gap). Based on known pharmacological class information referenced elsewhere in the evidence pack, Clotrimazole is a broad-spectrum imidazole antifungal that inhibits fungal CYP51 (14α-demethylase), disrupting ergosterol synthesis in the fungal cell membrane; its efficacy in dermatophyte and candidal infections is well established.

Acne, however, is primarily a disorder of sebaceous gland activity, follicular hyperkeratinisation, and *Cutibacterium acnes*-driven inflammation — it is not a fungal disease. The mechanistic link is therefore indirect: it applies mainly to "fungal acne" (*Malassezia folliculitis*), a yeast-driven condition frequently misdiagnosed clinically as acne, rather than to acne vulgaris itself.

The single relevant trial identified is a fixed-dose triple combination (beclometasone + gentamicin + clotrimazole), from which clotrimazole's individual contribution cannot be isolated, and the trial was suspended before completion. Taken together, the mechanistic rationale is plausible only in a specific misdiagnosed subgroup, and the clinical evidence base is insufficient to support this prediction at present.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanistic link between clotrimazole's antifungal activity and acne pathology is indirect and applies mainly to a misdiagnosed subgroup (fungal acne), and the only supporting trial is a suspended combination-product study from which clotrimazole's individual effect cannot be isolated. Evidence level (L4) is insufficient to support progression.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action (MOA) data for Clotrimazole (currently a data gap)
- UK/MHRA label warnings and contraindications (currently a data gap; Blocking severity)

- Clarification of UK regulatory/marketing status, since no licences are currently on record
