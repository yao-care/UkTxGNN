## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data for clotrimazole is not available in the data compiled for this report. As a class, azole antifungals inhibit fungal lanosterol 14-alpha-demethylase and disrupt ergosterol synthesis. Clotrimazole is also reported to have some antibacterial and anti-inflammatory activity.

A plausible link to acne would be a fungal or bacterial contribution to the condition, for example Malassezia-associated folliculitis (which can resemble acne) or *Cutibacterium acnes*. This remains speculative. The high TxGNN score is a model prediction only and does not show that clotrimazole works in acne.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction for acne rests on a high model score alone. The single related trial is a suspended study of a three-drug combination, and no publications address clotrimazole in acne. The evidence level is therefore L5.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Clinical or preclinical studies testing clotrimazole alone in acne, or in a defined subtype such as Malassezia folliculitis
- Detailed mechanism of action data to support the proposed link
- Label warnings and contraindications from the SmPC, and a review of the interaction profile for the intended population and formulation
- Confirmation that a suitable topical formulation is available for dermatological use
