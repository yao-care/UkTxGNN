## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Febuxostat is known to be a non-purine selective xanthine oxidase inhibitor, which reduces uric acid formation.

Renal hypouricaemia is characterised by already low serum urate, typically from loss-of-function variants in urate transporters such as URAT1 or GLUT9. Further urate lowering is therefore mechanistically counterintuitive, and could add risk (for example xanthinuria-like effects). The high TxGNN score most likely reflects network proximity to urate metabolism rather than a true therapeutic rationale.

The only mechanistic link in the literature is a single report proposing that non-purine xanthine oxidoreductase inhibitors such as febuxostat might help prevent exercise-induced acute kidney injury (EIAKI) in renal hypouricaemia. This is hypothesis-level and indirect. It does not show benefit for renal hypouricaemia itself.

- **Conditions of concern (relation-level data, not label text)**: Cerebrovascular disorders (stroke, cerebral infarction), myocardial infarction and coronary thrombosis, and haemoglobinopathy.
- **Disease-specific consideration**: Xanthine oxidase inhibition can cause hypoxanthine and xanthine accumulation, and in renal hypouricaemia, which already carries a risk of exercise-induced acute kidney injury, further urate lowering could be harmful.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanism runs against the disease biology, since renal hypouricaemia involves low urate to begin with. The evidence is at Level L4, limited to one report proposing febuxostat for exercise-induced acute kidney injury prevention, with no clinical study of benefit. The single registered trial concerns hyperuricaemia with calculi and has unverified relevance.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism-of-action data and a clear rationale for lowering urate further in a low-urate state
- Controlled clinical data, or at minimum a structured case series, on EIAKI prevention in renal hypouricaemia
- A safety assessment covering xanthine accumulation, renal risk and cardiovascular risk
- Full SmPC warnings and contraindications for review
