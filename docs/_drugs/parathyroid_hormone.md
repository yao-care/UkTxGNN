## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Parathyroid hormone is a calcium-regulating hormone, and its receptor (PTH1R) is shared with the related peptide PTHrP. A 2025 genetic study (PMID 40297711) links variants in the PTHrP receptor pathway to migraine susceptibility. This is the main reason the prediction is biologically plausible.

PTH1R signalling may plausibly be connected to CGRP-related vasodilation, since PTHrP and CGRP belong to related peptide signalling families. CGRP is a well-established migraine mediator. The remaining literature is indirect: it describes calcium, vitamin D, magnesium and bone status in people with migraine.

The link should be read cautiously. A genetic association with a receptor pathway does not show that giving PTH would help migraine. PTH agonism could plausibly aggravate vasodilation or cause hypercalcaemia-related symptoms, so benefit is not assured and harm is possible.

Other predicted indications (migraine with brainstem aura, amenorrhoea, Raynaud disease, oesophageal conditions, erectile dysfunction and others) have either no evidence or only comorbidity associations, and are not recommended for further pursuit at this stage.

## Safety Considerations

**Drug Interactions** (5 interactions recorded, of moderate or major severity):

- **Major:** Digoxin and digitoxin. Hypercalcaemia can increase sensitivity to cardiac glycosides and raise the risk of arrhythmia.
- **Moderate:** Calcitriol (topical), calcipotriol (topical) and alendronic acid.

**Additional considerations from the literature:** PTHrP, a related peptide, is secreted by some oesophageal tumours and is associated with humoral hypercalcaemia. This signals a potential safety concern and should be considered in any development plan. In migraine specifically, PTH agonism could plausibly worsen vasodilation or hypercalcaemia-related symptoms.

Please refer to the SmPC and BNF for full warnings and contraindications. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is high, but support is limited to a genetic association and indirect observational studies. There are no clinical trials, no evidence that PTH benefits migraine, and a plausible risk of harm through vasodilation or hypercalcaemia. This is a research question rather than an actionable repurposing candidate.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data and the licensed indication from the SmPC
- Safety information (warnings and contraindications) from the SmPC and BNF
- Preclinical studies testing whether PTH1R modulation (agonism or antagonism) affects migraine-relevant models, including CGRP pathways
- A clear hypothesis on the direction of effect, since genetic findings may point towards receptor antagonism rather than PTH administration
- Assessment of calcium-related risks and the interactions listed above before any clinical testing
