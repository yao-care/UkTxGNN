# Norethisterone: From Endometriosis and Menopausal Conditions to Amenorrhea

## One-Sentence Summary

Norethisterone is a synthetic progestin, originally used for conditions such as endometriosis, primary ovarian failure and postmenopausal osteoporosis (including in combination with estradiol as a transdermal patch).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **amenorrhea**, but **no clinical trials** are registered and the **20 publications** retrieved are mostly contraceptive reviews, so direct supporting evidence is weak.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general pharmacology, norethisterone is a synthetic progestin that acts as a progesterone receptor agonist. Progestins are established in the management of secondary amenorrhea, where a progestin withdrawal challenge induces endometrial shedding if the endometrium has had oestrogen priming. They are also used for cycle regulation.

This fits with the original indications. Endometriosis and primary ovarian failure both involve hormone-responsive endometrial and ovarian tissue, so a mechanistic link to menstrual disorders is biologically plausible.

However, the retrieved literature does not address amenorrhea treatment directly. Most papers are contraceptive reviews in which amenorrhea appears as a side effect or bleeding-pattern outcome rather than a treatment target. The high TxGNN score is a computational prediction and does not substitute for clinical evidence.

## Clinical Trial Evidence

Currently no related clinical trials registered.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications (suggested by knowledge-graph associations, not label text):** hypertension, epilepsy, porphyria, kidney disease, liver disease, gallbladder disease, diabetes mellitus and hyperglycaemia, hypertriglyceridaemia, hypothyroidism, systemic lupus erythematosus, asthma, hypercalcaemia, and female breast carcinoma. These must be verified against the SmPC.
- **Drug Interactions:** 159 interactions were identified. All 20 listed are of moderate severity. They are mainly with glucose-lowering agents (acarbose, metformin, dapagliflozin, linagliptin, nateglinide, GLP-1 agonists and insulins), plus aprepitant, clotrimazole, metreleptin and prucalopride.

Please refer to the SmPC and BNF for full safety information. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The TxGNN score is high and progestins have a plausible role in secondary amenorrhea. However, there are no registered trials, and the literature concerns contraception rather than treatment of amenorrhea, so the evidence level is only L4.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data to support the mechanistic reasoning
- Clinical studies or guideline evidence directly assessing norethisterone for amenorrhea
- UK licence details (indication, dosage form and a verified PL number) and SmPC safety information
- Assessment of route compatibility, since the UK authorisation is a transdermal oestrogen-progestin patch
- Review of the knowledge-graph contraindications, particularly cardiometabolic and hepatic conditions, against the SmPC
