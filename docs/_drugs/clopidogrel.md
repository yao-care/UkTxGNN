## One-Sentence Summary

Clopidogrel is an oral antiplatelet medicine that blocks the P2Y12 receptor on platelets. The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **migraine with brainstem aura**, with a prediction score of 0.9944. The data compiled for this report contain **no clinical trials** and **15 publications** for this specific subtype. The publications mostly concern migraine with aura in people with a patent foramen ovale (PFO), so the evidence is indirect.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Clopidogrel is known as a P2Y12 inhibitor. By reducing platelet activation, it may limit microembolic and platelet-derived (for example serotonin-mediated) triggers of migraine aura. This has been proposed particularly for people with a PFO.

The published work looks at migraine with aura and PFO more broadly, including a pilot randomised trial of clopidogrel as migraine prophylaxis, retrospective reviews and case series. None of it studies the brainstem aura subtype specifically. Any benefit for this subtype is therefore an extrapolation from the wider migraine-with-aura population.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindication-type relations (not label text):** the data compiled for this report list acute liver failure, hepatitis, blood coagulation disease, injury, thrombocytopenic purpura, active peptic ulcer disease and intracranial haemorrhage as conditions where clopidogrel is generally unsuitable. These should be checked against the SmPC.
- **Drug interactions (100 recorded; examples):**
  - Major: omeprazole, esomeprazole, rabeprazole, pioglitazone, loperamide, repaglinide
  - Moderate: acetylsalicylic acid, morphine, clarithromycin, cimetidine, lansoprazole, pantoprazole, bupropion

Please refer to the SmPC and BNF for full safety information. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.
