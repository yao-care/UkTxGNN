## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Formal mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank is currently unavailable for this drug (flagged as a High-severity data gap, remediation pending via the DrugBank API). However, the evidence pack's mechanistic assessment describes tizanidine as a **centrally acting α2-adrenergic receptor agonist**, in the same pharmacological class as clonidine. It is understood to inhibit noradrenaline release from the locus coeruleus and to reduce excitatory neurotransmitter release from spinal interneurons.

This pathway overlaps mechanistically with the central sensitisation and trigeminovascular system activation processes implicated in migraine pathophysiology, providing a theoretical rationale for a preventive role in migraine attacks. Tizanidine's established muscle-relaxant properties may also be relevant to migraine subtypes with a significant myofascial/tension component.

This mechanistic plausibility is reinforced by a body of older literature (dating back to the 1990s–2000s) exploring tizanidine in chronic daily headache and tension-type headache, together with a currently recruiting Phase 3 RCT (NCT05484349) designed specifically to test tizanidine for migraine prevention in adults — the most direct evidence source identified to date.
