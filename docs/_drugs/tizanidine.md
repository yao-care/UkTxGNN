## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, tizanidine is a central alpha-2 adrenergic agonist. It may reduce trigeminal nociceptive transmission and central sensitisation, and may lower the muscle tension that contributes to headache.

This makes a role in migraine prevention plausible, particularly for chronic daily headache with a muscle-tension component. The link is inferred from known pharmacology rather than from mechanism data in the compiled record.

The strongest supporting study is a double-blind, placebo-controlled multicentre trial of tizanidine as adjunctive prophylaxis in chronic daily headache (PMID 12167135). Most patients in that trial had migraine. It did not enrol a purely migraine population, so the evidence is indirect for migraine alone.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: Interaction screening returned 100 records. Major-level interactions include famotidine, cimetidine, morphine, dolasetron and obeticholic acid. Moderate-level interactions include hyoscyamine, loperamide, atropine, clarithromycin, levofloxacin, metoclopramide, naltrexone and several laxatives and antiemetics.

Label warnings and contraindications were not available in the data compiled for this report. Please refer to the SmPC and BNF for full safety information. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
One placebo-controlled randomised trial in chronic daily headache, in which most patients had migraine, supports an effect on headache prophylaxis (evidence level L2). The Phase 3 trial specific to migraine has no results and an unknown status, so efficacy in migraine is not yet confirmed.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Results from NCT05484349, or confirmation of its current status
- Mechanism-of-action data to support the inferred pharmacological link
- Label warnings and contraindications from the SmPC and BNF, including interaction management with the major-level interacting drugs
- A defined prophylaxis population (episodic versus chronic migraine) and a monitoring plan
