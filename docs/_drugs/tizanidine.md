## Tizanidine: From Spasticity to Migraine Disorder
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Tizanidine is a centrally acting alpha-2 adrenergic agonist, marketed in the UK as a 2 mg tablet. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **migraine disorder**, with **1 directly relevant Phase 3 trial** (status unknown, no results) and **a small number of older clinical studies on chronic daily headache** currently supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, tizanidine is a central alpha-2 adrenergic agonist. By this action it may reduce trigeminal nociceptive transmission and central sensitisation, and it may also lower muscle tension that contributes to headache.

The original indication is not recorded in the compiled UK licence data, so a direct comparison with the new indication cannot be made. Tizanidine is, however, a recognised muscle-tone agent. The literature has examined it as a preventive option in chronic daily headache, chronic migraine and tension-type headache, particularly where a muscle-tension component is present.

The prediction is therefore mechanistically plausible, mainly for **chronic daily headache and migraine prophylaxis**. The broader group of predicted migraine-related entities (brainstem aura) comes from the TxGNN model (deep learning) and may reflect similarities the model learned between related diseases, not biological evidence; it carries no specific evidence of its own.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications (TxGNN model associations, not label text):** The TxGNN model (deep learning) links tizanidine with liver failure, kidney disease, pyoureter, psychotic disorders (including schizophreniform and schizoaffective disorder), mental disorder and hypotensive disorder. These may reflect similarities the model learned between related diseases, not biological evidence, and should be verified against the SmPC.
- **Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions were identified. Those graded **Major** include:
  - Famotidine
  - Cimetidine
  - Morphine
  - Dolasetron
  - Obeticholic acid

  Moderate interactions include clarithromycin, levofloxacin, metoclopramide, atropine, hyoscyamine, dicyclomine, loperamide, dronabinol, nabilone and naltrexone, among others.
- **Additional consideration:** Sedation is a known adverse effect of tizanidine and may matter in a migraine population using other CNS-active drugs.

Please refer to the SmPC and BNF for full warnings and contraindications. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
A placebo-controlled RCT and an open-label study support tizanidine in chronic daily headache, and a directly relevant Phase 3 migraine prevention trial exists. However, the trial status is unknown with no results, the supporting studies are old and of modest quality, and the original indication and mechanism data are missing. Other predicted indications (brainstem aura, genetic susceptibility, Tourette syndrome, trichotillomania, dysthymia, rare skin disorders) are on Hold, and fibromyalgia is a research question only.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Status update and results from NCT05484349
- UK licence details (approved indication, dosage form, MHRA PL number) and SmPC warnings and contraindications
- Mechanism of action data
- A safety plan covering sedation, hypotension, liver function monitoring and the major interactions (notably CYP1A2 inhibitors such as cimetidine)
- Confirmation of the appropriate evidence grading (L1 versus a lower level)
