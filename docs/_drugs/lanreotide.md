## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available for this evidence pack (data gap). Based on known clinical information, Lanreotide is a somatostatin analogue whose efficacy in acromegaly and neuroendocrine tumours is established through inhibition of the growth hormone (GH)/IGF-1 axis and somatostatin receptor-mediated suppression of hormone secretion.

Hypertrichosis (excessive hair growth) is primarily driven by androgenic and follicular growth-regulatory pathways. The connection to the GH/IGF-1 axis is, at best, indirect and theoretical, and there is no clinical or preclinical evidence in this pack linking somatostatin receptor activity to hair follicle regulation. Given the absence of confirmed MOA data and the complete lack of supporting trials or literature, this candidate should be treated as a possible knowledge-graph artefact rather than a credible repurposing signal.

Notably, four further TxGNN candidates for this drug (malformation syndrome with dental/periodontal features, Dandy-Walker malformation syndrome, isolated genetic hair shaft abnormality, and Ambras-type congenital hypertrichosis) were also generated with similarly high scores (~99.96%) but represent congenital/structural developmental disorders with no known relationship to somatostatin signalling. This pattern — high confidence scores clustered around rare, mechanistically unrelated genetic conditions — is consistent with low-specificity prediction noise on sparsely connected disease ontology nodes, rather than a genuine pharmacological signal.
