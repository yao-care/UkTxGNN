# Cimetidine: From Peptic Ulcer Disease to Smouldering Systemic Mastocytosis

## One-Sentence Summary

Cimetidine is an H2-receptor antagonist originally used to treat gastric and duodenal ulcers, reflux disease and gastric hypersecretory states.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **Smouldering Systemic Mastocytosis** (top-ranked prediction), but **no clinical trials and no publications** specific to this indication were found in the data compiled for this report. The prediction rests on model score alone.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Cimetidine is known to be a competitive histamine H2-receptor antagonist that reduces gastric acid secretion, and its efficacy in acid-related disease is well established.

Systemic mastocytosis is already listed among cimetidine's original indications in the knowledge graph. In mast cell disorders, excess histamine drives gastric hypersecretion, and H2 blockade can control these symptoms. Smouldering systemic mastocytosis is a related subtype, so the high score likely reflects this shared link.

Any benefit would be **symptomatic only** (acid-related symptoms), not disease-modifying. Because the drug is already linked to mastocytosis, this is better viewed as an extension within an existing indication family than as a new therapeutic direction.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindication-related disease associations (knowledge graph, not label text):** liver disease, gallbladder disease, impaired renal function, and gastric cancer or neoplasm (including diffuse gastric adenocarcinoma, linitis plastica and gastric carcinoma). Acid suppression may mask symptoms of gastric malignancy.
- **Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions were recorded. Examples of moderate-level interactions include paclitaxel, fentanyl, acalabrutinib, lidocaine, acebutolol, dihydrocodeine, codeine, tramadol, nifedipine and alfentanil. Minor-level examples include dolutegravir, zidovudine, paracetamol, ibuprofen, ketorolac and ethinylestradiol. Cimetidine's broad CYP inhibition is a particular concern for long-term use.
- **Case reports:** Several reports describe peptic ulcer perforation occurring during cimetidine therapy, and one describes perforation after withdrawal.

Please refer to the SmPC and BNF for full safety information. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction for smouldering systemic mastocytosis is supported only by the model score (L5), with no trials or literature. Any benefit would be symptomatic rather than disease-modifying, and the high score likely reflects cimetidine's existing link to mastocytosis. Most other top predictions are either original indications or lack direct evidence.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**

- Mechanism-of-action data (for example from DrugBank)
- Warnings, contraindications and approved indication text from the current MHRA product information and BNF
- A review of interaction risks for patients on concomitant mast cell therapies or other CYP-metabolised drugs
- Clarification of whether the use falls within existing mastocytosis labelling or represents a genuinely new indication
