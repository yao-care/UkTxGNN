# Amikacin: From Bacterial Pneumonia to Paratyphoid Fever

## One-Sentence Summary

Amikacin is an aminoglycoside antibiotic, originally used for serious Gram-negative bacterial infections such as bacterial pneumonia, *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* infection and bacterial meningitis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **paratyphoid fever**, but there are **no registered clinical trials** and only **8 publications** (mostly surveillance, antibiogram and case-report data) supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, amikacin is an aminoglycoside that inhibits bacterial protein synthesis by binding the 30S ribosomal subunit. It has proven activity against many Gram-negative organisms, and *Salmonella enterica* (the cause of paratyphoid fever) is susceptible to it in vitro. This makes the prediction biologically plausible.

However, the plausibility is limited. Aminoglycosides penetrate cells poorly, and *Salmonella* is an intracellular pathogen, so clinical efficacy in enteric fever is doubtful. Guideline-preferred agents (azithromycin, third-generation cephalosporins, and carbapenems for extensively drug-resistant strains) are used instead. The very high TxGNN score appears to reflect Gram-negative antibacterial class similarity rather than independent clinical support.

Other predictions for this drug (typhoid fever, salmonellosis, meningococcemia, epiglottitis) are similar in nature, with indirect or in vitro support only. Several others (diffuse scleroderma, hyperamylasemia, polyclonal hyperviscosity syndrome, congenital analbuminemia, punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis) have no plausible mechanistic link and are likely graph-prediction artefacts.

## Safety Considerations

- **Disease associations flagged in the knowledge graph (not label text)**: kidney disease, pyoureter, hypercalcaemia, nephrocalcinosis, phosphorus metabolism disease, central nervous system origin vertigo, myasthenia gravis (including adult-onset), neuromuscular junction disease, parkinsonian disorders and Parkinson disease. These are consistent with the known nephrotoxic, ototoxic and neuromuscular-blocking risks of aminoglycosides.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were identified. Those graded **Major** are:

  - Mannitol

  Moderate interactions include other aminoglycosides (kanamycin, neomycin), amphotericin B formulations, proton pump inhibitors (omeprazole, esomeprazole, lansoprazole, pantoprazole, rabeprazole, dexlansoprazole), aminosalicylates (mesalazine, balsalazide, olsalazine), acetylsalicylic acid and exenatide.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The predicted indication has no clinical trials, and the literature is limited to surveillance, in vitro and case-level data. The one small comparative report showed poor clinical response with amikacin. Poor intracellular penetration and well-established alternatives make repurposing unlikely to add value, apart from possibly rare multidrug-resistant cases.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Prospective clinical data on amikacin for paratyphoid or enteric fever, especially in multidrug- or extensively drug-resistant cases
- Mechanism of action data and intracellular activity data against *Salmonella*
- The approved indication text and full safety information from the SmPC, so that safety screening can proceed
- A renal, auditory and neuromuscular safety monitoring plan
