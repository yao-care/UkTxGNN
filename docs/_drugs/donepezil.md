## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, donepezil is a reversible acetylcholinesterase inhibitor that raises synaptic acetylcholine. Its efficacy in Alzheimer's disease rests on this cholinergic enhancement.

Striatal cholinergic dysfunction has been proposed as a contributor to tics and attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD). In mouse models, donepezil reduced the head-twitch response induced by DOI, a behaviour used as a model of tic symptoms in Tourette syndrome. This suggests cholinergic and serotonergic modulation of motor circuits. Small human reports in children and adolescents with tics and ADHD point in the same direction.

This rationale is plausible but preliminary. Alzheimer's disease and tic disorders are very different conditions, and the human data are limited to small, likely open-label or case-level reports. The study designs could not be confirmed from the titles and abstracts available.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications and conditions of concern (knowledge graph, not label text)**: epilepsy, asthma (including intrinsic and allergic asthma), bladder neck obstruction, active peptic ulcer disease, sick sinus syndrome (including the familial form) and atrioventricular block.
- **Drug Interactions** (100 interactions recorded; examples shown):
  - **Major**: bupropion.
  - **Moderate**: anticholinergic or antimuscarinic agents (atropine, hyoscyamine, glycopyrronium, clidinium, dicyclomine); H2 blockers (famotidine, ranitidine, cimetidine); loperamide; dexamethasone; metronidazole; dolasetron; picosulfuric acid; polyethylene glycol with electrolytes.
  - **Minor**: aprepitant, acetylsalicylic acid, lorcaserin, clarithromycin.
- **Indication-specific concerns**: cholinesterase inhibitors have been reported to induce or worsen movement disorders (for example Pisa syndrome and jaw tremor) and may aggravate peptic ulcer disease. These signals are relevant when considering use in movement disorders.

Please refer to the SmPC and BNF for full safety information. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold (research question only)**

**Rationale:**
- Chronic tic disorder has a high model score and a plausible cholinergic rationale, but human evidence is limited to small open-label or case-level reports and preclinical work, with no registered trials. The other predicted indications (for example psychogenic movement disorders, primary orthostatic tremor and benign shuddering attacks) have no supporting evidence, and the evidence for extrapyramidal and tardive movement disorders is mixed, with a safety signal.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Confirmation of the study designs and outcomes of the paediatric tic studies (PMID 18343255 in particular).
- A randomised, placebo-controlled trial in tic disorders, with ADHD comorbidity considered.
- Mechanism of action data and the UK approved indication text for the licence.
- A review of the safety signals for movement disorders and cardiac conduction, particularly in children.
- Confirmation of the UK licence details, including product format and MHRA licence number.
