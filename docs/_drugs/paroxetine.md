# Paroxetine: From Depression and Anxiety Disorders to Ohdo Syndrome and Variants

## One-Sentence Summary

Paroxetine is a selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor (SSRI), originally used for depressive and anxiety-related disorders.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Ohdo syndrome and variants**,
but there are currently **no registered clinical trials** and **no publications** supporting this direction. The prediction is based on the computational model alone.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not currently available. Paroxetine is known to be an SSRI, and its efficacy in depression and anxiety-related disorders is established. It is also reported to inhibit GRK2, although this is not confirmed by the data compiled for this report.

Ohdo syndrome and its variants (for example, Say-Barber-Biesecker-Young-Simpson syndrome) are rare genetic conditions linked to KAT6B variants. They typically involve intellectual disability and craniofacial or developmental features. No link between paroxetine's pharmacology and KAT6B-related chromatin regulation, or the disease phenotype, is supported by the available data.

The very high TxGNN score (0.991) reflects a pattern in a knowledge graph and is not evidence of efficacy. At most, paroxetine might offer symptomatic benefit for comorbid anxiety or depression. That would be an extension of its existing use, not treatment of the underlying disease.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests on a model score alone (L5), with no clinical trials, no literature and no supported mechanistic link between paroxetine and Ohdo syndrome. Safety data from the product label are also incomplete.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data, and a plausible biological link to KAT6B-related pathology
- Preclinical or case-based evidence in Ohdo syndrome and variants
- Confirmation of MHRA licence details (PL number, dosage form, licensed indications)
- Label-based warnings and contraindications from the SmPC and BNF, to allow a proper safety screen
- Assessment of route compatibility and suitability for the target (largely paediatric) population
