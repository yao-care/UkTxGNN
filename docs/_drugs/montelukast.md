## Montelukast: Bronchitis
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Montelukast is an oral cysteinyl leukotriene receptor antagonist that is already marketed in the UK. The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **bronchitis**. The data compiled for this report include **16 clinical trials** and **20 publications**. Most of these concern bronchiolitis, bronchiolitis obliterans or eosinophilic bronchitis rather than common acute bronchitis. Overall evidence is at **L2**, and results to date are mixed.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Montelukast is known as a CysLT1 receptor antagonist. Blocking this receptor may reduce leukotriene-driven airway inflammation, bronchoconstriction and mucus secretion.

These processes are also thought to play a part in viral bronchiolitis and post-bronchiolitis wheeze. In bronchiolitis obliterans syndrome after lung or stem-cell transplantation, leukotriene-driven inflammation and fibrosis may be modulated as well. Eosinophilic airway inflammation, which causes cough in non-asthmatic eosinophilic bronchitis, is a further plausible target.

Trial results are mixed, and benefit has not been established in any of these settings.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: The data record 100 potential interactions. Those classed as moderate are dexamethasone, metronidazole, miconazole, nateglinide, nitisinone and troglitazone. Many others, including pantoprazole, omeprazole, lansoprazole, metformin, glimepiride, morphine and doxycycline, are listed with an unknown severity level.

No key warnings or contraindications were available in the data compiled for this report. Please refer to the SmPC and BNF for full safety information. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Evidence is at L2, from small controlled trials in non-asthmatic eosinophilic bronchitis and recurrent obstructive bronchitis. The larger trials and systematic reviews address bronchiolitis, which is a different condition, and their results are mixed. A high model score alone does not justify a change in practice.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Larger placebo-controlled trials in the specific bronchitis population of interest, with clear cough and symptom endpoints
- Review of the published outcomes of the completed bronchiolitis trials, including the large Phase 3 trial, to judge whether the signal is consistent
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data
- Safety and interaction review against the current SmPC and BNF, particularly for paediatric use
