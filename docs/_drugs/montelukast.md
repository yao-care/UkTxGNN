## One-Sentence Summary

Montelukast is a cysteinyl leukotriene receptor antagonist, originally used for allergic rhinitis and asthma.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **bronchitis** (in the data, mainly bronchiolitis and bronchiolitis obliterans syndrome), with **17 clinical trials** and **19 publications** currently supporting this direction. Results are mixed and benefit is not established.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, montelukast is a selective CysLT1 receptor antagonist. Its efficacy in asthma and allergic rhinitis is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to bronchitis-type airway disease.

Cysteinyl leukotrienes are released during viral airway infection, such as RSV bronchiolitis. They promote airway inflammation, bronchoconstriction and mucus secretion. Blocking CysLT1 could therefore reduce these effects in bronchiolitis and post-bronchiolitis wheeze. In bronchiolitis obliterans syndrome after transplant, leukotriene-driven inflammation and fibrosis may also be modulated.

The link between asthma and bronchitis is plausible, but trial results are mixed. The Cochrane review of leukotriene inhibitors for bronchiolitis in infants (PMID 25773054) and a systematic review of post-bronchiolitis wheezing (PMID 24118637) are in the evidence set; their conclusions should be read in full before any decision.

## Safety Considerations

- **Neuropsychiatric events:** The literature compiled for this report includes systematic reviews and cohort studies on neuropsychiatric adverse events with montelukast, particularly in children. The US FDA added a boxed warning in 2020. Monitor patients and carers for mood, sleep and behavioural changes.
- **Knowledge-graph contraindication signals** (not label text): anxiety disorder, neurotic disorder, dysthymic disorder, anxiety, hypereosinophilic syndrome and cutaneous vasculitis.
- **Drug interactions** (100 recorded; examples only):
  - Moderate: dexamethasone, metronidazole, miconazole, nateglinide, nitisinone, troglitazone.
  - Severity unknown: calcitriol, phentermine, pantoprazole, glimepiride, mesalazine, doxycycline, clotrimazole, acarbose, morphine, metformin, omeprazole, rosiglitazone, lansoprazole, sucralfate.

Please refer to the SmPC and BNF for full safety information. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Only one large Phase 3 trial exists, and the supporting evidence is a mix of small, single-arm, combination-therapy and infant-specific studies. The mechanistic rationale is plausible but benefit is not established. "Bronchitis" also covers different conditions (bronchiolitis, bronchiolitis obliterans, eosinophilic bronchitis) that do not share one evidence base. Safety data from the label are missing, and the neuropsychiatric signal in children needs careful weighing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Review the results of NCT00076973, the Cochrane review (PMID 25773054) and the other systematic review (PMID 24118637) to confirm whether efficacy was shown.
- Define the target population precisely (for example, post-transplant bronchiolitis obliterans syndrome versus infant bronchiolitis).
- Obtain the SmPC warnings and contraindications and the MHRA indication text.
- Obtain detailed mechanism of action data.
- Develop a neuropsychiatric safety monitoring plan, especially for children.
