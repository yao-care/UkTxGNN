## Montelukast: Bronchitis
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Montelukast is an oral cysteinyl leukotriene receptor (CysLT1) antagonist. The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **bronchitis**. The data compiled for this report include **17 registered trials** and **20 publications** retrieved for this prediction. Only two small trials study bronchitis itself (non-asthmatic eosinophilic bronchitis and recurrent obstructive bronchitis). The rest address related conditions such as bronchiolitis, so the evidence level is **L2**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Montelukast is known to be a leukotriene receptor antagonist. It blocks CysLT1, the receptor through which cysteinyl leukotrienes act. These lipid mediators promote airway inflammation, bronchoconstriction, mucus secretion and eosinophil recruitment.

Airway inflammation driven by eosinophils and leukotrienes is also a feature of some forms of bronchitis. Two small controlled studies tested this idea:
- Add-on montelukast to inhaled budesonide in non-asthmatic eosinophilic bronchitis (NCT01121016, with a related publication, PMID 25563311).
- Montelukast in children with recurrent obstructive bronchitis (NCT04613180).

Most other studies in the data are in bronchiolitis, a different condition, and their results are mixed. They give only indirect support. The mechanistic rationale is plausible, but clinical benefit in bronchitis is not established.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanistic rationale is plausible, and two small controlled trials in bronchitis subtypes (evidence level L2) are the only direct evidence. Their results are not available in the data compiled, and the larger bronchiolitis trials are in a different condition with mixed findings. The very high TxGNN score (0.9995) is a computational prediction and does not replace clinical evidence.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Published results of NCT01121016 and NCT04613180, both with status "Unknown", and confirmation of their primary outcomes
- A larger, adequately powered randomised trial in a defined bronchitis population, with clear cough and symptom endpoints
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Review of the current SmPC warnings and contraindications for the intended population, particularly children
- Review of the recorded drug interactions against likely co-medications
