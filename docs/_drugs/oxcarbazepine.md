## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, oxcarbazepine is a keto-analogue of carbamazepine. Its active metabolite, the monohydroxy derivative (MHD), blocks voltage-gated sodium channels. Its efficacy in partial-onset seizures is established.

Visual (occipital or photosensitive) epilepsy is a focal or reflex seizure type. A sodium-channel mechanism is therefore plausible. However, the retrieved literature consists of general antiseizure-drug reviews and guidelines with no visual-epilepsy-specific data. Efficacy in this subtype is inferred from the parent indication and has not been shown directly.

## Safety Considerations

- **Knowledge-graph contraindication signals** (not label text): anxiety disorder, neurotic disorder, dysthymic disorder, impaired renal function, inappropriate ADH syndrome, and nephrogenic syndrome of inappropriate antidiuresis. These should be verified against the SmPC. The hyponatraemia-related signals are consistent with the known sodium-lowering effect of the drug.
- **Drug Interactions** (100 interactions recorded; all 20 shown in the data were moderate-level): examples include hydrocortisone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, triamcinolone, omeprazole, clarithromycin, morphine, aprepitant, pioglitazone, saxagliptin, linagliptin, cholecalciferol, calcitriol and dronabinol.
- **Seizure aggravation:** a case report describes oxcarbazepine-induced myoclonic status epilepticus in juvenile myoclonic epilepsy. Sodium-channel blockers can worsen some generalised epilepsy syndromes.

Please refer to the SmPC and BNF for full safety information. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.
