# Oxcarbazepine: From Epilepsy to Visual Epilepsy

## One-Sentence Summary

Oxcarbazepine is an established antiepileptic drug (the keto-analogue of carbamazepine) used for partial-onset and generalised tonic-clonic seizures. The TxGNN model predicts it may also be effective for **Visual Epilepsy**, a photosensitive/reflex epilepsy subtype, with **1 clinical trial** and **20 publications** currently identified as related evidence — though none of these studies specifically investigates visual/photosensitive epilepsy as a distinct entity.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in this evidence pack. Based on the supporting literature, Oxcarbazepine is a sodium-channel-blocking anticonvulsant, structurally derived from carbamazepine, which limits high-frequency neuronal firing and stabilises hyperexcitable membranes — a mechanism directly relevant to seizure suppression regardless of trigger type.

Visual epilepsy (photosensitive/reflex epilepsy) is not a separate disease from epilepsy in the pharmacological sense — it is a seizure-triggering phenotype that sits within the same broad epilepsy spectrum that oxcarbazepine is already used to treat. This is therefore less a "new indication" in the traditional repurposing sense and more a plausible extension of an existing, well-established use into a specific epilepsy subtype.

However, it is important to note that none of the identified trials or literature specifically studies visual/photosensitive epilepsy; the evidence base consists of general antiepileptic drug (AED) literature and broad-spectrum efficacy trials. The mechanistic rationale is sound, but disease-specific clinical confirmation is currently absent.
