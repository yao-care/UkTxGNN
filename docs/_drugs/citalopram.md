## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data for citalopram is not available in the data compiled for this report. Citalopram belongs to the SSRI class, whose mechanism is well characterised: it blocks serotonin reuptake and so increases serotonergic signalling.

Serotonergic dysfunction in cortico-striato-thalamo-cortical circuits is implicated in obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD), and SSRIs are an established first-line pharmacological class for it. This class-level rationale is consistent with the very high TxGNN score.

The direct citalopram evidence is modest. It consists of open-label paediatric and adult studies, a treatment-resistant case series and a small single-blind comparison with other SSRIs. No citalopram-specific placebo-controlled Phase 3 RCT is present in the supplied data.

## Safety Considerations

**Contraindication-type associations recorded for citalopram** (not taken from label text; check against the SmPC): long QT syndrome (including familial), torsades de pointes, epilepsy, bipolar disorder, major affective disorder, liver disease, gallbladder disease, potassium deficiency, blood coagulation disease, heart disease, cardiovascular disease, neuroleptic malignant syndrome, inappropriate ADH syndrome (including nephrogenic syndrome of inappropriate antidiuresis) and serotonin syndrome.

**Drug Interactions** (100 interactions were found; examples of Major-level ones are listed):
- Bupropion
- Omeprazole
- Cimetidine
- Clarithromycin
- Cisapride
- Dolasetron
- Lorcaserin
- Dexfenfluramine
- Diethylpropion

Moderate-level examples include famotidine, loperamide, morphine and acetylsalicylic acid. Several of the major interactions involve QT prolongation, serotonergic effects or CYP2C19 inhibition, which should be checked in the SmPC and BNF.

Please refer to the SmPC and BNF for full safety information. Report suspected adverse reactions via the Yellow Card Scheme.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
SSRIs are an established drug class for OCD, and the citalopram-specific evidence (one small single-blind comparison and several open-label studies) is supportive but limited, giving an evidence level of L2. The Phase 3 trial found is a paediatric add-on psychotherapy study, not a citalopram-specific trial, and the safety profile (especially QT prolongation risk) needs careful attention.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A placebo-controlled or active-comparator RCT of citalopram in OCD
- Confirmation of the licensed indications and safety wording from the current SmPC and BNF
- An ECG and electrolyte monitoring plan, and a review of interacting medicines, especially for patients with cardiac or hepatic conditions
- Detailed mechanism of action data
