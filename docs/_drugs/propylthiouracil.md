## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available for propylthiouracil in this evidence pack. Based on known pharmacology, propylthiouracil is a thionamide-class antithyroid agent that inhibits thyroid peroxidase, thereby blocking thyroid hormone synthesis, and additionally inhibits peripheral conversion of T4 to T3. Its efficacy in hyperthyroidism and Graves' disease is well established clinically, as reflected repeatedly in the supporting literature (e.g. PMID 31345521, PMID 25185644).

Resistance to Thyroid Hormone (RTH) due to TRβ mutations is a related but distinct endocrine disorder: patients present with elevated circulating thyroid hormone (T3/T4) but reduced tissue responsiveness due to a defective receptor, rather than true glandular overproduction. Because the biochemical presentation (raised thyroid hormone levels, variably suppressed TSH) can mimic thyrotoxicosis, patients with RTH have on occasion been misdiagnosed and empirically treated with antithyroid drugs, including propylthiouracil — this is explicitly documented in the evidence pack (PMID 10724359: a patient with RTH "was previously treated with propylthiouracil based on the diagnosis of thyrotoxicosis"; PMID 3097618: RTH patients "treated with propylthiouracil...for suspected thyrotoxicosis").

This shared biochemical axis — thyroid hormone synthesis and receptor-mediated signalling — provides a plausible, though indirect, mechanistic rationale for the TxGNN prediction. However, the existing literature describes propylthiouracil use in RTH primarily as a historical misdiagnosis/mistreatment scenario rather than as an intended therapeutic strategy, which should temper enthusiasm for this repurposing candidate.
