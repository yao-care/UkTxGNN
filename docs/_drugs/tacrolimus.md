## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed formal mechanism-of-action documentation was not supplied in this Evidence Pack (original_moa: Data Gap). However, the evidence pack's own repurposing rationale confirms that tacrolimus acts by inhibiting the calcineurin/NFAT pathway, thereby suppressing antigen-specific T-cell activation and downstream release of pro-inflammatory cytokines. This is the same mechanism that underlies its established efficacy in atopic dermatitis.

Seborrheic dermatitis and atopic dermatitis are both chronic inflammatory skin conditions. Seborrheic dermatitis pathology involves *Malassezia*-triggered local immune inflammation and T-cell activation — a process mechanistically analogous to the T-cell-driven inflammation seen in atopic dermatitis. Because tacrolimus dampens this shared inflammatory cascade regardless of the triggering antigen, its anti-inflammatory action is plausibly transferable to seborrheic dermatitis.

This mechanistic plausibility is further reinforced by direct clinical evidence: completed Phase 3 and Phase 4 randomised trials have already tested topical tacrolimus specifically as a maintenance therapy for severe facial seborrheic dermatitis, with results demonstrating reduced relapse frequency and reduced need for topical steroids. Mechanism and clinical evidence therefore converge on the same conclusion.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Rationale:**
