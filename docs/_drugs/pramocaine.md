## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Pramocaine is generally understood to be a topical surface anaesthetic that blocks voltage-gated sodium channels in sensory nerve endings, and it is used to relieve itch and discomfort. This general description has not been verified against the compiled data.

The suggested link to papillary conjunctivitis is symptomatic: itch is a hallmark of allergic and contact-lens-related papillary conjunctivitis, so a local anaesthetic with antipruritic action could in theory ease ocular itching and discomfort. This is speculative. The high TxGNN score is a computational output only and is not backed by clinical or literature evidence.

There is also an important concern. Topical anaesthetics can cause corneal toxicity with repeated use, and pramocaine formulations are not designed or approved for ophthalmic use. The route of administration is therefore a major unresolved issue.
